# Chapter 9: The Diary

The cold hit Nora like a punch.

She sat on the floor, the diary on her knees. The light bulb flickered, then steadied. Shadow whined next to Theo.

Nora’s pulse throbbed in her temples. A girl’s handwriting on the first page. Purple ink.

*Lina’s Diary*

“It got ice-cold,” Theo whispered. He rubbed his arms. “When you opened it. Right that second.”

“I know.” Nora swallowed. The cold crept through her fingers and up into her wrists. But she didn’t put the book down.

Shadow whined once more. Then he went quiet. He lay down right next to Theo’s knee, his head on his paws. His amber eyes were open. Alert. But calm. As if he knew something important was happening.

“Should I read it out loud?” Nora said.

Theo nodded. He sat across from her, his knees pulled up, his back against the wall. His face was pale under the ceiling light.

Nora turned to the first entry.

---

“*March 12, 1974*,” she read aloud. “*New diary! Mom gave it to me for my birthday. Purple ink, because that’s my favorite color. I’m twelve now.*”

Nora stopped. Twelve. Like her.

“*School was boring today*,” she read on. “*Math. Mrs. Kettner got mad at me again because I was daydreaming. But after school was fun. Hanna and I were down by the river. Luna jumped in the water and brought back a stick. Three times. Then she shook herself off and got us wet.*”

“Luna?” Theo said.

“Her dog.” Nora turned the page. “She writes about Luna a lot.”

Shadow lifted his head. His ears perked up.

The next entries were short. School days, friends, cotton candy at the fair. One entry was about going up on the roof with a flashlight, because Lina wanted to count stars. She had drawn the constellations, tiny dots in purple ink, joined by wobbly lines. Next to them she had written: “When I grow up, I want to be an astronomer. Or a vet. Or both.” A normal girl’s life. With every entry, Nora could picture Lina a little better.

“She sounds nice,” Theo said quietly. “Cotton candy. Stars. A dog.” He stroked Shadow’s head. “At least she had a dog.”

“Yeah.” Nora’s voice was thick. “She sounds like someone you’d want to know.”

She kept turning pages: summer, the pool, ice cream from the corner store. Then it was September.

---

“*September 14*,” Nora read. “*Something strange happened. My room was ice-cold this morning. Everywhere else was normal. Only my room. Dad says the window must have been open. It wasn’t.*”

Nora paused. The hair on the back of her neck stood up.

“*September 17. The cold room again. Mom turned up the heat. Didn’t help. And at night Luna sat outside my door and growled. She never growls.*”

Theo sat up. “Luna growled.”

“Yeah.” Nora looked at him. “Outside her room.”

Shadow growled softly. Deep in his throat.

She kept reading. Her voice got quieter.

“*September 22. Noises at night. Scratching in the wall. I woke Mom up. She was annoyed. She said it was mice. But mice don’t scratch to a beat.*”

“To a beat,” Theo repeated. His voice sounded thin. “Like the knocking. At our place.”

Nora nodded. Her tongue stuck to the roof of her mouth.

“*September 29. This morning my desk chair was by the window. I didn’t put it there. Mom says I was sleepwalking. I’ve never walked in my sleep.*”

“Sleepwalking,” Theo muttered. “Sure. Because chairs move by themselves all the time.”

The entries got shorter and shorter. The round letters crowded closer together, as if Lina had written faster. Or as if her hand had been shaking.

---

“*October 5. Luna won’t come into my room. She stands in the doorway and whines. I sleep in the living room with her now.*”

Shadow lifted his head. Only for a moment. Then he laid it back down on his paws. His eyes stayed on Nora.

“Luna acted just like Shadow,” Theo said quietly.

“I know.” Nora swallowed. “Exactly the same.”

“And her parents had an explanation for everything.” Theo shook his head. “Like you did with the pipes.”

Nora said nothing. Because he was right.

She kept reading.

“*October 10. I told Hanna. She laughed. She says I watch too many scary movies. I don’t watch scary movies. I live in one.*”

Theo pulled his knees tighter to his chest. “Her best friend,” he murmured. “Even she didn’t believe her.”

“*October 15*,” Nora read. Her voice was barely a whisper now. “*Dad says it’s my imagination. Mom says it’s my imagination. Hanna says it’s my imagination. But I’m not imagining it. At night someone stands in my room. I can’t see it. But I can feel it. Cold. Right next to my bed. And Luna howls in the hallway.*”

Silence. Shadow pressed his head harder against Theo’s knee. Theo put his hand on Shadow’s neck.

Nora turned the page. The paper shook in her hands.

“*October 19. I’m scared. I’m really scared. Nobody believes me. I asked Mom to let me sleep at Grandma’s. She said I should stop making such a big deal out of it. ‘There’s no such thing as ghosts,’ she said. ‘Stop it.’*”

Theo’s chin trembled. Nora saw it. She heard his breath catch. But she kept reading. She had to keep reading.

“*October 23. Last night the door opened by itself. Slowly. I watched it. Nobody was standing there. But the cold came in like a wave. Luna screamed. Really screamed. Like a person.*”

Nora closed her eyes. Just for a moment. Behind her eyelids she saw a girl. Twelve years old. Alone in a dark room.

“Theo?”

“Yeah.”

“I’m sorry.” Her voice cracked. “For not believing you. When you said something was wrong. I didn’t even let you finish. Just like Lina’s parents.”

Theo was quiet. Then he said, “If you’d believed me, I probably would’ve been even more scared.”

Almost a laugh. Almost.

A hot stab went through her chest. Guilt. Hot and sharp.

Nora opened her eyes. Her hands were ice-cold. A weight sat in her chest, heavy and tight.

---

“*October 27*,” she read. Her voice almost broke. “*I’m writing this down because nobody else listens to me. If something happens to me, somebody should know that I told the truth. I wasn’t dreaming. I wasn’t making a big deal out of it. There’s something in this apartment. And it’s getting worse.*”

Theo sobbed.

One short sob. He pressed his hand over his mouth and turned his head away. His shoulders shook.

Nora lowered the book into her lap. “Theo—”

“Keep reading,” he whispered. His voice was all choked up. “Please. Keep reading.”

Shadow pressed his nose harder against Theo’s arm. Theo’s fingers dug into Shadow’s fur. He held on. As if Shadow were an anchor.

Nora’s eyes burned. She blinked the sting away. She had to be the strong one now.

Theo wiped his face. “Nora.” His voice was small. “I want to go home.”

“We are home, Theo.”

He shook his head. But he didn’t say anything else.

She picked the book back up.

---

“*October 30.* Only two entries left,” Nora said quietly. She could feel it. The end was coming. The last pages.

“*I’ve been thinking. The basement is the coldest place. Luna won’t go down the basement stairs. She shakes all over and drags me away. But I think the answer is down there. It started down there.*”

Nora turned to the last page with writing on it.

The handwriting was different. Bigger. Shaky. In some places the purple ink was smeared, as if drops had fallen on the paper. Tears.

“*November 2*,” Nora read. Her voice was a whisper.

“*Tomorrow I’m going down to the basement. I have to know what’s down there. Luna is coming with me. I’m scared. But I have to know. If I understand it, maybe it will stop. If I understand it, maybe everybody else will believe me.*”

Nora turned the page.

Empty.

The next page was blank. White. Untouched.

And the one after that. And the one after that.

Lina never wrote again. That was her last entry. On November 2, 1974. The day before she died.

The silence was so thick that Nora could feel it on her skin. Heavy. Cold.

Theo was crying. Quietly, without sobbing. The tears just kept coming. He didn’t wipe them away. He sat there, one hand in Shadow’s fur, and the tears dripped onto his knees.

“She was all alone,” he whispered. “Nobody believed her. And then she went down there. Alone.”

Nora’s throat squeezed shut. She hugged the diary close. A girl. Twelve years old. Purple ink. And no one to believe her.

Shadow lay still between them. His warm body. His calm breathing. He just lay there, as if he knew they needed him.

Nora opened the diary to the first page and ran her hand over it. Over the name. Over the little heart.

“We heard you, Lina,” she whispered. “Someone listened to you.”

Theo swallowed hard. Then he wiped his face with his sleeve. His eyes were red.

“Nora.”

“Yeah.”

He looked at her. No jokes. No sarcasm. Just a ten-year-old boy with wet eyes.

“She went down to the basement.”

“Yeah.”

“And she didn’t come back up.”

Nora’s stomach tightened. The cold in the apartment seemed to grow thicker. Closing in.

Theo looked her straight in the eye.

“What’s in the basement, Nora?”

Shadow lifted his head. His ears turned forward. Under their feet, deep in the building, something creaked.

Nora closed the diary. Her hands were stiff.

“That,” she said quietly, “is what we’ve got to find out.”
