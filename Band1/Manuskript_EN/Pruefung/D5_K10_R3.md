# D5 Line Edit: Chapter 10 "The Basement" (Round 3)

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9-12, spooky-funny).
Source read: `Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_10.md` only.
House style respected: short fragments at scary beats are NOT flagged on their own. They are flagged only when they stack redundantly or blunt a beat.

Overall: This is a clean, readable chapter, and the scare escalates well. Theo's humor lands. Most findings are small: a handful of literal-sounding phrases, a few redundant fragment pairs, and some continuity slips around Shadow and Theo's physical state.

---

## Findings

1. **L3** · "Nora pointed her phone's flashlight down the stairs." · RHYTHM · "stairs" closes the sentence right after "The basement stairs were steep." The echo is audible. · *Suggestion: "...pointed her phone's flashlight down."*

2. **L19** · "Old wood, swollen with moisture." · TRANSLATED · This is the second "moisture" in four paragraphs (L3). "Moisture" is a clinical, dictionary word. US kids' books say "damp" or "wet." · *Suggestion: "Old wood, swollen and damp."*

3. **L19** · "Nora pushed down on the handle." · TRANSLATED · A US kid pictures a doorknob, and "pushed down on the handle" reads like a European lever handle rendered literally. The Germany setting makes it defensible, but it still makes readers stop. · *Suggestion: "Nora pressed the handle down" or just "Nora pushed the door."*

4. **L21** · "Storage lockers with padlocks." · UNCLEAR · American houses don't have locked storage cages in a shared basement, so a 10-year-old may picture gym lockers. · *Suggestion: "Storage cages with padlocks" or "Wire storage stalls, each with a padlock."*

5. **L25** · "Past the storage lockers, past the old bike, past the boxes." · OTHER · L21 says "Old bikes" (plural), and here it's "the old bike" (singular). The inconsistency is small but visible.

6. **L25** · "His nose was working." · TRANSLATED · This sounds like a literal "his nose was working." In English it suggests the nose is functional, not sniffing. · *Suggestion: "His nose twitched." / "He sniffed hard at the air."*

7. **L31** · "Concrete, fluorescent tubes, outlets." · BRITISH · "Fluorescent tubes" is British and technical. Americans say "fluorescent lights." · *Suggestion: "fluorescent lights."*

8. **L31** · (same passage) · OTHER · If the front basement has fluorescent lights, a sharp kid will ask why they're using a phone flashlight. Add one clause (the lights don't work, or the switch is upstairs), or cut the lights.

9. **L41** · "True. In a movie, at least there's background music. That way you know when it's getting dangerous." · RHYTHM · The joke is good, but it's spread over two flat sentences and the punch goes soft. "Background music" is also bland. · *Suggestion: "True. In a movie there's creepy music, so at least you know when to panic."*

10. **L55** · "Nora checked the battery. Eighty percent." · RHYTHM · "Battery" repeats from Theo's line just above. · *Suggestion: "Nora checked. Eighty percent."*

11. **L61** · "In the middle of the last room. In front of a corner." · TRANSLATED · "In front of a corner" isn't idiomatic English. It reads like a literal preposition. · *Suggestion: "In the middle of the last room, facing a corner."*

12. **L61** · "And then he made a sound Nora had never heard him make. Not like this." · UNCLEAR · "Never heard" and "Not like this" contradict each other: either she has never heard it, or she has heard it but not like this. L65 confirms she has heard a whine before. · *Suggestion: "...a sound Nora had heard before, but never like this."*

13. **L67** · "Nora's skin tightened. Like an electric shock." · STIFF · The simile doesn't fit, because a shock jolts and doesn't tighten. · *Suggestion: "Nora's skin prickled, like static before a shock."*

14. **L81** · "Worn smooth by decades of water. Fifty years old." · UNCLEAR · Nora states the age as fact, but she can't know it. The jump from "decades" to an exact number reads like author knowledge leaking in. · *Suggestion: "Fifty years old, maybe. Exactly fifty."*, which makes the guess hers.

15. **L95** · "Her arms hung heavy at her sides. ... Sadness. So heavy she could hardly breathe." · RHYTHM · "Heavy" appears twice in five short sentences. The triple "Something... Something else. Something deeper." already loads the beat. · *Suggestion: change the first one to "Her arms hung limp."*

16. **L101** · "The temperature had dropped. Slowly. Bit by bit." · RHYTHM · "Slowly" and "Bit by bit" are redundant fragments, and "The temperature had dropped" is a science-class phrasing. · *Suggestion: "It was getting colder. Bit by bit."*

17. **L107** · "That's not the pipes, is it?" · UNCLEAR · Seeing your breath has no obvious link to pipes. A 10-year-old won't follow (cold pipes? leaking pipes?). · *Suggestion: "That's not just a draft, is it?"*

18. **L111** · "Shadow stopped whining. Suddenly. Just like that." · RHYTHM · The two fragments say the same thing twice. · *Suggestion: keep one, e.g. "Shadow stopped whining. Just like that."*

19. **L113-115** · "Grief. / The dog looked sad." · RHYTHM · "Grief" lands as a strong one-word paragraph, and then "The dog looked sad" restates it more weakly, which takes the air out of the moment. Also, L111 says she "couldn't place" it, but she names it immediately. · *Suggestion: cut "The dog looked sad." and keep "As if he knew something he couldn't say."*

20. **L143** · "Shadow stood up. Slowly." · OTHER · Continuity problem: Shadow has been standing the whole time (L61, L71, "He stood in front of the corner"). He never sat or lay down. · *Suggestion: "Shadow moved. Slowly."*

21. **L143** · "His body wasn't trembling anymore." · OTHER · Continuity problem: the text never said he was trembling. "Anymore" points back to nothing. · *Suggestion: add trembling at L61/L71, or cut "anymore."*

22. **L145** · "The presence changed." · TOO ADULT · "Presence" is ghost-hunter-show jargon. In L123 the text did this well by showing it as a feeling. · *Suggestion: "The feeling behind her changed." / "Whatever was behind her changed."*

23. **L149** · "Until now, she hadn't even noticed how tense she'd been." · STIFF · The fronted "Until now," sounds written rather than thought. · *Suggestion: "She hadn't even noticed how tense she'd been."*

24. **L151** · "He swallowed." (also L37, L93) · OTHER · This is Theo's third "swallowed" in the chapter, and the tic is getting noticeable. Here it also interrupts his best comic run. · *Suggestion: cut it, or vary it ("His voice cracked.").*

25. **L161** · "They led right up behind the spot where Nora had just been standing." · UNCLEAR · Nora has only turned around and is still standing there. "Had just been standing" makes it sound as if she moved. · *Suggestion: "They led right up to the spot behind her heels."*

26. **L161** · "Toes, ball, heel. Perfect." · TRANSLATED · "Perfect." as a standalone fragment reads like approval ("Perfect!") instead of "perfectly formed." · *Suggestion: "Toes, ball, heel. Every line sharp."*

27. **L163** · "These were bare feet. A child's feet." · RHYTHM · This repeats L159 word for word ("Bare feet. A child's feet."). Repeating it right away kills the chill. · *Suggestion: "Nora and Theo were wearing sneakers. These prints had toes."*

28. **L165** · "His eyes were huge." · OTHER · The eyes beat is overused: Shadow's "eyes were wide" (L111), Theo's "eyes went wide" (L133), and now "eyes were huge." · *Suggestion: give Theo a different reaction here (he grabs the doorframe, covers his mouth).*

29. **L173** · "Nora reached for Theo's hand. He squeezed back." · OTHER · "Back" needs a first squeeze, but she only reached. · *Suggestion: "Nora grabbed Theo's hand and squeezed. He squeezed back."*

30. **L175** · "But the footprints stayed in the dust." · STIFF · "Stayed" is flat and sounds literal. The intent ("they didn't fade like the ghost did") doesn't come through. · *Suggestion: "But the footprints didn't go away."*

## Summary Questions

### (1) Would I guess this was translated?

**Yes, on a close read. A kid wouldn't notice.** About 90% of the prose reads naturally as American. Theo's dialogue sounds especially native ("Not even to get a soda," "I'm one of the people in the theater"). The tells cluster in the narration, not the dialogue:
- literal phrasings: "His nose was working," "In front of a corner," "pushed down on the handle," "Perfect.," "the footprints stayed"
- a vocabulary register that's slightly too formal or European: "moisture," "fluorescent tubes," "storage lockers," "the presence"
- stacked synonym fragments ("Slowly. Bit by bit." / "Suddenly. Just like that.") that read like a translator keeping both halves of a source-language doublet.

### (2) Voice ratings

- **Nora (12): 3/5.** Her inner experience works well (the metallic taste, the sadness, the sense of someone behind her). But she speaks fewer than ten short lines, and almost all of them are reactive ("Yeah." "No." "We're not in a movie."). She needs one line that shows her rational, older-sister personality, for example trying to explain the cold before giving up.
- **Theo (10): 4/5.** He's funny, anxious and specific, and the movie-theater bit is the best line in the chapter. He loses a point for the repeated "swallowed" tic and the flat "background music" joke.

### (3) Is the chapter ending strong?

**Mostly, yes, but it could hit harder.** The footprints are an excellent final image, and "Run" is a punchy last word. The weakness is that nothing new triggers "Run": the presence has already faded, so the command comes after the danger has passed. If one fresh stimulus came right before it (a new print appearing in the dust, a sound from the corner, the phone light dying again), "Run" would be a true cliffhanger rather than a closing beat. L175 ("stayed in the dust") also slows the run-up, so tightening it would help.
