# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 2: "Shadow"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Read in isolation, chapters 2–4 only.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L3** · "It vibrated in Nora's chest, dull and deep." · TRANSLATED · Two adjectives tacked onto the end ("dull and deep") is a German pattern. In English "dull" also sounds like "boring." · → "She could feel it in her chest, low and deep."
2. **L5** · "The dog sat in front of the door with the SILBER name card and didn't move." · TRANSLATED · "Name card" is not American usage for a door label. · → "nameplate"
3. **L5** · "His dark hackles were up." · OTHER · Hackles are a ridge of raised fur, not a color, so "dark" reads oddly. · → "His hackles were up."
4. **L15** · "She pushed the handle down." · OTHER · US kids picture doorknobs. The lever handle is correct for Germany, but "pushed down" makes them stop and picture it. Low priority. · → "She pressed down on the handle."
5. **L25** · "A crack ran down the right-hand wall from the ceiling to the floor, as wide as a finger." · TRANSLATED · "Right-hand wall" is stiff and "as wide as a finger" is a calque of *fingerbreit*. The sentence is also long. · → "A crack as wide as her finger ran down the wall on the right."
6. **L25** · "A small table, broken, one leg snapped." · RHYTHM · "Broken" and "one leg snapped" say the same thing twice. · → "A small table with one leg snapped."
7. **L31** · "Be quiet." · STIFF · An American older sister whispering in a creepy apartment says "Shh" or "Quiet." "Be quiet" sounds like a teacher. · → "Shh."
8. **L49** · "The dog, who had gone ahead of them through the apartment, stopped." · STIFF · It repeats L35 ("The dog had already gone ahead"), and the relative clause stalls the scare beat. · → "Up ahead, the dog stopped."
9. **L45** · "From one step to the next, the temperature dropped like a stone." · TRANSLATED · Calque of *von einem Schritt auf den nächsten*. · → "With one step, the temperature dropped like a stone."
10. **L45** · "Twenty degrees. Maybe more." · UNCLEAR · US readers assume Fahrenheit, and a 20°F drop does not make your breath steam. If Celsius is meant, the number undersells the drop. · → "It felt like stepping into a freezer." (avoid the number)
11. **L53** · "The dog was refusing." · STIFF · Flat, and "refusing" has no object. · → "The dog wouldn't budge."
12. **L53** · "His whole body said: No." · TRANSLATED · Using a colon before imagined speech is German typography. US style is italics with no colon. · → "His whole body said *no*."
13. **L69** · "…with a look that said: No farther." · TRANSLATED · The same colon convention, and "No farther" is formal. · → "…with a look that said *Don't.*"
14. **L67** · "She wanted to say something sensible." · BRITISH · "Sensible" leans British. An American 12-year-old would think "logical." · → "something logical"
15. **L77** · "They didn't stop until the second floor." · UNCLEAR · Floor numbering: in Germany the ground floor is 0. If "second floor" and "third floor" were carried over from German, a US reader will miscount the building. Check that it is consistent across the book. · → Make sure the US numbering is intended, or say "their own landing."
16. **L91** · "I ran into the downstairs neighbor, by the way, Mrs. Held." · RHYTHM · "By the way" wedged between the noun and the name makes Mom's line clunky. · → "By the way, I met the downstairs neighbor. Mrs. Held."
17. **L133** · "He was a stray they didn't even know." · STIFF · Logically odd, since you don't "know" a stray. It reads as translated narration. · → "They didn't know anything about him."
18. **L141** · "Quick and short, as if he had recognized the name." · TRANSLATED · "Quick and short" mirrors *kurz und schnell*. · → "His ears twitched, as if he knew the name already."
19. **L147** · "On the third floor. With the room that was so cold she had seen her breath." · TRANSLATED · "With the room" is a calque (*bei dem Zimmer*). · → "In the room that was so cold…"
20. **L151** · "Theo fed Shadow half a slice of salami." · UNCLEAR · Half a slice of salami is a tiny sliver. Is it a pizza slice? US kids would say pepperoni. · → "Theo fed Shadow the salami off his pizza."
21. **L165** · "The first real moment since they got here that felt something like home." · RHYTHM · A long fragment with a soft landing. · → "For the first time since they got here, it felt a little like home."
22. **L169** · "Kirchgasse lay quiet in the evening light." · TRANSLATED · A street that "lay" is German (*lag*). · → "Kirchgasse was quiet in the evening light."
23. **L173** · "…like a shadow moving without any light to cast it." · UNCLEAR · The dog was named Shadow 30 lines earlier, so for a beat the reader thinks of the dog. · → "like a dark shape moving…"
24. **L187** · "He was standing now. Motionless. Focused on something Nora couldn't see." · OTHER (POV) · Nora is in bed with the door between them. She can hear claws but cannot see that he is motionless or focused. · → "She pictured him standing there, staring at something."
25. **L199** · "Right behind her headboard. Directly above her." · UNCLEAR · "Behind" and "above" are two different directions. How does a wall knock from the apartment upstairs? A 10-year-old will try to map this. · → Pick one: "In the wall right behind her headboard."
26. **L209** · "Outside the door, Shadow still stood motionless. Not a sound. Not a breath." · OTHER (POV) · She can't see him. "Not a sound" works, but "stood motionless" doesn't. · → "Outside the door, Shadow made no sound at all."

## End-of-chapter assessment

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, but only on a close read. The dialog is lively and idiomatic: "Five stars," "Drama queen / Survival expert," and "It's scientifically proven / Says who? / Me. Just now." The narration gives it away: the colon-before-thought convention, "name card," "from one step to the next," "lay quiet," and "with the room." The floor numbering and the bare "twenty degrees" also suggest a European source. An average kid reader would not notice. An editor would, within two pages.

**(2) Voice:** Nora (12): 3/5. Her inner voice works ("It looked like someone had run"), but her spoken lines are thin and generic ("Be quiet," "Fine. We'll go."). She needs one line that is clearly hers, rational and a bit bossy. Theo (10): 4/5. He is funny and consistent, and every scare gets a comeback. He is slightly too polished in places but well within the range of MG voice.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Yes. The knocking pattern, the dog going silent, and then "a low whine" make a classic, effective cliffhanger. The only problem is the POV slip in L209.
