# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 6: The List of Names

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). Findings only; no rewrites.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L7 · "Normal warm." · TRANSLATED · "Normal warm" is German syntax. In English it reads as an error. · "Ordinary warm."
2. L7 · "No ice, no steaming breath." · TRANSLATED · "Steaming breath" again (see Ch. 5). Breath fogs.
3. L9 · "Her hands were on her knees." · STIFF · A static state where the reader needs an action. · "She bent over, hands on her knees."
4. L11 · "As if outside were a different world." · STIFF · A formal subjunctive with a vague idea behind it.
5. L35 · "Only theirs wasn't anymore." · TRANSLATED · "Nur ihre nicht mehr." · "Just not theirs."
6. L45 · "The fear gave way to curiosity. It always happened like that." · TOO ADULT · The narrator tells an abstract emotional shift, which breaks the "emotions are physical" rule. The next line, "First the pounding heart, then the questions", already does the job.
7. L45 · "Those aren't living people." · STIFF · Awkward double negative for a 12-year-old talking. · "Those people are all dead."
8. L61–63 · "'Whatever Mrs. Silber was doing,' Nora said slowly, ..." · OTHER · The same speaker gets two paragraphs in a row, with a new tag on the second. Readers will think someone else is talking.
9. L69 · "We investigate ghost cases." · STIFF · Sounds like a job description. · "We hunt ghosts."
10. L75 · "Theo tied Shadow's leash to a bike rack." · RHYTHM · "Shadow" in two sentences back to back. Use "his leash".
11. L85 · "Somewhere, a computer hummed away to itself." · BRITISH · "Hummed away to itself" has a British, cozy ring. · "Somewhere, a computer hummed."
12. L85 · "Here, there were answers. Here, the world was in order." · TRANSLATED · "Hier war die Welt in Ordnung" is a German set phrase. · "Here, things made sense."
13. L117 / L125 · "Three paragraphs." / "That was all. Three paragraphs." · RHYTHM · "Three paragraphs" is said three times in ten lines, twice by the narrator. Let Theo own the joke.
14. L135 · "She thought of Shadow, waiting upstairs outside the door." · UNCLEAR · They're in the basement, so "upstairs outside the door" is a confusing location. · "...waiting outside by the bike rack."
15. L137–139 · "'...for so long—' She broke off. / 'Forty years what?' Theo said." · UNCLEAR · Nora never says "forty years". Theo is answering a line the reader didn't see. Probably a translation or cut slip.
16. L159 · "Little strips of film with photos of newspaper pages." · OTHER · Strips of film are microfilm. Microfiche is flat sheets. A small accuracy point that some teachers or librarians will catch.
17. L167 · "It's like Netflix without the picture or the sound." · OTHER · The joke doesn't make sense, because they are looking at pictures. · "It's like Netflix, but it's only the boring channel."
18. L169 · "Focus. We need 1974." · UNCLEAR · They already pulled the 1974 box. Nora should be asking for November.
19. L173 · "He was whining. She couldn't hear it, but she could see it in his body." · STIFF · The narration contradicts itself (states he's whining, then says she can't hear it). · "He looked like he was whining."
20. L177 · "Then her eyes stopped." · TRANSLATED · "Ihr Blick blieb hängen." In English this sounds like her eyes broke. · "Then something caught her eye."
21. L195 · "Lina Vogt (12), daughter of the Vogt family, Kirchgasse 14, ..." · TRANSLATED · Newspaper style is fine, but "daughter of the Vogt family" sounds like a German formula even inside the story world. Drop it, or use "daughter of Karl and ... Vogt".
22. L199 · "Nora searched along the edges of the picture." · STIFF · · "Nora scanned the faces."
23. L199 · "That was exactly what made it so hard." · TOO ADULT · Abstract and reflective. Show it physically instead (lump in her throat).
24. L217 · "'That's our address.'" · UNCLEAR · The tag before it is "Nora nodded", so it isn't clear who says this line.
25. L221–223 · "Not just anywhere. / Home." · UNCLEAR · "Not just anywhere" has no antecedent. Shadow was staring "toward the Old Town", not home, so the link the reader is supposed to make doesn't land.

## Chapter verdict

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, though it's lighter than Ch. 5. The library scene and Theo's jokes read American. The giveaways are "Normal warm", "the world was in order", "her eyes stopped", "only theirs wasn't anymore", plus the "Forty years what?" continuity slip.

**(2) Voice (1–5):** Nora 3: competent but bookish ("Those aren't living people"), and she has little fun of her own. Theo 5: the best Theo so far ("Great résumé", "Spookology", "Bless you", "My hamster report was longer", "chapter one of *We Should Have Stayed Home*").

**(3) Ending strong?** Mostly. "Kirchgasse 14 … That's our address" is a great reveal. The two closing fragments ("Not just anywhere. / Home.") are vague and blunt it. Ending on "That's our address." would be stronger.
