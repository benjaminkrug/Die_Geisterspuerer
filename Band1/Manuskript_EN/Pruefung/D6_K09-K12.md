### Overall verdict (kid, after 12 chapters)

**Do I want to read the last four? YES.** The First One is the best thing in the book, Mrs. Silber's "I believe I am next" is a great hook, and I want to know what Shadow really is (forty years?!). Theo is still the best part. In Ch. 11 he became the brave one, and that was the best character moment so far.

**Scare curve, Ch. 9–12:** 7, 8, 7, 6. Ch. 10 is the peak, and it should be. Nothing is nightmare-level except, possibly, the "girl's fingers scratched at stone" picture (Ch. 10:97). That's the one place I'd soften.

**How Chapter 12 lands:** It hits me in the feelings. Reading to the ghost, the cold melting, and **THANK YOU** on the window is a real payoff, and I'd feel good. Three things weaken it:
1. The Ch. 11 knocking cliffhanger gets thrown away in one sentence (12:15).
2. It feels like a repeat of Ch. 9 (reading the diary, crying, "We heard you, Lina"). The book has to say clearly why it works this time.
3. "UP" repeats Ch. 4's arrow, so the final hook is weaker than Ch. 10's "Run." or Ch. 11's knocking.

**Crying load:** Theo cries in Ch. 9 (twice), has red eyes in Ch. 11, and cries in Ch. 12. Nora's eyes burn in Ch. 7, 9, and 12. A 10-year-old boy reader might think "this is a lot of crying" across four chapters in a row. One fewer would help. The best candidate to cut is Ch. 9:171, the second cry.

**Fix first:**
1. Ch. 11:141, "Shadow" in Mrs. Silber's notebook plus "For forty years." Either make the kids react (a big mystery moment) or neutralize it.
2. Ch. 12:15, the swallowed knocking cliffhanger.

## Part 2: Read-aloud (parent reading to a kid)

### Tongue-twisters or hard to pronounce
- Nothing serious in Ch. 9–12. That's a clear improvement over the place names in Ch. 5–6.
- Ch. 9:117 "A hot stab went through her chest. Guilt. Hot and sharp." "Hot" twice in three beats, and "hot stab" is clunky in the mouth.
- Ch. 11:141 "Shadow senses them…" is a slight sibilant run. It's okay.
- Ch. 12:129 "Frost spread across the window glass" is a mild s-cluster. It's okay.
- "Margret" (Ch. 11:115): parents will say "Margaret." That's harmless.

### Sentences hard to read aloud
- **Ch. 9:147** “*October 30.* Only two entries left,” Nora said quietly. The parent reads the diary date in "Lina voice" and then has to switch to Nora mid-quote. Listeners will hear "Only two entries left" as part of the diary. Suggest: "Only two entries left," Nora said quietly. "*October 30. I've been thinking…*"
- **Ch. 10:161** "They led from the corner with the scratch marks to right behind the spot where Nora had just been standing." "to right behind the spot where" trips the tongue. It's the longest sentence in the four chapters (20 words) and lands at the scare peak. Split it.
- Ch. 10:61 "And then he did something Nora had never heard him do quite like this." Awkward ending ("do quite like this").
- Ch. 10:157 "But on the floor." It's a dangling fragment. Aloud it sounds like the parent lost the line. It works only with a long pause.
- Ch. 11:135 "Only when someone grasps that does the chain come loose." The inverted word order makes a parent restart the sentence.
- Ch. 11:125 "Between what was and what could have been." Abstract. It's fine for the grown-up reader, but it floats past a kid.
- Ch. 12:155–169: the run of similes ("like blood flowing back into hands that had fallen asleep… Like a fall morning. Not like a freezer… Like a knot coming undone. Like a breath that was finally let out."). Aloud, it gets sing-song. Cut two.
- Ch. 12:207 "THANK YOU. UP." needs a clear pause between the words, or it sounds like one phrase.

### Unclear who is speaking
- Ch. 9:147, see above. It's the only real problem.
- Ch. 9:47–103: the diary entries come one after another, mostly untagged. That's fine, because Nora is clearly reading. The italics and the parent's "diary voice" carry it.
- Ch. 11:125–163: Mrs. Silber's quotes are nested in single quotes inside Nora's double quotes. On the page it's fine. Aloud the parent needs a third voice, but it's clear from context.
- Ch. 12:103–107: "You're not going to freeze to death." / "You sure?" / "No." These are untagged and quick. On the page they're fine. Aloud, the parent must switch voices fast, or the "No." gets lost. It's worth it, because it's a great beat.
- Ch. 11:39–43 and 12:83–91 alternate cleanly.

### Rhythm stumbles
- Ch. 9:113–119: joke, then guilt, then body feelings, then eyes opening. The emotional order is backwards aloud.
- Ch. 10:111–115: "Grief." / "The dog looked sad." The redundancy is very audible.
- Ch. 11:161 and 173: "The air… grew thicker. Heavier." is said twice.
- Ch. 11:169/171/187 and Ch. 9:167: the "never wrote again" refrain.
- Ch. 12:15: the flashback sentence breaks the urgency right after "Until now."

### Words that sound like "Shadow"
- **Ch. 12:51, "A shadow. But not our Shadow. A different one."** **It works aloud.** "Our" does the work, and "A different one. It was as big as a kid" fixes it right away. The half-second where the listener thinks "the dog?" is exactly the intended effect. Tip for the parent: stress "**our**." No change needed.
- Ch. 12:41 avoids "shadows" ("dark shapes on the walls… They were moving"). Good. Here "dark shapes" is meant to be ghostly, so the creature reading flagged for Ch. 8 is correct this time.
- **Possessive "Shadow's" = "shadows" aloud:**
  - **Ch. 11:51 "Only Shadow's breathing. Slow and even."** This starts a sentence with no other subject nearby. A listener can hear "Only shadows breathing," which is a creepy image that isn't intended. Suggest "Only Shadow breathed. Slow and even." or "Nobody spoke. Shadow breathed, slow and even."
  - Ch. 10:91 "And Shadow's whining, soft now…" can mildly be heard as "and shadows whining." It's at a scary moment, so the misreading is creepy. Suggest "And Shadow, whining softly now…"
  - Ch. 9:39/95/133/171 and Ch. 11:5 ("Shadow's head/neck/fur/claws") are fine. The noun right after makes them clear.
- Other sound-alikes ("show" Ch. 11:129, Ch. 12:73; "shut" Ch. 9:175, Ch. 11:11/195) are not confusable.
