# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 4 "Condensation"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Only Kapitel_02–04.md were read.

Format: line · sentence · category · why · suggestion

## Findings

1. **L1** · "Chapter 4: Condensation" · TOO ADULT · A science-class word as a chapter title has no spook appeal for a 10-year-old. The word works inside the chapter as Nora's excuse, but it's weak on the contents page. · *Chapter 4: Written in Fog* / *Fog Letters*

2. **L7 (vs. ch. 3 L197)** · "The letters were clear, the edges a little blurred." · OTHER · Chapter 3 just said "clear and sharp." Small, but readers going straight from one chapter to the next will notice. · Align the two.

3. **L11** · "Pressure spread beneath her ribs." · TRANSLATED · Sounds like *Druck breitete sich unter ihren Rippen aus*. It's physical, but not how an American kid's book says it. · *Her chest went tight.*

4. **L17–19** · "Theo shuffled into the kitchen barefoot." · OTHER · Continuity break. In chapter 3, Theo is already sitting at the table with his chocolate milk when the bowl falls. The note is also re-introduced although Theo already reported it. · Have Theo jump up from the table instead: *Theo's chair scraped back.*

5. **L23–31, L67** · "NORA!" / "I TOLD YOU!" / "SOMEBODY WHO ISN'T THERE!" / "NOW?" · RHYTHM · Five all-caps shouts in a short stretch. It starts to look like yelling on every line, and the caps lose force. · Keep one or two in caps and use italics for the rest.

6. **L37** · "Just a damp streak on her sweater." · OTHER · A sweater in July. The cold explains it, but readers may stumble. · *on her pajama sleeve*

7. **L47** · "Her head spun. Her knees buckled, and she grabbed for the counter." · RHYTHM · Nora's knees give out three times across ch. 3–4 (ch. 3 L115, here, L161). The body tells are being reused. · *Her head spun. The kitchen tilted.*

8. **L53** · "Her little brother, who jumped at every little noise." · RHYTHM · "little / little." · *Her little brother, who jumped at every creak.*

9. **L57** · "Mom doesn't write cries for help on the kitchen window, Nora." · TOO ADULT · "Cries for help" is an adult idiom, close to a psychology term. · *"Mom doesn't write HELP on the kitchen window, Nora."*

10. **L59** · "Mom had gotten it the night before, along with a leash." · OTHER · Continuity: last night Mom fell asleep on the couch after pizza, and the kids planned to buy dog things "tomorrow." · *Theo had made it out of a moving blanket*, or move the purchase into a scene.

11. **L69** · "First day at the new school." · UNCLEAR · For an American 10-year-old, July means summer vacation. School in July will confuse readers unless it's explained (a different school calendar in Germany, or summer school). · Add half a line: *In Germany, school didn't let out until the end of July.* Or skip the month.

12. **L105** · "On the way to the bathroom. In the mirror over the sink, for a split second, she saw something behind her." · UNCLEAR · "On the way to" and then suddenly at the sink, so the location jumps. · *In the bathroom. In the mirror over the sink…*

13. **L131** · "Then the stairwell, the smell of floor wax, the familiar creak of the stairs." · STIFF · A verbless list, and "familiar" after one day in the building. · *The stairwell smelled like floor wax. The stairs creaked, same as always.*

14. **L149** · "As if written with a child's finger." · UNCLEAR · New and important information (child-sized), but slipped in with no sign that Nora noticed it. Kids may skip right past the clue. · *The letters were small. Like a kid had written them.*

15. **L153** · "Next to it, an arrow. Not a letter. A line with a point." · STIFF · Over-explains what an arrow is. "Not a letter" is a strange aside. · *Next to it, an arrow. Pointing up.*

16. **L161** · "Her fingers wouldn't close right. The glass clinked against the counter. Her breath caught. Her knees went weak." · RHYTHM · Four physical tells in a row, and she already "set the glass down" before her fingers fail. The order is off and the effect is crowded. · *The glass rattled as she set it down. Her breath caught.*

17. **L185** · "The ticking of the radiator." · UNCLEAR · In an American kid's experience, a ticking radiator means the heat is on. In July that's either a great creepy clue or a mistake. Make it clear which. · If intentional: *The radiator ticked. Like it was warming up. In July.*

18. **L191** · "But he wasn't looking at the door. He was looking to the left. Toward the stairwell. Up." · UNCLEAR · The apartment door is closed. How can he look toward the stairwell? The direction words stack up and confuse. · *He stared at the door, nose up, as if he could see through it to the stairs.*

## Summary

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** No, mostly. Apart from "pressure spread beneath her ribs" and a few verbless lists, the prose reads natively. The bigger problem is continuity between chapters (Theo's entrance, the dog bed and leash, sharp vs. blurred letters), which feels like drafts stitched together, not translation. The July school day is the one setting detail a US reader will hit hard.

**(2) Voice:**
- Nora (12): **4/5.** Best chapter for her: wiping away the evidence, "That was condensation," "Order. Control. One thing at a time," "I'm acting like we have to go to school. Because we have to go to school." The moment she cracks is earned.
- Theo (10): **5/5.** "Condensation doesn't spell!", "ghost flu," "That's going on my tombstone / Tell that to the window," the hamster kid, and then the serious turn ("Somebody's asking for help. And we wiped the message away"). Excellent range for a 10-year-old.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Good, but slightly over-explained. HERE with an arrow, Shadow nosing the glass, and Theo going quiet all build well. "He was waiting for them" is a fine last beat. The final line, "Patient. Calm. As if he knew exactly where they had to go," explains what the reader already feels and softens the hook. End on *He was waiting for them.* or on a single sound from upstairs.
