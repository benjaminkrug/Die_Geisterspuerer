# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 5 "Mrs. Silber's Apartment"

Reviewer: D5 (US middle-grade line editor, spooky-funny list)
Scope: Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_05.md only. German names and places, "Mrs." with German surnames, and short fragments at scary beats are house style and are not flagged.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L5 · "Just that patient waiting that said: I'm ready. Are you?" · TRANSLATED · A colon used to introduce unquoted inner speech is a German punctuation habit. US middle-grade would use italics or an em dash. · *...the kind of waiting that said, I'm ready. Are you?* (italicized)

2. L7 · "Behind her, back in the kitchen, was the window." · TRANSLATED · The inverted "Behind her ... was the window" order reads like German verb-second syntax, and it is a little hard to place spatially. · "The kitchen window was behind her."

3. L7 (also L97, L173) · "Her heart beat faster." · RHYTHM · The same stock heartbeat line appears three times in one chapter, so it loses force. · Vary it or cut one.

4. L17 · "The ticking of the radiator." · OTHER · Later chapters say it is summer, and a radiator does not tick in summer. A sharp kid reader will catch it. · "The hum of the fridge."

5. L25 · "Every part of her said: Don't go up there." · TRANSLATED · Same colon habit as L5. · Use italics, no colon.

6. L27 · "Like a finger pointing up." · RHYTHM · This repeats "The arrow pointing up" from L7, two paragraphs earlier. · Cut, or choose a different image.

7. L37 · "It was open. Just a crack. But this time was different." · UNCLEAR · "But" suggests a contrast with the door being open, but the contrast is really about the air. A 10-year-old will stumble here. · "It was open a crack, just like before. But something had changed."

8. L39 · "Like blood when you bite the inside of your cheek." · OTHER · This is the only blood image in the chapter, and it pushes toward body sensation more than the series' tone needs. "Like coins" already does the job. · Cut the blood simile.

9. L41 · "The wood was cool, not cold." · RHYTHM · "Cool" appears three times in two paragraphs (air cool / wood cool / cool not cold). · Vary it, for example "The wood felt normal."

10. L47 · "He had planted himself sideways across the hallway. His whole body had said no." · STIFF · Stacked past perfect ("had refused / had planted / had said") drags right after the quick "Then he went in." · Drop to simple past after the first "had."

11. L73 · "Maybe ten feet by twelve." · TOO ADULT · Real-estate measurements are not how a 12-year-old sizes up a room. · "About as big as her bedroom."

12. L75 · "Old covers, some leather, some brittle cardboard." · TRANSLATED · "Cardboard covers" is a literal rendering. US readers would say "hardcovers" or "cracked cloth covers." · "...some leather, some cracked cloth."

13. L87 · "Nora moved in closer. Her eyes moved over the books." · RHYTHM · "Moved / moved" in back-to-back sentences. · "Her eyes ran over the spines."

14. L87 · "History books and old city records." · RHYTHM · This repeats "Old books about the city" from the sentence before. · Cut.

15. L111 · "Next to the stacks of books, something was hanging on the wall." · STIFF · The withheld subject is a translated-feeling inversion, and it is weak because "Notes" follows right after anyway. · "Notes covered the wall next to the books."

16. L119 · "Dozens of names." · RHYTHM · This echoes "Dozens of them" from L111. · Vary it or cut.

17. L133 · "And the longer she looked, the more something came together in her head." · TRANSLATED · A "je länger ... desto" calque with a vague "something." · "The longer she looked, the clearer it got."

18. L139 · "Cold pressure spread through her chest." · STIFF · This reads as abstract, almost clinical. · "Her chest went tight and cold."

19. L149/155 · "M. Silber, 2019." · OTHER · A black-and-white, yellowed photo dated 2019 is odd, because 2019 photos are digital and color. Kids will notice. · Make the photo color but faded, or explain it.

20. L175 · "It was no accident, Nora thought." · STIFF · "It was no accident" is formal. A 12-year-old thinks "It wasn't an accident" or "It wasn't random." · "It wasn't random."

21. L187 · "Why would somebody hang a list of dead people on the wall?" · RHYTHM · This nearly duplicates Theo's line at L141 ("Why would somebody write a list of dead people on the wall?"). · Cut Nora's version or change the angle ("What was she *doing* with them?").

22. L199 · "Even here, ... he could almost make her laugh. Almost." · RHYTHM · Three "almost"s in four sentences (Almost a laugh / almost make her laugh / Almost). · Keep only the final one.

23. L203 · "Her skin tightened." · STIFF · An odd physical cue that does not read as fear to kids. · "Goosebumps ran up her arms."

24. L223 · "His face was white." · RHYTHM · This repeats "His face was pale" from L207 sixteen lines earlier. · Cut one.

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would I guess this was translated?** No, but it is borderline. The dialog is natural and very American ("I want it on the record," "Historians like candles. They're nostalgic."). The tells are in the narration: colons before inner speech, the "cardboard" covers, the "the longer ... the more" construction, and a few abstract body lines ("Cold pressure"). A casual reader would not notice. An editor would sense a slight European cadence in the descriptive passages.

2. **Voice:** Nora 3/5. She is competent but mostly a camera, and her one sharp moment ("You don't know what nostalgic means") is good. Theo 5/5. He is consistently funny, scared and specific, and every line sounds like a 10-year-old.

3. **Ending:** Strong. The ghost child's footsteps, the sudden stop and the "Someone who wasn't there" staircase make a textbook middle-grade cliffhanger. The only drag is "His face was white" repeating earlier phrasing just before the run.
