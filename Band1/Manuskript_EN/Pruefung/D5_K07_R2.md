# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 7 "Lina"

Reviewer: D5 (US middle-grade line editor, spooky-funny list)
Scope: Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_07.md only. German names and places, "Mrs." with German surnames, and short fragments at scary beats are house style and are not flagged.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L19 · "She couldn't shake the thought, even though she didn't know why yet." · UNCLEAR · Why what? The antecedent is vague, so a 10-year-old won't know what she doesn't know. · "She couldn't shake the thought."

2. L47 · "The cemetery was right across from the library." · RHYTHM · This orienting fact arrives after the description and after Shadow has already reacted. It reads as an afterthought, and the kids walked past it on the way in. · Move it to the front of the paragraph, or cut it.

3. L53 · "...as if he wanted to throw something off." · TRANSLATED · "Abschütteln" rendered as "throw off." The English idiom is "shake off." · "...as if he were shaking something off."

4. L69 · "She had looked at this building again and again since yesterday." · STIFF · "Again and again" plus "since yesterday" is a clunky time marker. · "She'd seen this building a hundred times by now."

5. L87 · "Kind of strange, right?" · RHYTHM · This echoes Mom's "Weird, huh?" four lines earlier, and both are filler tags. · Cut one.

6. L95 · "Nora went into the kitchen. Automatically." · STIFF · "Automatically" as a fragment reads like a translated adverb ("automatisch"). · "Nora headed for the kitchen without thinking."

7. L105 · "Nora's glass slipped out of her hand." · OTHER · Continuity: she only *wanted* a glass of water, and she never picked one up before seeing the window. · Add "She grabbed a glass from the shelf" before the window.

8. L119 · "Not again. Not one more time." · TRANSLATED · "Nicht noch einmal" rendered twice. In English, "Not one more time" is redundant and sounds slightly off. · "Not again. Not ever."

9. L125 · "Like an echo from long ago." · STIFF · An echo is a sound and these are written words. The line is also a vague literary cliché. · Cut, or "Like a message that had waited fifty years."

10. L131/L133 · "---" then "Then they heard it." · RHYTHM · The scene break interrupts a continuous moment (same room, seconds later), and "Then" right after a break is contradictory. · Remove the break.

11. L139 · "He sat upright. His back was straight." · RHYTHM · Saying the same thing twice. · Keep one.

12. L135 · "Claws on plaster." · OTHER · Small accuracy point: the wall is covered in wallpaper (L139, L145), so the sound is claws on paper. · "Claws on old paper."

13. L145 · "The dog didn't react." · TRANSLATED · "Reagierte nicht" rendered literally. "React" is clinical for a dog in US kid lit. · "Shadow ignored him."

14. L151/L159 · "In exactly the same spot." · RHYTHM · "Same spot" appears five times in the last 20 lines. The repetition is deliberate, but at this point it reads as stuck. · Keep two.

15. L161 · "As if he wanted to show them where they had to look." · STIFF · The closing line explains what the image already showed, and "where they had to look" is translated phrasing ("wo sie suchen mussten"). · End on L157 (Theo's movie line) or L155 ("There's something behind the wall").

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would I guess this was translated?** No. This is the most natural chapter of the four. Mom's dialog ("I've been on my feet since five. There's food in the fridge.") and Theo's lines ("Newspaper articles are the smaller miracle," "Best idea all day") are idiomatic. Only isolated phrases give it away ("didn't react," "throw something off," "Not one more time"), and a lay reader would not notice them.

2. **Voice:** Nora 4/5. "We won't forget you" is a genuine, earned emotional beat, and her quiet resolve suits a 12-year-old. Theo 4/5. He is funny where he needs to be, and his serious turn ("She lived there. And she's still there.") is well judged, although "No jokes. No sarcasm." tells us what he already shows.

3. **Ending:** Medium. The setup is great (the dog scratching at the wall and the bump under the wallpaper), but the final line explains the hook instead of landing it. Ending on Theo's "In movies, that's usually the line right before everybody screams" or on Nora's "There's something behind the wall" would be sharper.
