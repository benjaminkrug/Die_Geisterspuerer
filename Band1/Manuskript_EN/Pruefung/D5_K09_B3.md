# D5 Line Edit – Chapter 9: The Diary

Reviewer stance: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). German setting, "Mrs." + German surnames and short fragments at scary beats accepted as house style.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L7** · "Nora's pulse throbbed in her temples." · STIFF · Stock adult-thriller phrasing; kids feel their heart, not their temples. · *Her heart was pounding.*
2. **L11 (also L47, L119, and throughout)** · "It got ice-cold," Theo whispered. · TRANSLATED · "Ice-cold" shows up again and again. That reads like a calque of German *eiskalt*. American kids say "freezing." · Swap in "freezing" or "super cold" for at least half of them.
3. **L17** · "Should I read it out loud?" Nora said. · OTHER · A direct question tagged with "said" sounds slightly off here. US MG uses "asked" freely. · *Nora asked.*
4. **L37** · "One entry was about going up on the roof with a flashlight, because Lina wanted to count stars." · RHYTHM · At 21 words it is over the house limit, and it trails off on the "because" clause. · Split it: *One night she went up on the roof with a flashlight. She wanted to count stars.*
5. **L37** · "A normal girl's life." · TRANSLATED · This sounds like German *ein ganz normales Mädchenleben*. No American kid would phrase it this way. · *Just normal kid stuff.*
6. **L61** · "But mice don't scratch to a beat." · TRANSLATED · "To a beat" is *im Takt*. In English it suggests music or dancing. Theo echoing it at L63 makes it stand out more. · *But mice don't scratch in a rhythm.*
7. **L71** · "The round letters crowded closer together, as if Lina had written faster." · UNCLEAR · "The round letters" assumes the reader already knows her handwriting is round. Only "purple ink" was set up. · Set it up at L7: *Round, bubbly handwriting.*
8. **L93** · "At night someone stands in my room. I can't see it." · UNCLEAR · "Someone" switches to "it" mid-thought, and a 10-year-old will trip over that. · *I can't see them.*, or *something stands … I can't see it.*
9. **L103** · "Luna screamed. Really screamed. Like a person." · OTHER · This is the strongest image in the chapter and sits right at the "tingle, not nightmares" line. Flag it for the scare budget, and keep it only if it's intended. · *Luna howled. Like she was hurt.*
10. **L117** · "A hot stab went through her chest. Guilt. Hot and sharp." · RHYTHM · "Hot" appears twice in three words. It also comes right after Theo's "almost a laugh," so it undercuts that light beat, and it names the emotion ("Guilt") outright. · Cut "Guilt. Hot and sharp." or move it before Theo's line.
11. **L119** · "Her hands were ice-cold. A weight sat in her chest, heavy and tight." · RHYTHM · This is the third body cue in a row after L117 and L105. The stacking slows the scene down. · Keep one.
12. **L133** · "Shadow pressed his nose harder against Theo's arm." · OTHER · Repeats "pressed … harder" from L95. · *Shadow nudged Theo's arm.*
13. **L147** · "*October 30.* Only two entries left," Nora said quietly. · UNCLEAR · The diary date and Nora's own comment sit inside one set of quotation marks. A kid can't tell what is being read and what Nora is saying. · Put the comment first, then a new line for the reading: *"Only two entries left," Nora said. "October 30 …"*
14. **L147** · "She could feel it. The end was coming. The last pages." · RHYTHM · Three ways of saying the same thing. · Keep one.
15. **L161–163** · "Empty. / The next page was blank. White. Untouched." · RHYTHM · "Empty" lands as a strong one-word beat, and then the next line explains it again. · Cut "The next page was blank." and keep *White. Untouched.*
16. **L167** · "On November 2, 1974. The day before she died." · UNCLEAR · How do the kids know the date she died? If an earlier chapter established it, fine. If not, a reader will stop and wonder. · (Check continuity.)
17. **L179** · "Over the name. Over the little heart." · UNCLEAR · The heart was never shown. At L9 the title page only gives the name. · Add the heart at L9: *Lina's Diary ♡*, or "with a little heart over the i."
18. **L177** · "He just lay there, as if he knew they needed him." · OTHER · Tic: "as if he knew" also appears at L15 ("as if he knew something important") and again in Ch. 10. · Vary it or cut it here.
19. **L197** · "The cold in the apartment seemed to grow thicker. Closing in." · STIFF · "Seemed to grow thicker" is weak, and "Closing in" hangs with no subject. · *The cold crept closer.*

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No (borderline).** The diary voice is convincing and very American in 1974 terms (cotton candy, the fair, "Mom", "corner store"). Theo's dialogue is natural. Only a handful of tells give it away: "to a beat", "a normal girl's life", the constant "ice-cold". An attentive editor would notice those on a second read, but a kid reader would not.
2. **Voice:** Nora **4/5**. She is warm and believably 12, and the apology at L111 is lovely, but too much of her inner life goes through stacked body cues. Theo **4/5**. "Sure. Because chairs move by themselves all the time" and "I probably would've been even more scared" are spot-on, but he gets few jokes in a very heavy chapter. That's acceptable as one of the book's "no humor" exceptions.
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes.** "What's in the basement, Nora?", the creak under their feet, and Nora's quiet answer make a clean, classic hook.
