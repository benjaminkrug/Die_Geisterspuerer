# D5 – Final line edit, Chapters 14 & 15 (US middle-grade editor pass)

Scope: only what I would change before print. Deliberate choices (German street names, "Mrs." + German surname, "the Ring", fragments at scary beats, no common noun "shadow") are accepted and not flagged.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · suggestion

---

## Chapter 14: The Map

1. **L29** · "Ghost release." · TRANSLATED · In English "release" reads like a product launch or a prison release, not like setting a ghost free; the joke doesn't land. · "Freeing a ghost." (or "Ghost rescue.")

2. **L53** · "No," Nora said. "We did it." · TRANSLATED · The answer to "she never managed to help her" is "We did," not "We did it" (which sounds like "Wir haben es geschafft"). · "No," Nora said. "But we did."

3. **L145** · "Next to it, it said: *Lina V. – 1974.*" · READ-ALOUD · "it, it" stumbles when read aloud. · "Next to it, in small letters: *Lina V. – 1974.*"

4. **L163** · "But the map had been behind the loose board. Until a little while ago. And since then, Nora hadn't let go of it." · UNCLEAR / TRANSLATED · "Until a little while ago" as a separate fragment reads like a calque ("Bis vor kurzem") and breaks the logic a kid has to follow at the key clue. Not a scary beat, so the fragment isn't earning anything. · "But the map had been hidden behind the board until half an hour ago. And Nora hadn't let go of it since."

5. **L11** (minor) · "He lifted his head, yawned wide, and let it sink back down." · TRANSLATED · "let it sink back down" is a German construction ("ließ ihn sinken"). · "He lifted his head, yawned hugely, and dropped it back onto his paws."

**Would you guess this was translated? No.** Voice, jokes and pacing read as native. Items 1, 2 and 4 are the only spots where a sharp reader might sense a German original.

## Chapter 15: The Cemetery

1. **L29** · "Behind each one, a name. Behind each one, someone who couldn't go." · TRANSLATED / READ-ALOUD · Two problems: "behind" for a label next to a circle is German "hinter"; and "couldn't go" is ambiguous (a parent reading aloud will get a giggle about the bathroom). The meaning is "couldn't leave / move on." · "Next to each one, a name. Next to each one, someone who couldn't leave."

2. **L25** · "The elementary school on the Ring. Not yours." · UNCLEAR · To a US reader, a 10-year-old *is* in elementary school, so "Not yours" sounds like a different elementary school, which makes Theo's "Yet." confusing. · "The little kids' school on the Ring. Not yours." (or "The elementary school on the Ring. You're not there anymore.")

3. **L21** · "*Helene D. – 1921. Veil thin.*" · UNCLEAR · Cryptic notes are fine, but a 10-year-old won't know "veil" in the sense of the barrier between worlds, and nobody explains it. If it's a series term set up later, leave it; otherwise make it graspable. · "*Helene D. – 1921. Cold spot. Strong.*" or keep "Veil thin" and have Nora wonder what it means in one line.

4. **L57** · "Shadow stopped. In the middle of a step." · TRANSLATED · Calque of "mitten im Schritt." English has the idiom. · "Shadow stopped. Mid-step."

5. **L101** · "The figure lifted his head." · OTHER (consistency) · Two lines earlier the figure is "it" ("the gravestone behind it"); switching to "his" in the next beat is jarring. · "The man lifted his head." (He's already described as "the outline of a man.")

6. **L105** · "The gate had just slammed shut." · READ-ALOUD / UNCLEAR · "just" reads as "a moment ago," not "on its own." · "The gate had slammed shut all by itself."

7. **L185** · "The floorboard had an edge, barely visible." · UNCLEAR · Every floorboard has edges; the point is a gap or seam that shows it's loose. · "Around it ran a thin crack, barely visible."

8. **L187** · "Without a word, Theo handed her the screwdriver." · OTHER (check) · "the screwdriver" assumes the reader knows Theo has one. If no earlier chapter sets this up, use "Theo pulled a screwdriver out of his pocket and handed it to her." If it's set up, leave it.

9. **L189** (minor) · "Sealed with red sealing wax." · READ-ALOUD · "sealed ... sealing" sounds clunky aloud. · "Sealed with a blob of red wax."

### Special check: L53, "The evening sun stretched long stripes of shade across the sidewalk."

**Verdict: needs a fix.** The grammar is fine, but the sun is the wrong subject (the sun doesn't make stripes of shade; trees and buildings do), and "stripes of shade" sounds like a translated compound ("Schattenstreifen"). It doesn't sound like natural US prose. Two options without "shadow(s)":

- **A:** "The evening sun hung low, and the trees laid long dark stripes across the sidewalk."
- **B:** "Low evening sun slanted between the buildings and striped the sidewalk gold and gray."

(A keeps the "shade" image; B is warmer and sets up the contrast with the cold that comes later. Either works.)

**Would you guess this was translated? No, but only just.** Items 1 ("Behind each one ... couldn't go") and the sun/shade line are the two places where a careful reader could tell. Fix those and it reads as native.
