# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 2 "Shadow"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Only Kapitel_02.md was read. The German setting is intentional, so German names and places are flagged only where a 10-year-old American reader would get lost.

Format: line · sentence · category · why · suggestion

## Findings

1. **L5** · "The dog sat in front of the door with the SILBER name card and didn't move." · TRANSLATED · "Name card" is a literal rendering. On a door, Americans say "nameplate" or "the name by the doorbell." · *…in front of the door with SILBER on the nameplate…*

2. **L5** · "His dark hackles were up." · STIFF · Giving the hackles a color is odd. The fur is dark, and "hackles" means the raised ridge. · *The fur along his back stood up.*

3. **L25** · "A crack as wide as a finger ran down the wall on the right, from the ceiling to the floor." · TRANSLATED · "As wide as a finger" sounds like the German *fingerbreit*. · *A finger-wide crack ran from ceiling to floor.*

4. **L25** · "A lamp with a shattered shade lay next to it." · RHYTHM · It sits in a run of verbless fragments ("A chair on its side. A small table…"), and the full verb "lay" breaks the pattern. It also repeats "lay" from earlier in the paragraph. · *A lamp with a shattered shade.*

5. **L45** · "In one step, the temperature dropped like a stone. It fell twenty degrees." · UNCLEAR · The second sentence says the same thing again. A US reader will also read "twenty degrees" as Fahrenheit, which is a much smaller drop than the scene intends. · Cut "It fell twenty degrees," or write *It felt like winter had walked in.*

6. **L41 / L45** · "Nora's stomach tightened." / "Nora's skin tightened." · RHYTHM · Two "tightened" body tells within four lines. · Change one, e.g. *Nora's stomach clenched.*

7. **L11, L47, L59 (and L45)** · "In July." / "Except it was July…" / "In July. In the middle of summer." · RHYTHM · "July" and "summer" are hammered five times in one scene. By the third time it loses its punch. · Keep L59 (the breath-cloud moment) and drop one or two of the earlier ones.

8. **L63** · "There must be a cold pipe running under the floor." · STIFF · "There must be" sounds like an adult reasoning. A 12-year-old would blurt it out. · *"It's probably a cold-water pipe under the floor. Or—"*

9. **L69** · "…with a look that said not one step farther." · TRANSLATED · This reads like *keinen Schritt weiter*. The phrase is unmarked, so it blurs into the narration. · *…with a look that said, Don't even think about it.* (in italics)

10. **L87** · "Calmer than anything up there on the third floor." · UNCLEAR · This implies that whatever is up there is calm, which works against the scene. Kids will trip on it. · *Calmer than she'd been since they'd climbed those stairs.*

11. **L91** · "By the way, I ran into one of the neighbors downstairs. Mrs. Held. She talked like she hadn't seen anyone in weeks." · STIFF · An info-dump planted in Mom's first line, with "By the way" before any conversation has started. "Mrs. Held" also reads like the English verb *held*. · Move the neighbor mention after the dog scene, or cut it here.

12. **L133** · "Something about this dog pulled at her." · TRANSLATED · A literal-sounding abstraction. · *There was something about this dog.*

13. **L133** · "Still, she had the feeling he belonged here." · STIFF · An abstract emotion where the series style wants a physical one. · *Still, when he looked at her, her chest went warm.*

14. **L147** · "But her thoughts were upstairs." · TRANSLATED · Close to *mit den Gedanken war sie oben*. · *But her mind kept drifting upstairs.*

15. **L151** · "Theo fed Shadow half a slice of salami." · TRANSLATED · On a US pizza this is pepperoni. Salami on pizza signals a European source. Fine if the German flavor is intentional, but it's a tell. · *a piece of pepperoni*, or keep it on purpose.

16. **L165** · "For the first time since they got here, it felt something like home." · RHYTHM · "It felt something like" is grammatically wobbly. · *…it felt a little bit like home.*

17. **L169** · "Kirchgasse was quiet in the evening light." · UNCLEAR · A 10-year-old won't know Kirchgasse is a street. Nothing in this chapter says so. · *Down below, Kirchgasse Street was quiet…* or *Their street, Kirchgasse, was quiet…*

18. **L173** · "A flicker on the wall, like a dark shape moving without any light to cast it." · TOO ADULT · The idea is a shadow with no light source. That's an abstract optics concept, and the sentence is hard to parse. · *A dark shape slid across the wall. But nothing in the room had moved.*

19. **L199** · "The knocking came from the wall. Right behind her headboard. Directly above her." · UNCLEAR · Wall behind her head and directly above her (the ceiling) are different places. Since Mrs. Silber lives upstairs, the reader can't picture where the sound is. · Pick one, e.g. *From the ceiling. Right above her bed.*

20. **L205** · "Always the same. Always the same rhythm." · RHYTHM · Redundant doubling. · *Always the same rhythm.*

## Summary

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, mildly. The dialogue (Theo especially) sounds native. The tells are in the narration: "name card," "her thoughts were upstairs," "a look that said not one step farther," salami pizza, Celsius-sized "twenty degrees," and the dense run of verbless fragments. Each one is small, but together they add up.

**(2) Voice:**
- Nora (12): **3/5.** Her rational side comes through ("Cold pipes. Bad construction."), but her lines are few and flat ("Quiet." "Fine." "Everything's fine."). She has no line that's clearly hers.
- Theo (10): **4/5.** "Cozy. Very inviting. Five stars," "Drama queen / Survival expert," and "It's scientifically proven / Says who? / Me. Just now." are spot on. "Until the end of the world" is a touch long.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Solid but a little soft. The knocking plus the dog going rigid is a good setup. Ending on "a low whine" lets the tension fall when it should spike. Consider ending on the third set of knocks, or on Shadow's single growl at the wall. The headboard/ceiling confusion (L199) also blunts the climax.
