# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 7: Lina

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). Findings only; no rewrites.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L7 · "A girl lived in our building. Fifty years ago. And died. The same age as you." · RHYTHM · "And died." is wedged between two time and age facts, so the key word lands in the wrong place. · Put "She died." last.
2. L19 · "The thought wouldn't let go of her, even though she didn't know why yet." · TRANSLATED · "Der Gedanke ließ sie nicht los." · "She couldn't shake the thought."
3. L35 · "Alert. Attentive." · RHYTHM · This adjective pair for Shadow has now come up in Ch. 5, 6 and 7, and it has become a tic.
4. L37 · "That we found something out." · STIFF · Odd word order (German "etwas herausgefunden"). · "That we found out something."
5. L53 · "He turned his eyes away from the cemetery and looked at Nora." · TRANSLATED · "Wandte den Blick ab." · "He looked away from the cemetery, up at Nora."
6. L69 · "She had looked at this building again and again since yesterday." · UNCLEAR · "Since yesterday" raises questions (did they move in yesterday?), and "again and again" is padding.
7. L83 · "Strange business." · BRITISH · It has a British or old-fashioned ring. An American mom would say "Weird, huh?"
8. L85 · "Does the neighbor know her?" · STIFF · The tense is off for a missing person. · "Did the neighbor know her?"
9. L87 · "She sorted a letter onto the pile." · TRANSLATED · "Sortierte einen Brief auf den Stapel." "Sorted onto" isn't idiomatic. · "She tossed a letter onto the pile."
10. L87 · "Odd, isn't it?" · BRITISH · The tag question plus "odd" sounds British. · "Kind of weird, right?"
11. L89 · "I'm going to bed early tonight." · UNCLEAR · It's afternoon (L99), yet the bedroom door clicks shut right away. Readers will ask why Mom is going to bed now. · "I'm going to lie down for a bit."
12. L105 · "Nora's glass slipped out of her hand." · UNCLEAR · She only *wanted* a glass of water (L95). We never see her pick one up.
13. L117 · "Nora didn't touch the glass." · UNCLEAR · It's ambiguous: the window glass, or the drinking glass that just broke? · "Nora didn't touch the window."
14. L125 · "Silent. Still. Like an echo from another time." · STIFF · A stock literary phrase. An echo is a sound, so the image doesn't fit silent letters.
15. L133 · "Then they heard it." · RHYTHM · The same transition as Ch. 5 L209. Readers will notice it as a formula.
16. L139 · "He sat upright. His back was straight." · STIFF · Says the same thing twice.
17. L159 · "Patiently. Tirelessly." · STIFF · "Tirelessly" is a bookish adverb, and the adverb pair adds nothing.

## Chapter verdict

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Mildly, yes. This is the most natural chapter of the four. The window scene, "Don't forget", is lovely and reads native. Small tells remain ("turned his eyes away", "wouldn't let go of her", "sorted a letter onto"), and Mom sounds slightly British or dubbed ("Strange business", "Odd, isn't it?").

**(2) Voice (1–5):** Nora 3: her emotional core works ("We won't forget you"), but her lines are still mostly functional. Theo 4: good ("Newspaper articles are the smaller miracle", "the line right before everybody screams"), and his serious beat at L15 earns its weight.

**(3) Ending strong?** Moderate. "There's something behind the wall" is a good hook, but the final line ("As if he wanted to show them where they had to look") explains instead of teasing, and it ends on a soft note. Ending on Theo's "everybody screams" line or on the hollow bump would be stronger.
