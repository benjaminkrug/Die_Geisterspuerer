# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 15: The Cemetery

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12). Mark and explain only; no rewrites.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

- **L17** · "'Angry period. Grave period,' Theo read." · UNCLEAR · Reading the punctuation aloud is a fine kid joke, but two things get in the way. (a) In English "grave" means both "serious" and "tomb," and the text never uses that pun, so kids may be confused about which one is meant. (b) "Period" said twice by a 10-year-old boy invites a giggle the author didn't plan. The line comes back again at L39. · Suggest: let Theo use the pun ("Angry. Grave. As in, *in* a grave? Or as in, *seriously* angry? Both, probably.")
- **L25** · "The elementary school on the Ring. Not yours." · UNCLEAR · "The Ring" means nothing to a US kid. It reads like a ring you wear or a boxing ring. The German setting is deliberate, but this one needs to be recognizable as a place name. · Suggest: "on Ringstrasse."
- **L25** · "The elementary school … Not yours." · OTHER (cultural) · In the US, a 10-year-old is still in elementary school (4th/5th grade). An American reader will expect Theo to be in elementary school and will stumble over "Not yours." · Suggest: name a different kind of school ("The old girls' school on Ringstrasse"), or skip the school-type contrast.
- **L49** · "But he got up and got the leash." · RHYTHM · "Got up and got" sounds clumsy. · Suggest: "But he got up and grabbed the leash."
- **L53** · "The evening sun stretched long silhouettes across the sidewalk." · STIFF · The usual phrase is "long shadows." I understand it is avoided because the dog is named Shadow, but "silhouettes" can't lie on a sidewalk. · Suggest: "The evening sun stretched their outlines long across the sidewalk." or "The evening sun was low and gold."
- **L55** · "Until they were standing in front of the library." · RHYTHM · Static. A moving scene wants a moving verb. · Suggest: "Until they reached the library."
- **L63** · "The rush of the cars." · TRANSLATED · "Rush" for traffic noise is a literal rendering (Rauschen). In English "rush" suggests rush hour. · Suggest: "The hum of traffic."
- **L63** · "In the middle of the warm evening sun, the air turned cold." · STIFF · You can't be "in the middle of" the sun. · Suggest: "Even in the warm evening sun, the air turned cold."
- **L65** · "Nora could feel it in her bones." · OTHER (repetition) · "In her bones" was already used at an emotional peak in Ch. 13 (L117). Used twice, it starts to feel like a crutch. · Suggest: "Nora felt it through the soles of her shoes."
- **L93–101** · "He stood still. … The figure lifted its head." · OTHER (consistency) · The pronoun switches from "he" to "its" within eight lines. · Pick one. "He" fits the book's "ghosts are people" theme better.
- **L105** · "A bang that rang down the whole street. … The gate had just slammed shut." · RHYTHM · Iron on iron makes a "clang," not a "bang." "Slammed shut" also appears twice in three lines (L103 and L105). · Suggest: "A clang that rang down the whole street." Cut or vary the second "slammed shut."
- **L159** · "Nora had to laugh." · TRANSLATED · A classic German tell (musste lachen). In English "had to" means obligation. · Suggest: "Nora laughed. She couldn't help it."
- **L183** · "Right on one particular floorboard." · STIFF · "One particular" sounds like an adult narrator. · Suggest: "Right on top of one floorboard."
- **L187** · "Without a word, Theo handed her his letter opener." · UNCLEAR · Why does a 10-year-old carry a letter opener up the stairs at night? If an earlier chapter sets this up, a two-word reminder helps ("the letter opener he'd taken from the study"). If not, it reads like a continuity slip. · Suggest: a pocketknife, or a short callback.

## End-of-chapter assessment

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No**, though only narrowly. The action and the speech scene sound native, and the cemetery sequence is strong. A US editor would raise an eyebrow at "had to laugh," "the rush of the cars," "on the Ring" and the school-type confusion. They read like translation smudges, but they don't sink the chapter.
2. **Voice:** Nora 4/5. "We keep going," which takes the wind out of Theo's speech, is a great character beat. Her narration is solid, with a few stiff spots. Theo 5/5. The ghost finger, the zoo line, "I see that he sees something" and the forgotten third point are the funniest material in the book's final stretch.
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes.** The sealed envelope, "For those who come after me," and then the shaky "If you are reading this, I am no longer here" make a classic, effective cliffhanger. The only weak spot in the run-up is the unexplained letter opener.
