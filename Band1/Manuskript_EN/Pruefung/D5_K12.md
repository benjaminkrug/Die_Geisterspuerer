# D5 Line Edit – Chapter 12: Please

Reviewer stance: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). German setting, "Mrs." + German surnames and short fragments at scary beats accepted as house style.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L5** · "One single, sharp sound that echoed through the whole apartment." · TRANSLATED · "One single" is a calque of *ein einziger*. English uses one word or the other. · *One sharp bark.*
2. **L7** · "Stiff. Upright. Legs locked, hackles up, his eyes fixed on the bedroom door." · RHYTHM · "Stiff", "Upright" and "Legs locked" all say the same thing. · *Legs locked, hackles up, eyes on the door.*
3. **L11** · "Nora got goosebumps." · STIFF · Flat, and weaker than the bark that set it off. · *Goosebumps raced up Nora's arms.*
4. **L11** · "He had growled, whined, refused." · UNCLEAR · "Refused" needs an object (refused what?). It comes from German *verweigert*. · *He'd growled, whined, refused to go into rooms.*
5. **L15** · "That evening, the knocking had stopped before they worked up the nerve to open the door. Nora had lain awake for a long time." · UNCLEAR · This flashback arrives in the middle of a scare, after the bark, and kids will lose track of when things happen. It also resolves Ch. 11's knocking cliffhanger in a single throwaway line. · Move it to the very top as a short bridge, or give the knocking a real payoff.
6. **L19** · "The cold hit her like a punch." · OTHER · Verbatim repeat of Ch. 9's first line ("The cold hit Nora like a punch."). · Vary it: *The cold slapped her awake.*
7. **L19** · "Her breath came out of her mouth in a white cloud." · STIFF · "Out of her mouth" is redundant. · *Her breath puffed white.*
8. **L25** · "Nora turned around. Her fingers went cold." · RHYTHM · Everything is already freezing, so "Her fingers went cold" adds nothing and delays the reveal of the windows. · Cut the second sentence.
9. **L41** · "A presence. Stronger than ever before." · TOO ADULT · "A presence" is ghost-hunter TV vocabulary. Not wrong, but a 12-year-old narrator would put it more concretely. · *Someone was here. She could feel it.*
10. **L69** · "Only the ticking of the kitchen clock." · TRANSLATED · The third chapter in a row with the anchorless "Only…" silence beat (*Nur noch …*). · *Silence, except for the kitchen clock.*
11. **L73** · "She thought of Mrs. Silber's notebook. Of the method." · STIFF · "The method" sounds like a textbook. · *Of what Mrs. Silber had written.*
12. **L95** · "…and his eyes had a calm shine." · TRANSLATED · "A calm shine" doesn't work in English (*ein ruhiger Glanz*). · *and his eyes were calm.*
13. **L111** · "But the shaking didn't come from the frost." · TRANSLATED · German *Frost* means cold in general. In English, "frost" is ice crystals on surfaces. · *didn't come from the cold.*
14. **L129** · "Frost spread across the window glass in feathery patterns." · OTHER · Continuity: if frost covers the glass, the condensation words would freeze over or disappear. Later (L181–187) the words fade and the glass is warm. Make sure the frost is on a different window, or melts on the page. · (Continuity query.)
15. **L169** · "Like a breath that was finally let out." · STIFF · Passive voice, which breaks the house rule, and limp at the emotional peak. · *Like a breath someone had held for fifty years.*
16. **L175** · "One single, long dog sigh." · TRANSLATED · "One single" again (see L5). · *One long dog sigh.*
17. **L181** · "All gone. Erased, as if the warmth had wiped them away." · RHYTHM · "All gone", "Erased" and "wiped them away" are three ways of saying it. · *All gone, as if the warmth had wiped them away.*
18. **L201** · "Mrs. Silber's apartment was right above them. On the third floor." · OTHER · US floor numbering: if this is a German *dritter Stock*, an American reader counts it as the fourth floor. Keep the numbering consistent across the book, or drop the number. · *Right above them.*
19. **L209** · "His tail thumped the floor once. Then he was still again." · OTHER · A tail thump reads as friendly or happy, which softens the "UP" hook. If it's meant as a hint that whatever is upstairs is benign, fine. If not, it lets the tension out. · End on Theo's question or on *Shadow stared at the ceiling. And growled.*

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No.** This is the most American-sounding chapter of the four. The tombstone joke, hot chocolate with marshmallows, "Next time, maybe not at three in the morning", and "Like a fall morning. Not like a freezer." all read as natural US middle grade. The tells are small and fixable: "one single" twice, "frost" for cold, "a calm shine", and the "Only…" silence beat.
2. **Voice:** Nora **4/5**. She takes charge believably ("Now. Tonight."), and the reading-aloud ritual is moving. Her interior still leans on physical-cue formulas. Theo **5/5**. He balances fear, tears and jokes perfectly ("Her room is your room.", "I said okay, Nora. Before I change my mind.").
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes, with a caveat.** THANK YOU followed by UP is an excellent turn from release to new threat, and Theo's "What's up there, Nora?" echoes the Ch. 9 hook nicely. The last line (tail thump) softens the hook. Cut it or make it ominous.
