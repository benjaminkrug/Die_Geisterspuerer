# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 13 "The Release" (R2)

Reviewer: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Scope: Kapitel_13.md only. Fragments at scary or emotional beats are treated as house style. They are flagged only when they repeat or blur meaning.

## Findings

1. **L7** · "Sometime during the night, Nora had given it to him." · STIFF · The past-perfect aside lands in the middle of a live moment and reads like a stage note. · *Suggestion:* "Nora had handed it to him sometime last night."
2. **L11** · "Her heart was calm. For the first time in days, it was calm." · STIFF · "Her heart was calm" states a feeling, and it's flat. The chapter shows emotion physically everywhere else. · *Suggestion:* "Her heart wasn't pounding. For the first time in days."
3. **L17** · "At the first page, where the name was written in a girl's handwriting." · UNCLEAR · "the name" is vague. A 10-year-old may not connect it to Lina right away. · *Suggestion:* "where Lina had written her name in round, girlish letters."
4. **L19** · "“Should I bring it?” he said." · OTHER · Using "said" after a question is grammatical, but here it reads slightly off. "asked" is more natural, or drop the tag. · *Suggestion:* drop the tag.
5. **L29** · "Third floor." · TRANSLATED · German floor counting ("3. Stock") equals the American *fourth* floor. A US reader will picture the wrong height. Check this against the rest of the book. · *Suggestion:* "Top floor." (avoids the problem)
6. **L35** (also L195) · "Moonlight fell through the kitchen window…" · TRANSLATED · "Light fell through" is a classic German calque (*fiel durch*). It is used twice in this chapter. · *Suggestion:* "Moonlight spilled through…" / "came in through…"
7. **L41** · "The room Shadow had stayed away from on the first day. He had planted himself sideways… He hadn't let them in." · RHYTHM · Three past-perfect recap sentences in a row stall the walk toward the door. One is enough. · *Suggestion:* cut to "The room Shadow had refused to enter."
8. **L55** · "She stood in the dark and felt the presence." · TOO ADULT · "Felt the presence" is séance or adult-horror vocabulary and sounds translated (*spürte die Anwesenheit*). · *Suggestion:* "She could feel someone there."
9. **L55** · "Close. Right in front of her. Everywhere." · UNCLEAR · "Right in front of her" and "Everywhere" contradict each other. A kid will stumble on it rather than feel the intended dreaminess. · *Suggestion:* drop "Right in front of her."
10. **L61** · "He squeezed past Nora's leg through the door." · UNCLEAR · Nora is already inside the room, so Shadow can't squeeze past her *through the door*. The blocking is confused. · *Suggestion:* "He slipped in through the door behind her."
11. **L61** · "Without hesitating. Without growling." · RHYTHM · This is nearly identical to L31 ("No hesitating. No growling."). The second use reads as a copy, not a deliberate echo. · *Suggestion:* vary or cut one.
12. **L61** · "He turned around once in a circle, the way dogs do before they lie down." · STIFF · "Turned around once in a circle" is redundant. · *Suggestion:* "He turned in a circle, the way dogs do…"
13. **L89** · "“Yeah.” His voice caught." · OTHER · This echoes "Nora's breath caught" (L81) eight lines earlier. · *Suggestion:* "His voice cracked."
14. **L91** · "“Tell her we know her.”" · TRANSLATED · In English this sounds like "we're acquainted." The intended meaning is "we know who she is / what happened to her." · *Suggestion:* "Tell her we know who she is."
15. **L119** · "Now the light was beaming. Not blinding. Like a candle in a dark room." · STIFF · "Beaming" (*strahlte*) clashes with "like a candle." A candle doesn't beam. · *Suggestion:* "Now the light was glowing."
16. **L121–125** · "Then it got smaller." / "“The light's getting smaller.”" / "It shrank." · RHYTHM · The same information comes three times in a row. Theo's line only repeats the narration, which deflates the most important moment of the book. · *Suggestion:* cut L121 and let Theo's whisper carry it.
17. **L141** · "“…In case my teacher asks on Monday what I did this weekend.”" · OTHER · The joke works. But Chapter 16 places the story "in the middle of July," which is summer break for a US reader, so "teacher on Monday" is a continuity snag. Also, "watched a ghost go" is a bit vague. · *Suggestion:* check the school calendar, or "In case anyone asks what I did this summer."
18. **L145** · "Theo murmured." · OTHER · This is the second "murmured" for Theo in four lines (L141). · *Suggestion:* "Theo asked."
19. **L165** · "Someone had listened to her. And she had gone." · UNCLEAR · "Had gone" is soft and slightly ambiguous (gone where? left the room?). At this emotional point the line should land cleanly. · *Suggestion:* "And now she was free to go." / "And now she was gone."
20. **L171** · "“…all I felt was how it felt for her.”" · STIFF · The doubled "felt" is clunky in a 12-year-old's mouth. · *Suggestion:* "all I could feel was what she felt."
21. **L195** · "Her knees cracked." · OTHER · US kids would say knees *popped*. "Cracked" isn't wrong, but it reads older. · *Suggestion:* "Her knees popped."
22. **L197** · "Gravenstedt lay in front of her like a blanket of darkness and lights." · TRANSLATED · "The city lay before her" is a German construction (*lag vor ihr*), and "blanket of darkness and lights" is a mixed image. · *Suggestion:* "Gravenstedt spread out below her, dark and dotted with lights."
23. **L205** · "Like lightning striking underground." · UNCLEAR · You can't see underground lightning, so the simile produces no picture. · *Suggestion:* "Like a spark from a broken wire."
24. **L209** · "Her fingers on the windowsill turned white." · TRANSLATED · This is understandable, but the American idiom is knuckles. · *Suggestion:* "Her knuckles went white on the windowsill."

## Summary

**(1) Would you guess this was translated?** **No, but it's borderline.** A 10-year-old wouldn't notice. The emotional core (Shadow lying down and sighing, "for one heartbeat, Nora was Lina," "Believe me." / "We did.") reads as native and is genuinely moving. A professional editor would still sense a source language behind it from a handful of tells. These are "moonlight fell through" (twice), "the city lay in front of her," "felt the presence," "tell her we know her" and the German floor count.

**(2) Voice:**
- Nora (12): **4/5.** She's quiet and steady, and her short lines ("Say her name," "'Believe me.'") are strong. The "how it felt for her" line is the one place she sounds clumsy.
- Theo (10): **4/5.** "If the dog goes in, we go in," "He's about to start snoring," and the teacher joke all carry the voice. He's still believably ten in a tearful scene. The repeated "the light's getting smaller" line gives him nothing to do.

**(3) Is the chapter ending strong?** **Yes.** The cold blue glow is a sharp contrast to Lina's warm gold. Theo missed it, which is good: Nora is alone with the knowledge. Shadow's low growl is a clean cliffhanger. One caution: "And growled. Low. Deep." is a familiar series closer. If earlier chapters end the same way, it could start to feel formulaic.
