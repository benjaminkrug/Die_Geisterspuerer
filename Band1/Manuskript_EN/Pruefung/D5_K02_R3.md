# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 2 ("Shadow"), Round 3

Reviewer: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). I read only `Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_02.md`. The German setting is intentional, so German street names, lever door handles and European floor numbering are not flagged on their own.

Format: line · "sentence" · CATEGORY · why · suggestion

---

## Findings

1. **L5** · "The dog sat in front of the door with the SILBER name card and didn't move." · TRANSLATED · "Name card" reads like *Namensschild*. On a US door it's a nameplate or a name on the door. · "…in front of the door with SILBER on the nameplate…"

2. **L25** · "A finger-wide crack ran down the wall on the right, from the ceiling to the floor." · STIFF · "Finger-wide" is a European measuring habit. US kids say "a crack as wide as a finger" or just "a long crack." · "A crack as wide as her finger ran from the ceiling to the floor."

3. **L31** · "Quiet." · TRANSLATED · A bare "Quiet." as a command sounds like *Leise!* A US 12-year-old says "Shh." or "Be quiet." · "Shh."

4. **L33** · "Happy to." · TOO ADULT · It sounds like an office reply. The rest of Theo's line is great. · "Fine. I'm quiet. I'm the quietest person in the world. Can we go now?"

5. **L45** · "In one step, the temperature dropped like a stone. It fell twenty degrees." · UNCLEAR · A US reader will think in Fahrenheit, where twenty degrees is a mild drop. The double verb ("dropped… fell") also stalls the rhythm. · "In one step, the air went from summer to winter."

6. **L47** · "Except it was July, and the sun had been shining through the kitchen windows." · OTHER · This is the third "July" in the chapter so far (L11, L47, L59). It also says "windows" when L37 mentions a single windowsill. · Cut "it was July, and"; use "kitchen window."

7. **L49** · "He sat down sideways across the hallway and blocked the way." · STIFF · "Blocked the way" is flat, and "sideways across" is a little heavy. · "He planted himself across the hallway like a furry gate."

8. **L59** · "In July. In the middle of summer." · RHYTHM · This repeats the July beat yet again, which waters down the breath reveal. · "In the middle of summer."

9. **L63** · "There's got to be a cold pipe under the floor." · UNCLEAR · A 10-year-old won't know what a "cold pipe" is or why it would chill a room. Rational Nora would name something real. · "There's got to be a broken AC vent. Or—"

10. **L69** · "And the dog sat in front of her like a wall of dark fur…" · RHYTHM · "Wall" was already used for the cold at L45 ("hit Nora like a wall"), so the image doesn't land twice. · "…like a big dark furry roadblock…"

11. **L77** · "Theo leaned against the wall, both hands on his chest, as if he had to keep his heart from bursting." · STIFF · "Keep his heart from bursting" is formal for a comic beat. · "…as if his heart might jump right out."

12. **L87** · "Calmer than anything up there on the third floor." · UNCLEAR · The logic flips. They just fled because the thing upstairs was not calm, so "the dog was calmer than anything up there" reads backwards. · "A lot calmer than whatever was up there on the third floor."

13. **L91** · "By the way, I ran into the neighbor downstairs, Mrs. Held. She talked like she hadn't seen anyone in weeks." · STIFF · "By the way" is a bolted-on info-dump that Mom delivers before she even says hi properly. The appositive "the neighbor downstairs, Mrs. Held" sounds written, not spoken. · "Oh, I met Mrs. Held downstairs. That woman can *talk*."

14. **L97** · "Her eyes went from Nora to Theo and then to the dog, who was sitting in the middle of the hallway, sweeping his tail across the floor." · RHYTHM · At about 27 words with a trailing "who" clause, it's the longest sentence in the chapter, and it sits right where we want a comic snap. · "Her eyes went from Nora to Theo. Then to the dog. He sat in the middle of the hallway, sweeping his tail across the floor."

15. **L121** · "He looked up at Mom." · RHYTHM · "Mom" appears three times in two sentences. · "He looked up at her."

16. **L125** · "Theo punched his fist in the air." · TRANSLATED · The American idiom is "pumped his fist." "Punched the air" also works, but "punched his fist in the air" is a hybrid. · "Theo pumped his fist. 'Yes!'"

17. **L131** · "He had lain down next to Theo." · STIFF · It's grammatically correct but textbook. · "He'd flopped down next to Theo."

18. **L133** · "Still, she had the feeling he belonged here." · TRANSLATED · "Had the feeling" is a calque of *hatte das Gefühl*, and it's an abstract emotion. · "Still, something told her he belonged here."

19. **L133** · "And that scared her almost more than the cold upstairs." · UNCLEAR · A 10-year-old won't follow why a dog belonging with them is scary. The leap needs one more step. · "And she had no idea why. That scared her almost more than the cold upstairs."

20. **L137** · "Softly. Without a sound." · RHYTHM · An adverb fragment followed by its synonym is redundant. · "Without a sound."

21. **L141** · "A quick flick, as if he had recognized the name." · UNCLEAR · A dog can't "recognize" a name he has just been given. If this is meant as foreshadowing, make it explicit. · "…as if he already knew the name."

22. **L151** · "Theo fed Shadow half a slice of pepperoni." · UNCLEAR · Is it half a slice of pizza or one pepperoni slice? · "Theo fed Shadow half a slice of pepperoni pizza."

23. **L169** · "Nora stayed where she was and looked out the window." · STIFF · It reads like *blieb sitzen*, and it's not clear where "where she was" is. · "Nora curled up by the window."

24. **L169** · "Kirchgasse was quiet in the evening light." · UNCLEAR · Nothing tells a US kid that Kirchgasse is a street. Keep the name, but anchor it. · "Their street, Kirchgasse, was quiet in the evening light."

25. **L173** · "A flicker on the wall, like a dark shape moving without any light to cast it." · TOO ADULT · "Without any light to cast it" asks the reader to reason about how shadows are made, and that's abstract mid-scare. · "A dark shape slid across the wall. But nothing was there to make a shadow."

26. **L173** · "Just the empty wall in the evening light." · RHYTHM · "Evening light" repeats from four lines earlier. · "Just the empty wall."

27. **L177** · "Next to the bed, a moving box served as her nightstand." · STIFF · "Served as" is formal. · "A moving box next to the bed was her nightstand for now."

28. **L187** · "He was standing now. Motionless. Focused on something Nora couldn't see." · OTHER (POV) · Nora's eyes are shut and the dog is behind a door, so she can't know he's motionless or focused. Close-third POV breaks here. · "She pictured him standing there, stiff, staring at something she couldn't see."

29. **L185 / L191 / L211** · "Then the breathing stopped." / "Then she heard it." / "Then, very softly, a low whine." · RHYTHM · Three "Then" openers in the final scare sequence flatten the escalation. · Cut the first "Then": "The breathing stopped."

30. **L199** · "The knocking came from the wall. Right behind her headboard. Directly above her." · UNCLEAR · Behind the headboard (wall) and above her (ceiling) are two different places. L201 says the sound comes from Mrs. Silber's apartment, which is upstairs, so it should be the ceiling. · "The knocking came from the ceiling. Right above her bed."

31. **L205** · "Always the same. Always the same rhythm." · RHYTHM · It's redundant, and the second sentence adds nothing. · "Three times. Pause. Three times. Always the same."

32. **L207** · "Her fingers dug into the fabric." · STIFF · "Fabric" is a catalogue word, and a kid would say "blanket." · "Her fingers dug into the blanket."

## Summary questions

### (1) Would I guess this was translated?
**Yes, but only narrowly.** A casual kid reader wouldn't notice. A US editor would, because of a cluster of small calques and European habits: "name card," "finger-wide," the bare "Quiet." command, "punched his fist in the air," "had the feeling," "stayed where she was," and a temperature drop that only makes sense in Celsius. None of them is glaring, but together they give it a faint "imported" feel. The dialogue itself mostly reads as native.

### (2) Voices (1–5)
- **Nora (12): 3.5/5.** Her rational, clipped narration works ("Dust. Stale air. Silence."), and she gets real physical fear. Her spoken lines are thin, though ("Quiet.", "Wait.", "Fine. We'll go."), and her one rational argument ("cold pipe") is vague. She needs one sharper, smarter line of her own.
- **Theo (10): 4.5/5.** This is the chapter's engine. "Cozy. Very inviting. Five stars.", "Smartest thing you've said all day.", "Survival expert.", and "scientifically proven / Says who? / Me. Just now." are all strong, age-true and funny. The one false note is "Happy to."

### (3) Is the ending strong?
**Mostly yes.** The thump-thump-thump pattern, the dog going silent, and then the whine together make a good cliffhanger with a real tingle. Three things weaken it: the wall/ceiling confusion about where the knocking comes from (L199), the POV slip at L187, and the repeated "Then" openers. A low whine is also a slightly soft final note. It could end harder on a beat that escalates the knocking, for example "Then the knocking stopped. And Shadow began to whine." Fixing L199 matters most, because the reader has to know exactly where the sound is.
