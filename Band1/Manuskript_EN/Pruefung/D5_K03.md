# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 3: "Three Knocks"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Read in isolation, chapters 2–4 only.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L5** · "Nora lay frozen under the covers. Her heart hammered against her ribs." · OTHER · This repeats the end of Ch. 2 almost word for word (Ch2 L207). A short recap is fine, but this one stalls. · → Open on a new detail instead: the clock, the count, the cold.
2. **L9** · "Pipes didn't knock to a beat." · TRANSLATED · "To a beat" mirrors *im Takt*. It works in Theo's mouth later (L39) but sounds odd as Nora's narration. · → "Pipes didn't knock in a pattern."
3. **L13** · "That was worse than growling. As if he had heard it before." · UNCLEAR · The logic jump is missing. Why does waiting mean he has heard it before? · → "That was worse than growling. It was like he'd heard this before."
4. **L15** · "Only the tips of his hair stuck out." · TRANSLATED · A calque of *Haarspitzen*. · → "Only a tuft of hair stuck out."
5. **L29** · "They can probably hear it in Hamburg." · OTHER · This is fine for the German setting and gives some local color. A US kid may not know where Hamburg is, but the joke still lands. Low priority, keep it.
6. **L33** · "The wall vibrated under her hand." · UNCLEAR · Her hand was never put on the wall. They are also in Theo's room, while the knocking was set up as behind *Nora's* headboard. · → "She pressed her hand to the wall. It vibrated."
7. **L35** · "Where else." · TRANSLATED · A calque of *Wo sonst*. It sounds clipped and British-ish. · → "Of course it is."
8. **L63** · "A cold stab went through her chest." · TRANSLATED · A calque of *ein kalter Stich*. · → "Her chest went tight and cold."
9. **L69** · "As if he wanted to say: No. Nothing is okay." · TRANSLATED · A colon before imagined speech again. US style is italics. · → "As if he wanted to say, *No. Nothing is okay.*"
10. **L73** · "Between the boy and the stairwell." · STIFF · Calling Theo "the boy" in Nora's close POV is cold and adult. · → "Between Theo and the stairwell."
11. **L73–75** · "He took one step toward the door. … She opened the apartment door…" · UNCLEAR · Which door, Theo's or the apartment door? The paragraph order also puts Shadow guarding Theo before Nora leaves, which is staged backward. · → Name the door: "toward the front door."
12. **L79** · "Like a wall of ice pressing against her." · OTHER (repetition) · The "like a wall" simile appears for the third time in two chapters (Ch2 L45, Ch2 L69). · → Change the image: "Like opening a freezer."
13. **L109–111** · "The silence pressed on her ears. / She pressed her ear to the cold wood." · RHYTHM · "Pressed" twice back to back. · → "She put her ear to the cold wood."
14. **L113** · "Fast. Don't run. Whatever happens, don't run." · UNCLEAR · Nora's inner command is unmarked, so a 10-year-old may read "Don't run" as narration contradicting "Fast." · → Italicize: *Don't run. Whatever happens, don't run.*
15. **L113** · "Every step straight." · STIFF · Meaning unclear, and it sounds translated. · → "Every step steady."
16. **L115** · "Her knees said something else. Her knees almost gave out." · RHYTHM · The second sentence explains the joke of the first. · → Cut the second sentence, or merge: "Her knees had other ideas."
17. **L129** · "Nobody else lives up here." · UNCLEAR · They are downstairs and talking about the third floor. · → "Nobody lives up there."
18. **L133** · "That slams a heavy wooden door." · OTHER · Nobody heard a slam. The door is simply closed, so Theo is overstating a detail the reader knows is wrong. · → "That closes a heavy wooden door."
19. **L139** · "That's the third time you've said that." · OTHER · Only two "There's an explanation" lines appear in this chapter (L41, L137). If the third is in Ch. 1, fine. Otherwise kids will count and catch it.
20. **L169** · "Sunlight came through the kitchen window. Nora stood in the kitchen…" · RHYTHM · "Kitchen" twice in two short sentences. · → "Sunlight came through the window."
21. **L175** · "Theo stirred his hot chocolate without much interest." · STIFF · "Without much interest" is adult narrator language. · → "Theo stirred his hot chocolate. He didn't drink any."
22. **L181** · "I stared at the wall all night." · OTHER · This contradicts L171 ("slept for three hours"). It can pass as kid exaggeration, but the narrator just stated a fact.
23. **L195** · "Lines. Strokes." · TRANSLATED · "Strokes" is a calque of *Striche*, and to US kids it can mean a medical stroke. · → "Lines. Smudges."
24. **L201** · "Nora's cereal bowl crashed to the floor." · OTHER · We never saw her carrying the bowl to the window (L189), so the drop lacks a setup. · → Add "Still holding her bowl," at L189, or write "The bowl slipped from her hands."

## End-of-chapter assessment

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Leaning no. This is the most natural of the three chapters. The Theo dialog is excellent and idiomatic: "practicing being a blanket," "You can turn off a movie," "he's babysitting me," "ghosts keep business hours." Converting to "sixty-five pounds" helps. The remaining tells are isolated calques ("tips of his hair," "cold stab," "Where else," "to a beat," "Strokes") and the colon-before-thought habit. A kid would not notice. An editor would flag maybe 5 spots.

**(2) Voice:** Nora (12): 3/5. Her brave-but-shaking interior is good ("she would never admit that her hands were shaking"), but her dialog is mostly the repeated "There's an explanation." She needs one sharp, specific rational line. Theo (10): 5/5. This is the best Theo in the batch: funny, scared, and the humor lands right after each scare, as the series wants.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Yes, very. "HELP" alone on a line, then the bowl crashes. It is clean, visual, and makes you turn the page. Only the bowl setup (L189) needs a fix.
