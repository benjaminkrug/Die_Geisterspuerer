# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 5: Mrs. Silber's Apartment

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). Findings only; no rewrites.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L5 · "His tail lay still on the floor." · STIFF · Reads like a report rather than something a reader sees, and it sits inside a long chain of fragments ("Upright. Motionless. His eyes on Nora."). · "His tail didn't move."
2. L7 · "Behind her, the kitchen window." · UNCLEAR · Nora is in the hallway, so a 10-year-old can't picture how the kitchen window is "behind her". It needs a quick spatial anchor. · "Behind her, through the kitchen door, was the window."
3. L17 · "The ticking of the radiator." · OTHER · Chapter 7 says it's summer, and a ticking radiator suggests the heat is on. A US kid links that sound with winter. · Use a fridge hum or a clock instead.
4. L25 · "Everything in her said: Don't go up there." · TRANSLATED · Mirrors German "Alles in ihr sagte". English would say "every part of her" or put the voice in her head. · "Every part of her screamed: Don't go up there."
5. L27 · "But the word was behind her on the window." · TRANSLATED · The German adverb order ("hinter ihr am Fenster") leaves the line limp. · "But the word was still there on the window behind her."
6. L39 · "Strange and wrong." · STIFF · Abstract two-adjective fragment that comes after strong concrete images. It weakens them.
7. L41 · "No steaming breath." · TRANSLATED · "Dampfender Atem". In American English breath fogs or puffs white, it doesn't steam. · "Her breath didn't fog."
8. L57 · "Theo swallowed loudly." · TRANSLATED · "Schluckte laut". The English idiom is a single verb. · "Theo gulped."
9. L63 · "Her footsteps creaked on the dusty floor." · STIFF · Footsteps don't creak. Floorboards do. · "The floorboards creaked under her feet."
10. L67 · "Nora could breathe more easily." · STIFF · Sounds formal, almost medical. · "Breathing was easier."
11. L69 · "They had been running, after all." · TRANSLATED · A tacked-on "after all" (schließlich) is a classic translation fingerprint. · "They'd been too busy running away."
12. L73 · "Specks of dust danced in the strip of light." · RHYTHM · Repeats "strip of light" from the sentence before. · "...danced in the beam."
13. L75 · "On the wall across from the window: books." · TRANSLATED · Colon-plus-noun fragment is German stage-direction style. · "Books were stacked against the far wall."
14. L87 · "Old books about the city: history books, local archives." · STIFF · An archive isn't a kind of book, and the list sounds like a catalog entry.
15. L79 / L87 · "Nora stepped closer." · RHYTHM · Same sentence twice within four paragraphs.
16. L111 · "Something was hanging on the wall next to the bookshelf." · UNCLEAR · There is no bookshelf. The books were described as stacks on the floor (L75).
17. L111 · "Pinned up with thumbtacks, in rows one above the other." · TRANSLATED · "Untereinander". English would say "in neat rows" or "in columns".
18. L133 · "Nora let her eyes move along the rows." · TRANSLATED · "Ließ ihren Blick wandern". · "Nora scanned the rows."
19. L133 · "...the more something came together that she didn't want to say out loud." · STIFF · The relative clause comes too late and is hard to parse. Also abstract. · "...the more a thought crept in that she didn't want to say out loud."
20. L139 · "Not address books. Not phone lists." · TRANSLATED · "Phone lists" isn't a thing for a US kid, and nobody suggested address books, so the denial comes out of nowhere.
21. L155 · "'M. Silber, 2019.'" · OTHER · A black-and-white, yellowed photo dated 2019 will puzzle readers. It reads as very old, not five or six years ago.
22. L175 · "Not a coincidence outside our door." · TRANSLATED · "Kein Zufall vor unserer Tür." The compressed fragment doesn't parse in English. · "It wasn't luck that he showed up at our door."
23. L187 · "Why would somebody hang a list of dead people on the wall?" · RHYTHM · Nearly word for word what Theo asked at L141. It reads like an echo, not progress.
24. L189 · "Or a genealogist." · TOO ADULT · A 10-year-old who supposedly doesn't know "nostalgic" wouldn't say "genealogist". · "Or one of those family-tree people."
25. L199 · "Theo looked at her and grinned a lopsided grin." · STIFF · The "grinned a grin" construction is clunky. · "...gave her a lopsided grin."
26. L201 · "With a jerk." · TRANSLATED · "Mit einem Ruck." · "Suddenly Shadow's head jerked up."
27. L203 · "Her skin tightened." · STIFF · Unclear physical image. · "Goose bumps rose on her arms."
28. L203 · "Shadow was the early warning system." · TOO ADULT · Adult tech or military jargon. It also explains a tension beat when the moment should just be felt.
29. L223 · "His face was white." · RHYTHM · L207 already said "His face was pale". Two pale-face beats so close together.
30. L225 · "Shadow whined. Softly. Pitifully." · STIFF · "Pitifully" is a weak, slightly old-fashioned adverb. · "Shadow let out a thin whine."
31. L7 / L47 / L97 / L173 · "Her heart beat faster." / "Nora's heart skipped a beat." etc. · RHYTHM · There are four or more heart beats in one chapter. The physical-emotion cues need more variety.

## Chapter verdict

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes. The fingerprints are the run-on chains of noun fragments ("Upright. Motionless. His eyes on Nora."), German idioms carried over ("Everything in her said", "With a jerk", "let her eyes move", "after all"), and colon constructions. The dialogue mostly passes, but the narration doesn't.

**(2) Voice (1–5):** Nora 3: clear, but flat. Her lines are mostly "I know" / "Yeah", and her thinking voice is generic. Theo 4: "I want it on the record. We ran." and the "nostalgic" exchange are good, but "genealogist" breaks his voice.

**(3) Ending strong?** Yes. Running bare feet that stop, then the "Someone who wasn't there" stack, is a strong, kid-friendly chill. The four one-line beats are slightly too many. Three would hit harder.
