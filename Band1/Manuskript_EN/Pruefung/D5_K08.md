# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 8: Behind the Wallpaper

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). Findings only; no rewrites.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L7–11 · "'What's wrong with him?' ... 'Why is he scratching the wall?'" · OTHER · This restarts the scene from scratch. Ch. 7 ended with Nora already saying "There's something behind the wall", so Theo's questions sound like he missed the last page.
2. L13 · "As if someone had only stuck it on loosely." · TRANSLATED · "Nur lose aufgeklebt." · "As if someone had barely glued it on."
3. L15 · "No growling. No raised hackles." · RHYTHM · The "No X. No Y." negation pattern runs across all four chapters. It's become a tic.
4. L25 · "The wallpaper came off like skin peeling." · OTHER · A body-horror image that brushes against the "no body horror" line, and it's stronger than the moment needs. · "...came off like an old sticker."
5. L27 · "It went from top to bottom, about a foot long." · UNCLEAR · "Top to bottom" suggests floor to ceiling, which contradicts "a foot long". · "It ran straight down, about a foot long."
6. L43 · "Shadow had sat down. He sat upright next to the spot..." · STIFF · He was already sitting (L15), and "sat ... sat" is repetitive.
7. L59–61 · "'Didn't you say there were tools in one of the moving boxes?' ... 'That's right. Dad's old toolbox.'" · UNCLEAR · Nora is asking Theo about his own remark, then he reacts as if it were news ("eyes went wide"). The logic is reversed.
8. L65 · "...walk through the dark apartment alone right now..." · UNCLEAR · It was afternoon in Ch. 7. Why is it dark? It needs a time marker (dusk, evening).
9. L111 · "Nora went to the door and pushed down the handle." · TRANSLATED · A German lever handle (fine for the setting), but the phrase reads translated to US kids, who think "doorknob". · "...grabbed the handle."
10. L123 · "In the light from the screen, she saw something rectangular." · UNCLEAR · She was using the phone's flashlight (L119), not the screen glow.
11. L133 · "And in Nora's hand lay a book." · TRANSLATED · The inverted German word order ("In Noras Hand lag ein Buch"). · "And in Nora's hand was a book."
12. L135 · "Nora's fingers closed tighter around it." · RHYTHM · Right after "She closed her fingers around it" (L131).
13. L137 · "...like a tuning fork behind her temples." · TOO ADULT · Many 10-year-olds won't know what a tuning fork sounds like. · "...like a buzzing phone inside her head."
14. L137–139 · (paragraph order) · RHYTHM · The flicker/humming paragraph cuts in, then "Small. Barely bigger than her palm." goes back to describing the book. The reader loses track of what "small" refers to.
15. L5 / L99 / L167 / L75 · "dull glow" / "dull clunk" / "dull glow" / "dull thud" · RHYTHM · "Dull" four times in one chapter.
16. L173 · "Nora's fingers were stiff with tension as she turned the first page." · STIFF · "With tension" is an abstract label. · "Nora's fingers felt clumsy as she..."
17. L179 · "And in that moment, it turned cold." · TRANSLATED · "In diesem Moment wurde es kalt." · "And the room went cold."
18. L181 · "Not slowly. Not creeping in." · STIFF · "Not creeping in" is a dangling participle fragment. · "Not slowly, not bit by bit."

## Chapter verdict

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, but only lightly. The toolbox banter ("a very aggressive spoon", "I'm telling Mom you started it") and the door-slam scare read native. Word-order tells ("in Nora's hand lay a book", "it turned cold") and the scene restart at the top show the seams.

**(2) Voice (1–5):** Nora 3: decisive and competent ("We open it."), but she has no personality quirks in her speech. Theo 4: funny and scared in the right mix ("GPS for creepy stuff", "Ten very long, very dark feet").

**(3) Ending strong?** Good, not great. The sudden freezer cold and the flickering light make a solid cliffhanger. The last line ("That was all he could get out") is a soft landing. Ending on a concrete threat, such as something seen or heard, would hit harder.
