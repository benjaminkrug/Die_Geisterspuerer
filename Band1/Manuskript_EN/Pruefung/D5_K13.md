# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 13: The Release

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12). Mark and explain only; no rewrites.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

- **L7** · "At some point in the night, Nora had given it to him." · TRANSLATED · "At some point in the night" is a word-for-word time phrase; American narration would say "sometime" or "during the night." · Suggest: "Sometime during the night, Nora had handed it to him."
- **L9** · "Lina had lived down here, in Nora's room." · UNCLEAR · A 10-year-old will read this literally and wonder how Lina lived in Nora's bedroom. The point is that she lived in the apartment where Nora's room is now. · Suggest: "in the apartment where Nora's room was now."
- **L11** · "Her heart was beating calmly. For the first time in days, it was beating calmly." · STIFF · The repetition is meant to stress the point, but "beating calmly" is an odd collocation in English and twice makes it clunkier. · Suggest: "Her heart was calm. For the first time in days."
- **L13** · "His tail swung slowly back and forth." · STIFF · Dogs' tails "wag," they don't "swing." · Suggest: "His tail wagged slowly."
- **L17** · "At the first page with the name in a girl's handwriting." · TRANSLATED · The prepositional chain ("page with the name in a … handwriting") is German syntax. Whose name is also left vague. · Suggest: "At the first page, where Lina had written her name in round, girlish letters."
- **L35** · "Not like usual. Not like the first time." · STIFF · "Not like usual" is not idiomatic. Americans say "Not like before" or "Not like always." · Suggest: "Not like before."
- **L55** · "Like a breath someone was holding back." · UNCLEAR · Cool air compared to a held breath doesn't produce a clear picture for a kid. Is it tense? Waiting? · Suggest: "Like the room was holding its breath."
- **L55** · "She stood in the dark and felt the presence. Close. Right in front of her. Everywhere." · TOO ADULT · "The presence" is grown-up ghost-show vocabulary. "Right in front of her. Everywhere." also contradicts itself. That may be intended, but it reads like a slip. · Suggest: "She could feel someone there."
- **L61** · "Without hesitating. Without growling. He trotted into the room as if he had done it a thousand times." · OTHER (repetition) · This nearly repeats L31 ("No hesitating. No growling. He trotted through the doorway like it was the most natural thing in the world."). Thirty lines apart, the echo feels accidental. · Change one of the two.
- **L69** · "Something pressed behind her breastbone." · TOO ADULT · "Breastbone" sounds like an anatomy textbook. Kids say "chest." · Suggest: "Something pressed inside her chest."
- **L89** · "His voice broke." · OTHER · For boys this age "voice broke" also means puberty, which can draw giggles at the most tender moment. · Suggest: "His voice cracked." (or "wobbled")
- **L105** · "He looked like a dog who had finally come home after a long time." · TRANSLATED · "After a long time" is a word-for-word time phrase and ends the image weakly. · Suggest: "…come home after a long trip."
- **L109** · "Something was coming loose." · TRANSLATED · This sounds like a literal rendering of a German verb for "letting go/detaching." In English it makes you think of a screw or a button. · Suggest: "Something was letting go."
- **L119** · "The light was shining now. Not blinding." · STIFF · "Was shining now" is flat, and the beat repeats things the reader already knows. · Suggest: "The light glowed brighter. Not blinding."
- **L121–123** · "Then it got smaller." / "The light's getting smaller." · OTHER (redundancy) · Theo repeats exactly what the narration just said. It reads as an echo, not a reaction. · Cut one, or give Theo a different line ("It's shrinking.").
- **L135** · "No raised hackles. No tense body." · STIFF · "No tense body" isn't natural English. · Suggest: "No stiff legs."
- **L141** · "In case the teacher asks on Monday what I did this weekend." · TRANSLATED · An American kid says "my teacher." "The teacher" is a German-style article. · Suggest: "my teacher."
- **L147** · "I think exactly like this." · TRANSLATED · This is ungrammatical as an answer. An English speaker would say "Just like this." · Suggest: "Yeah. Just like this."
- **L161** · "In the room that had been ice-cold for months and was now as warm as any other room in the house." · RHYTHM · At 22 words, this fragment is far over the house limit and drags at a quiet moment. · Split into two.
- **L165** · "Someone had listened to her. And she had gone." · UNCLEAR · "Gone" is the book's euphemism, but "she had gone" sounds as if she walked off. · Suggest: "And now she was free." or "And now she was gone."
- **L167** · "What actually happened down there? In the basement?" · TRANSLATED · "Actually" in this spot is a classic German "eigentlich" tell. In English it sounds slightly accusatory. · Suggest: "What happened to her? In the basement?"
- **L169** · "Nora saw the scratch marks again." · UNCLEAR · Nora is on the third floor, so a kid may think the marks are physically there. · Suggest: "Nora pictured the scratch marks again."
- **L171** · "Just now, when the light was here, all I felt was what it had felt like." · UNCLEAR · "Felt … what it had felt like" is a tongue-twister, and "it" has no clear referent. · Suggest: "…all I felt was how it felt for her. Cold. And all alone."
- **L179** · "Believe me." · UNCLEAR · Without italics or attribution, a kid will hear Nora telling Theo "believe me," not quoting Lina. · Suggest: italicize it, or write "'Believe me.' That's what she wanted."
- **L203** · "Three, maybe four streets away." · BRITISH · American kids measure distance in "blocks." "Streets away" sounds British. The German setting gives a little leeway. · Suggest: "Three, maybe four blocks away."
- **L205** · "Like lightning striking underground." · UNCLEAR · Lightning underground can't be pictured, because you couldn't see it. · Suggest: "Like a flash of lightning, but blue and close to the ground."
- **L223** · "She thought of Mrs. Silber's list of names. Of the names." · STIFF · "List of names. Of the names." is redundant and stalls the build-up just before the final beat. · Cut "Of the names."

## End-of-chapter assessment

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **Yes**, though only mildly. The emotional core works, but a cluster of small tells gives it away: "actually" in a question (L167), "I think exactly like this" (L147), "the teacher" (L141), "coming loose" (L109) and "after a long time" (L105). One or two of these would pass. Six of them in the chapter's tenderest stretch make a careful reader notice.
2. **Voice:** Nora 4/5. She is quiet and observant, and her grief is shown physically, which is good, though her dialogue at L171 is muddled. Theo 4/5. The snoring joke and the teacher joke are pitch-perfect, and "That must have been lonely" is lovely. He loses a point only because L123 just echoes the narration.
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes.** The blue light (cold, the opposite of Lina's gold) set against Shadow's low growl is a clean hook. Shadow growling at the very end is a great button. Fixing L205 would sharpen the image.
4. **Counts:** TRANSLATED 7 · STIFF 6 · BRITISH 1 · TOO ADULT 2 · UNCLEAR 7 · RHYTHM 1 · OTHER 3 · **Total 27**
