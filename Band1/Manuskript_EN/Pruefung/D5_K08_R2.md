# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 8 "Behind the Wallpaper"

Reviewer: D5 (US middle-grade line editor, spooky-funny list)
Scope: Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_08.md only. German names and places, "Mrs." with German surnames, and short fragments at scary beats are house style and are not flagged.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L5 / L65 · "The ceiling light cast a dim glow..." / "...walk through the dark apartment alone right now" · OTHER · Continuity: Chapter 7 is explicitly a summer afternoon (Mom going to bed early), and now the apartment is dark with the ceiling light on. Either time has passed, which the text doesn't say, or the darkness is a mistake. · Add a time marker ("By the time ... it was getting dark") or say "the gloomy hallway."

2. L13 · "As if someone had barely glued it on." · STIFF · "Barely glued" is unclear phrasing. · "As if someone had used hardly any glue."

3. L25 · "The wallpaper came off like skin peeling." · OTHER · A skin-peeling image edges toward the body horror the series avoids, and it is a bit gross for the tone. · "...came off like a sticker."

4. L27 · "And a crack ran right through ... It ran straight down, about a foot long." · RHYTHM · "Ran / ran" in neighboring sentences. · "It went straight down..."

5. L39 · "Somebody sealed it with mortar." · TOO ADULT · Many 10-year-olds won't know "mortar," and it recurs through the scene without a gloss. · "...filled it in with cement" once, then "mortar."

6. L43 · "Shadow had sat down. He sat upright next to the spot..." · OTHER · He was already sitting at L15, and "had sat down. He sat" is redundant within the line too. · "Shadow sat upright next to the spot..."

7. L51 · "There was an empty space under the surface. Small, but there." · TRANSLATED · "Klein, aber da" rendered literally. "Small, but there" is not an English idiom. · "Something was hollow back there."

8. L57 · "They needed tools." · STIFF · A flat narrator summary that interrupts snappy dialog. · Cut, and let Nora's next line carry it.

9. L61 · "That's right." · STIFF · An adult confirmation phrase, where a 10-year-old would say "Oh yeah!" · "Oh yeah!"

10. L111 · "The door opened." · STIFF · Bland at a tense beat, and it sounds as if the door opened on its own. · "The door swung open easily."

11. L113 · "Cold air was streaming out of the hole in the wall." · UNCLEAR · Nora is at the hallway door, and with no transition we are suddenly at the hole feeling the draft. A young reader loses track of where she is. · "She went back to the wall. Cold air was streaming..."

12. L115 · "There's an empty space back there," · RHYTHM · Already established at L51 ("It sounded hollow. There was an empty space"). · "It goes back pretty far," or cut.

13. L121/L125 · "And there was something inside." / "There's something in there," she said. · RHYTHM · The narration states it and then the dialog repeats it four lines later. · Keep only the dialog.

14. L133 · "And in Nora's hand was a book." · TRANSLATED · The inverted subject is German word order. It is tolerable as a dramatic device, but noticeable. · "Nora was holding a book."

15. L155 · "It's hers. It belongs to Lina." · RHYTHM · Saying the same thing twice. · "It's Lina's."

16. L165 vs. L169 · "Or somebody put it there so it would be found." / "As if nobody was ever supposed to find it." · UNCLEAR · Nora says one thing aloud and then the narration, in her POV, says the opposite. A 10-year-old won't know which to believe. · Make the contradiction explicit ("But then why brick it up?"), or drop one of the two.

17. L137 / L183 · "...the shadows on the wall twitched..." / "Shadows leaped across the walls..." · UNCLEAR · The dog is named Shadow. A sentence starting with capital-S "Shadows", immediately followed by "Shadow whined", is genuinely confusing at the climax. · Use "dark shapes" or "the darkness" for the visual effect in this chapter.

18. L181 · "Her breath came out of her mouth in a white cloud." · STIFF · "Came out of her mouth" is redundant and wordy. · "Her breath puffed out white."

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would I guess this was translated?** No. The dialog is lively and idiomatic ("a very aggressive spoon," "Now our dog is a GPS for creepy stuff," "I'm telling Mom you started it"). The tells are a few literal phrases ("Small, but there," an inverted "And in Nora's hand was a book") and some narration stiffness. These read more like first-draft roughness than translation.

2. **Voice:** Nora 3/5. She is practical and brave, which fits, but she mostly narrates actions, and her one interpretive line contradicts the narration (L165/169). Theo 5/5. He is the best he has been: scared and joking at once, and the "my hands are shaking too much" line after the big grin is a lovely contradiction.

3. **Ending:** Strong. The sudden freeze, the flickering light, the trembling dog and Theo choking out only "Nora" make a good physical cliffhanger. It would be stronger if the dog/shadows ambiguity were fixed, so the final beats are clear.
