# D5 Line Edit – Chapter 11: Signs on the Wall

Reviewer stance: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). German setting, "Mrs." + German surnames and short fragments at scary beats accepted as house style.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L7** · "She yanked open the basement door and stumbled into the hallway." · OTHER · Continuity: in Ch. 10 the heavy basement door is at the *bottom* of the stairs. Here it's at the top. Readers who are paying attention will notice. · Make it clear there are two doors, or say *the door at the top of the stairs*.
2. **L11** · "The basement door fell shut behind them." · TRANSLATED · "Fell shut" is *fiel ins Schloss*. US: "slammed shut" or "banged shut". · *The basement door slammed behind them.*
3. **L35** · "Alone. Like for the last fifty years." · TRANSLATED · "Like for the last …" follows German *wie die letzten fünfzig Jahre*. A US kid would put a verb in it. · *Like she's been for fifty years.*
4. **L47** · "Just now in the basement, you were clinging to my sleeve." · BRITISH · "Just now" at the start of a sentence, meaning "a moment ago", sounds British or translated. · *Five minutes ago you were hanging on to my sleeve.*
5. **L49** · "Straight on. Without looking away." · RHYTHM · Both fragments repeat "looked her in the eye". · Cut both.
6. **L51** · "Only Shadow's breathing. Slow and even." · TRANSLATED · The same anchorless "Only…" silence beat as in Ch. 10 (*Nur noch …*). It's becoming a tic. · *Silence. Just Shadow breathing.*
7. **L53** · "She had no argument. Not against the truth." · TOO ADULT · Sounds like a sermon, with an adult narrator stepping in. · *She had nothing to say to that.*
8. **L55** · "Shadow trotted past her. Slow, calm. He lay down at Nora's feet." · UNCLEAR · He trots *past* her but lies down *at her feet*, and "trotted" doesn't fit "slow". · *Shadow padded over and lay down at Nora's feet.*
9. **L59** · "Not panic. Something else." · OTHER · Repeated tic: "Not X. Something else." also appears at L133 and twice in Ch. 10. · Vary it, or give the actual physical sensation.
10. **L87** · "Anything that explains whether Mrs. Silber helped the ghosts, and how." · STIFF · An expository sentence that no 12-year-old would say aloud. · *Anything about how she helped them.*
11. **L97** · "She had a direct line." · UNCLEAR · The joke depends on knowing the idiom "direct line to …", and the "… to the other side" part is missing. · *Guess she had the ghost hotline.*
12. **L105–107** · "A dull thud. Small. Light." / "Something fell out." · RHYTHM · A "dull thud" that is also "light" contradicts itself, and Theo then repeats what the narration just said. · *A soft thump.* Then cut Theo's line or make it a joke.
13. **L121** · "Every page was full. Not one empty spot." · OTHER · Continuity: L169 has "Empty pages" after the last entry. Forty years of notes in a book "barely bigger than a postcard" is also a stretch. · *Page after page was full.*, and make the book thicker or one of several.
14. **L125** · "Between what was and what could have been." · TOO ADULT · An abstract, philosophical phrase that a 10-year-old won't picture. It's fine as Mrs. Silber's adult voice only if Theo or Nora reacts to it. · *Stuck between then and now.*
15. **L133** · "Not from fear. From something else. From understanding." · OTHER · Names an abstract emotion, which goes against the house rule that emotions are physical, and repeats the tic from L59. · *Her heart beat faster. It finally made sense.*
16. **L135** · "Only when someone grasps that does the chain come loose." · TOO ADULT · Inverted syntax, "grasps", and a "chain" metaphor that hasn't been set up. · *Only then does the chain let go.*
17. **L141** · "He was my compass. For forty years." · UNCLEAR · Kids know dogs don't live forty years. If Shadow being uncanny is intentional, it needs a reaction ("Wait, how old *is* he?"). If not, it's a plot hole. · Have Theo react with a joke beat.
18. **L147** · "Every release was written down." · TOO ADULT · "Release" in this sense means nothing to a 10-year-old. · *Every ghost she'd set free was in here.*
19. **L147** · "Three nights." · UNCLEAR · Three nights of what: searching, or how long the haunting took? · *It took three nights.*
20. **L159** · "The Veil is getting thinner." · UNCLEAR · A capitalized concept appears with no explanation. Fine as a series term, but the kids need a beat ("What veil?") so readers don't feel lost. · Add a one-line Theo question.
21. **L173** · "The air in the room grew heavier. Thicker." · RHYTHM · Nearly word-for-word repeat of L161 ("grew thicker. Heavier."). · Change the second one to a new sensation, such as a sound or the candle flame.
22. **L177** · "Not trotting. Fast." · STIFF · An odd contrast; "fast" isn't the opposite of trotting. · *He didn't trot. He hurried.*

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No (narrowly).** The dialogue is strong and idiomatic: "Your eyes were wet. That counts.", "dog growls, lady runs over", "Who would I tell? The ghost?" Mrs. Silber's formal notebook voice reads as a deliberate character choice, not translation. The tells are in the bridging narration ("fell shut", "just now", "Only Shadow's breathing", "Like for the last fifty years"), and there are fewer of them than in Ch. 10.
2. **Voice:** Nora **4/5**. Her attempt to quit is believable, and the "Since right now. Don't tell anyone." exchange is great. She loses half a point to abstract interior lines (L53, L133). Theo **5/5**. His turn from scared to brave is earned and he stays funny while doing it. This is the best Theo chapter in the block.
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes.** The "I am next" line, "He is searching", the click of a door, and the knock pattern returning make a strong, layered cliffhanger with a series-level hook (the First One). One caveat: the next chapter opens by saying the knocking simply stopped, which takes the air out of this hook. That's a Ch. 12 issue.
