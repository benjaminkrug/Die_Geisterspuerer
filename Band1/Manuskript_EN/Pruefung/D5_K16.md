# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 16: Those Who Come After Me

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12). Mark and explain only; no rewrites.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion
Note: I accepted Mrs. Silber's formal letter register as intentional. I only flagged letter lines where the issue is grammar or clarity, not formality.

## Findings

- **L15** · "For forty years, I have looked after the Restless of Gravenstedt." · BRITISH (mild) · "Looked after" leans British. It fits an old lady's formal letter, so this is borderline. "Cared for" or "watched over" is more American and still formal. · Optional: "watched over."
- **L23** · "She thought of the chronicle. *In 1823.*" · UNCLEAR · Putting "In 1823" in italics makes it look like a quote, but the chronicle said "founded in 1823." Kids may not connect "two hundred years ago" with the founding unless it is spelled out. · Suggest: "She thought of the chronicle. *Founded in 1823.* Two hundred years ago."
- **L37** · "It is the same for me to this day, with every one of them." · TRANSLATED · This is not a register problem. The word order is German, and "It is the same for me" is vague in English. A formal English letter would say "I still do, every time." · Suggest: "I still cry every time, for every one of them."
- **L57** · "More of the Restless are coming through than I can handle alone." · UNCLEAR · Coming through *what*? The Veil was just called a border, but a 10-year-old may wonder why trapped dead are "coming through" from somewhere. One word fixes it. · Suggest: "coming through the tears."
- **L87** · "Behind her, the apartment lay in darkness. The radiator ticked." · OTHER (logic) · It is July, so a ticking radiator will make careful readers stop. Nora is also in her room, so "the apartment" behind her is a little off. · Suggest: "The fridge hummed in the kitchen." or "The old house creaked."
- **L89–93** · "Shadow lay next to her… The map lay on her desk. The letter lay next to it. Outside, Gravenstedt lay in the night." · RHYTHM · "Lay" appears four times in five short paragraphs. It works as a lulling device the first time but starts to sound mechanical. · Vary two of them ("The map was on her desk, the letter beside it.").
- **L99** · "Now she sat at the window at night and listened for sounds no machine could record." · UNCLEAR · "No machine could record" comes out of nowhere unless it is a callback. Kids will wonder what machine. · Suggest: "sounds nobody else could hear." (This also echoes the letter's "voices that no one else hears".)
- **L129** · "You know what I was thinking before?" · TRANSLATED · "Before" (vorhin) is a German tell. Americans say "earlier" or "back there." · Suggest: "You know what I was thinking back at the cemetery?"
- **L139** · "Hm?" · OTHER (spelling) · US convention is "Hmm?" · Change to "Hmm?"
- **L153** · "She wanted to roll up the map." · STIFF · "Wanted to" plus an action is a German construction (wollte). English uses "went to" or just the action. · Suggest: "She went to roll up the map."
- **L161** · "Only in one spot. Only on one single circle." · STIFF · "One single" is redundant and reads as a translation of "einzigen." · Suggest: "Only on one circle."
- **L173** · "It felt as if a second hand lay on the other side at that very moment." · UNCLEAR · This is the most important sentence in the book, and it's the weakest. "The other side" of what: the paper, the Veil? "Lay" is passive, and "at that very moment" is stiff filler. The image is great, but it needs to be felt. · Suggest: "It felt as if another hand was pressing back from the other side."

## End-of-chapter assessment

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No.** The letter sounds intentionally old-fashioned, and the kitchen-table and bedside scenes are warm and natural. The "Dust." / "Sure. Dust." exchange and "I'm the only ten-year-old you know" are pure native middle grade. Only "before" (L129), "wanted to roll" (L153) and the closing sentence (L173) give it away.
2. **Voice:** Nora 4/5. She is protective and quietly brave, and her unspoken thoughts in the scene with Mom ("*He found us*") are lovely. Her narration goes a bit flat in the night scene (the "lay" chain). Theo 5/5. The dentist's waiting room line, "Dust," "One of us has to get enough sleep" and especially "That's worse than creepy. That's sad." show the full range from joker to heart.
3. **Ending strong?** **Mostly yes.** The frost creeping over Heinrich's circle on the map is a superb final image: it brings the cemetery ghost into her bedroom. The very last sentence (L173) is muddy, though, and it is what a reader remembers. Sharpen that one line and the ending goes from good to great. "In exactly the same spot." is a strong last beat.
4. **Counts:** TRANSLATED 2 · STIFF 2 · BRITISH 1 · TOO ADULT 0 · UNCLEAR 4 · RHYTHM 1 · OTHER 2 · **Total 12**

## Series ending: does the last page make kids want Book 2?

**Yes, with two caveats.**

What works:
- **Many open hooks, each with a face:** Heinrich (a named, angry ghost who has *seen* them, now reaching into Nora's room), the First One / Graven (the big bad, set up by the red circle and the letter's warning), the thinning Veil, and Mrs. Silber's disappearance ("I am no longer here"). A kid closing the book knows exactly who is coming next.
- **The emotional promise is clear:** "Eleven who were still waiting" turns the series into a mission with a countdown. Kids love a checklist.
- **The final image is physical and intimate:** a cold hand meeting hers through paper. It is scary but not traumatic, which suits the age group.
- **The quiet scene with Mom and Theo before the scare** gives the closing chill room to land.

Caveats:
1. **The fresh-ink X from Ch. 14 is never mentioned again.** That was the best mystery beat in the last act, and Ch. 15–16 drop it completely. Kids *will* notice ("Who drew the X?!"). One line in Ch. 16, such as Nora glancing at the wet X on the map before the frost spreads or Theo asking "And who crossed out Lina?", would turn a plot hole into a deliberate Book-2 hook. The obvious candidate is Mrs. Silber, or Shadow's calm recognition.
2. **The last sentence needs to be crisp** (see L173). Middle-grade readers remember the final line. Right now the strongest line in that passage is the second-to-last one.

Optional: a one-line teaser after "END OF BOOK 1" (e.g., "Heinrich is waiting. The Ghost Trackers return in Book 2.") is standard in US spooky series and helps drive pre-orders.
