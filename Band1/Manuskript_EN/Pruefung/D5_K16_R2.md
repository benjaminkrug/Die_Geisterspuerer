# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 16 "Those Who Come After Me" (R2)

Reviewer: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Scope: Kapitel_16.md only. Mrs. Silber's letter is formal on purpose, so formality alone is not flagged there. Only sentences that sound *translated* rather than *old-fashioned* are flagged. Fragments at scary beats are treated as house style.

## Findings

1. **L11** · "Inside were several sheets of paper, covered in close, tight writing." · BRITISH · "Close writing" is British and literary usage. Many US 10-year-olds will read "close" as "near." · *Suggestion:* "covered in tiny, cramped handwriting."
2. **L23** · "She thought of the chronicle." · UNCLEAR · "Chronicle" is not an everyday word for a 10-year-old, and on its own here it could be anything. · *Suggestion:* "the old town history book."
3. **L37** · "‘To this day, it is the same for me with every one of them.’" · TRANSLATED · Even allowing for the formal letter, this reads as a word-for-word German construction (*geht es mir bei jedem so*), not as old-fashioned English. · *Suggestion:* "‘It is still that way for me, every single time.’"
4. **L41** · "‘He senses them before I even suspect it.’" · STIFF · Suspect *what*? The "it" has nothing clear to point to. · *Suggestion:* "‘He senses them long before I do.’"
5. **L43** · "His eyes rested on Nora." · TOO ADULT · "Eyes rested on" is adult literary phrasing. · *Suggestion:* "He looked straight at Nora."
6. **L57** · "‘The Veil is getting thinner… more… than I can handle alone.’" · OTHER · This is a register slip. The rest of the letter is formal ("I do not know," "Please do not give up"), but "getting thinner" and "handle" are casual. · *Suggestion:* "‘is growing thinner… than I can manage alone.’"
7. **L75** · "“In the notebook, she wrote about him,” Nora said slowly." · TRANSLATED · Putting the place first is German word order (*Im Notizbuch hat sie über ihn geschrieben*). Nora's contrast is also slightly muddy. · *Suggestion:* "“In her notebook she was just writing *about* him.”"
8. **L87** · "The radiator ticked. Familiar sounds." · OTHER · Two problems. A radiator ticking "in the middle of July" (L165) is a logic snag, since the heat is off. And "sounds" is plural after only one sound. · *Suggestion:* "The fridge hummed in the kitchen. The old floor creaked."
9. **L89** · "Two bright points in the half-dark." · TRANSLATED · "Half-dark" is a calque of *Halbdunkel*. · *Suggestion:* "in the dark" / "in the dim room."
10. **L93** · "Outside, Gravenstedt lay in the night." · TRANSLATED · "The city lay in the night" is a German construction. · *Suggestion:* "Outside, Gravenstedt was asleep."
11. **L93–95** · "Rooftops and chimneys… The church tower… A completely normal city." · OTHER · This almost exactly repeats the Chapter 13 window passage (rooftops, chimneys, church tower, "completely normal city"). If it's a deliberate bookend, sharpen the difference. If not, it reads as recycled. · *Suggestion:* change one or two images.
12. **L97** · "Eleven who were still waiting." · UNCLEAR · Eleven *what*? The noun is missing, and a kid has to work backward to "circles = ghosts." · *Suggestion:* "Eleven of them. Still waiting."
13. **L95–97** · "normal city… normal roofs" · RHYTHM · "Normal" appears twice in three lines, after "completely normal" two lines up. · *Suggestion:* "Under ordinary roofs."
14. **L99** · "…listened for sounds no machine could record." · UNCLEAR · This only works if a recorder or device appeared earlier. Read on its own, it's an odd, techy aside. · *Suggestion:* "listened for sounds nobody else could hear." (Note that L57 has "voices that no one else hears", so it would make a nice echo.)
15. **L109** · "*If you only knew*, Nora thought." · STIFF · The idiom is "If only you knew." · *Suggestion:* "*If only you knew.*"
16. **L121 / L149** · "A little later, Theo padded in." / "A little later, a murmur came…" · RHYTHM · The same transition appears twice in one short scene. · *Suggestion:* "Then Theo padded in." / "Soon a murmur came…"
17. **L153** · "She got up and went to the desk. She went to roll up the map." · BRITISH · "Went to [do X]," meaning "was about to," is British usage, and it repeats "went." US kids may read it as walking somewhere again. · *Suggestion:* "She reached to roll up the map."
18. **L173** · "It felt as if, right then, on the other side, there was another hand." · RHYTHM · Three commas break the next-to-last line into pieces just when it needs to flow. "The other side" of what — the paper, the Veil? That's fine as ambiguity, but the syntax makes it read as hesitation instead of a chill. · *Suggestion:* "It felt as if another hand was pressing back from the other side."

## Summary

**(1) Would you guess this was translated?** **No.** The letter's formality reads as the voice of an old woman, not as translation, so it works. The sibling scenes ("I'm not crying. Dust." / "Sure. Dust.", "I'm the only ten-year-old you know." / "Still.") sound natively American. A professional would still catch a few tells: "half-dark," "lay in the night," "In the notebook, she wrote about him," "it is the same for me with every one of them" and the British "went to roll up."

**(2) Voice:**
- Nora (12): **4/5.** Her protective older-sister role comes through ("No. She hoped so." and "If you cry now, I'll cry too"). She mostly reads aloud and reacts, which is right for this chapter.
- Theo (10): **5/5.** This is his best chapter: "dentist's waiting room," the dust gag, "One of us has to get enough sleep," the sleep-talking flashlight line, and the genuinely empathetic "That's worse than creepy. That's sad."

**(3) Is the chapter ending strong?** **Yes.** The frost spreading over one circle, "In the middle of July. In the middle of her room.," and Shadow whining instead of growling make a great tonal turn. It's creepy and tender at once, and it sets up the 1887 ghost for Book 2.

**Do the book's last two lines land?** **Mostly. The idea lands, but the wording only half does.** "In exactly the same spot." is a good, quiet final line. It works because the image (a hand meeting hers through the paper) is chilling and also sad, which suits the series. But the line before it ("It felt as if, right then, on the other side, there was another hand.") is comma-chopped and weakens the build-up, so the last line arrives with less force than it should. Smoothing L173 into one flowing sentence would make the final fragment hit hard. "Exactly the same spot" is also a little flat. "Right where hers was." would add a touch more intimacy.
