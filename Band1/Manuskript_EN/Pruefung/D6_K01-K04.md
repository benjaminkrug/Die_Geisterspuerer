## Part 2: Read-aloud (parent reading to a kid)

### Biggest issue: "Shadow" and "shadow" sound the same

Out loud there is no capital letter. Every lowercase "shadow" sounds like the dog:
- Ch. 2, 173: "like a shadow moving without any light to cast it"
- Ch. 3, 83: "The shadows behind it were black and deep."
- Ch. 4, 53: "scared of his own shadow" (this one gets a laugh at the wrong moment)
- Ch. 4, 105: "A movement. A shadow."

Suggestion: use "shape", "darkness" or "dark shape" in these places.

### Knock. Knock. Knock.

This reads aloud very well. A parent can knock on the bed frame. The risk is that the listening kid answers "Who's there?" at the end of ch. 2 or the start of ch. 3. The "Pause." line helps keep it creepy.

Option: let Theo take the joke on purpose once, so it's defused inside the story. For example: "If it says 'who's there,' I'm moving out."

### Sentences hard to read aloud

- Ch. 1, 57: The long wallpaper paragraph. Nine short pieces in a row; the reader loses where the "five dents" payoff is.
- Ch. 1, 187: "You could count every rib under the dull fur, tangled on his sides, but his eyes were clear." The comma clause "tangled on his sides" is hard to phrase. It sounds like the ribs are tangled.
- Ch. 1, 231–233: "A sound that skipped Nora's ears. / And landed in her stomach." This works aloud with a pause, but the kid may ask "what?"
- Ch. 2, 45: "From one step to the next, the temperature dropped like a stone. Twenty degrees. Maybe more." "From one step to the next" reads like stairs.
- Ch. 2, 199: "The knocking came from the wall. Right behind her headboard. Directly above her." Spatially contradictory when heard.
- Ch. 4, 13: The note with "PS:" needs a voice change, but it's readable. Read it as "P.S."
- Ch. 4, 147: "The window had gone blind." The reader has to stop and explain.

### Rhythm stumbles

- **Fragment runs.** Fragments are fine for scares. But when the scene is calm they make the reading feel choppy:
  - Ch. 1, 5
  - Ch. 4, 129–133: "Number 14. Gray walls. Narrow windows... Stairwell. Floor wax. The familiar creak of the stairs."
- **Repeated beats a parent will notice aloud:**
  - "In July." / "In the middle of summer." (ch. 2, 11, 47, 59, 65; ch. 3, 79; ch. 4, 13, 193)
  - "Her throat was dry" / "mouth went dry" (ch. 1, 167; ch. 2, 27; ch. 4, 119, 181)
  - The knees (see above)
  - "Order helped. Order always helped." (ch. 1, 121), echoed again in ch. 4, 81. That echo works.
- **Recap openings.** The openings of ch. 3 (5) and ch. 4 (3–9) repeat the previous cliffhanger nearly word for word. Reading in one sitting, this sounds like a skip in the record.
- **Good for reading aloud:**
  - Theo's ALL-CAPS outbursts (ch. 4, 23–31)
  - The one-word lines "Cold." (ch. 2, 43; ch. 3, 77)
  - "HELP" / "HERE" on their own lines
  - The "Did he just—" / "Yeah." / "Just like that?" / "Yeah." exchange (ch. 1, 197–203)
