# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 3 "Three Times"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Only Kapitel_02–04.md were read.

Format: line · sentence · category · why · suggestion

## Findings

1. **L9 vs. L37** · "Pipes didn't keep a beat." · OTHER · In narration, Nora rules out pipes. Two pages later she argues it's the pipes. That can work as denial, but as written it reads like a contradiction, not a choice. · Add a beat after L37 (*She didn't even believe it herself.*) or move the L9 line to after Theo's "most organized pipes" joke.

2. **L9 / L11 / L15** · "Nora counted…" / "Nora pushed back…" / "Nora crept…" · RHYTHM · Three paragraphs in a row open with "Nora." · Vary one: *She pushed back the covers…*

3. **L15** · "She pushed the handle down and eased the door open." · TRANSLATED · Lever handles are European. A US kid reads "turned the knob." The setting allows it, but the phrasing stands out. · *She eased Theo's door open.*

4. **L33** · "The wall vibrated under her hand." · UNCLEAR · Her hand was never put on the wall. She sat on the bed. · *She pressed her palm to the wall. It vibrated.*

5. **L37** · "Pipes swell and shrink." · STIFF · A textbook phrase. · *"Old pipes bang. Everybody knows that."*

6. **L43** · "She pushed it away. Right away. But it had been there." · RHYTHM · The "away / away" echo sounds like an accident, not a choice. · *She shoved the thought aside. Fast. But it had been there.*

7. **L69** · "As if he wanted to tell her no, nothing was okay at all." · TRANSLATED · Indirect speech structure, *als wollte er ihr sagen…* · *As if to say: No. Nothing is okay.*

8. **L73–75** · "Then he stopped and planted himself in front of Theo's room… / She opened the apartment door…" · UNCLEAR · "She" follows a paragraph that's all about Shadow. It also isn't clear that Nora walked past him. · *Nora stepped around him and opened the apartment door.*

9. **L79** · "Like a wall of ice pressing against her." · RHYTHM · Chapter 2 already used "like a wall" twice for the cold and for Shadow. A third use flattens the image. · *Like stepping into a freezer.*

10. **L83** · "Outside the circle, the darkness was deep and black." · STIFF · "Deep and black" is a filler pair. · *Beyond it, everything was black.*

11. **L97** · "Her heart skipped a beat." · STIFF · A stock cliché at a key reveal. · Cut it and let the closed door land on its own.

12. **L113** · "Fast. Don't run. Whatever happens, don't run." · UNCLEAR · Nora's inner voice jumps into present-tense commands with no italics. Young readers may think someone is speaking. · Italicize: *Don't run. Whatever happens, don't run.*

13. **L133** · "That slams a heavy wooden door." · OTHER · Nobody heard a slam. Nora found the door closed. Theo's joke builds on something that didn't happen. · *"That closes a heavy wooden door. All by itself."*

14. **L139** · "That's the third time you've said that." · OTHER · Only two "There's an explanation" lines appear on the page (L41, L137). Sharp kids count. · Add a third earlier, or change to *"That's, like, the tenth time…"*

15. **L161** · "The door had been closed. They had left it open, and now it was closed." · RHYTHM · Third recap of the open/closed door in one chapter (L99, L125–129, L161). · *Someone—or something—had shut that door.* (keep only this)

16. **L177** · "Shadow lay under the table, chewing on a toy bone." · OTHER · Continuity: last night they had no dog supplies and no money for treats. Where did the toy come from? · *chewing on an old sock* (also funnier)

17. **L189–201** · "Nora went to the window. … Nora's cereal bowl crashed to the floor." · UNCLEAR · We never see her carry the bowl to the window, so the crash comes out of nowhere physically. · *She carried her bowl to the window. She wanted to open it.*

18. **L193** · "In the middle of July. In the middle of the morning, with the sun out." · RHYTHM · "In the middle" twice, and "July" yet again (heavily used in ch. 2). · *On a sunny July morning.*

## Summary

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Probably not, or only barely. Theo's dialogue is the strongest in these three chapters and sounds fully American ("practicing being a blanket," "You can turn off a movie," "sixty-five pounds… he's babysitting me," "ghosts keep business hours"). The only real translation tells are the door handle and the "as if he wanted to tell her no" construction. The other problems are logic and continuity, not language.

**(2) Voice:**
- Nora (12): **3/5.** Her stubborn "There's an explanation" is a good running gag, and "Every step controlled" shows character. Her spoken lines are mostly short commands, though. She needs one line of her own wit.
- Theo (10): **5/5.** Funny, scared, and believable. The humor-after-horror timing works.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Yes. "HELP" written in the fog, then the bowl crashing, is a great middle-grade chapter ending: concrete, visual, and a page-turner. Fix the bowl staging (#17) so the crash is earned.
