### Overall verdict (kid)

**Do I want to keep reading after 8 chapters? Yes.** The mystery finally has a face (Lina, 12, died in *our* building). Shadow being Mrs. Silber's dog is a great surprise. Theo is still the best part. The ending of Ch. 8 is the strongest cliffhanger so far.

**Better or worse after Chapter 4?** Up, down, then up again:
- **Ch. 5 is better than anything in 1–4** (footsteps, 7/10).
- **Ch. 6–7 sag.** They recap too much (the list, photo, and check marks get re-explained three times). Ch. 7 has almost no jokes. I drop a lot of things (glass again), and the windows stop being surprising because the third message works like the first two.
- **Ch. 8 is back up** (best jokes plus the 7/10 ending).

On balance it gets **slightly better** after Ch. 4, because the scares climb (Ch. 5–8: 7, 5, 5, 7) and the story now has a goal.

**Fix first:** "Forty years what?" (Ch. 6:139), the glass ambiguity (Ch. 7:105/117), "ghost dog" (Ch. 8:19), the book-description order (Ch. 8:133–139), and the bookshelf that doesn't exist (Ch. 5:111).

## Part 2 – Read-aloud (parent reading to a kid)

### Tongue-twisters / hard to pronounce
- Ch. 5:115 "Heinrich Voss, 1847, **Mühlenweg** 3." The umlaut stops an American parent cold.
- Ch. 5:129 and Ch. 6:61 "**Vogt**." Is it "Vote," "Vogg-t," or "Fohkt"? The name recurs a lot, so the parent will improvise and may be inconsistent.
- Ch. 5:87 book titles in quotes with "1830–1900." The parent has to signal "title" out loud and read the date range.
- Ch. 5:189 "genealogist" (gee-nee-AL-uh-jist). Parents will stumble on it.
- Ch. 6: "microfiche" ×3, "fluorescent" ×3, "voluntarily" ×3 (131 has it twice in one sentence pair: "left the apartment voluntarily… anybody left voluntarily"), "résumé," "Margret" (parents will say "Margaret").
- Ch. 8:119 "Nora **shone** her **phone's** light into the **hole**." Three "o" sounds in a row make a mild sing-song.
- Ch. 8:137 "like a tuning fork behind her temples." A mouthful in a scary beat.

### Sentences hard to read aloud
- Ch. 6:123 and 195: newspaper style with parentheses, "Margret Silber (75)" and "Lina Vogt (12)." The parent must decide on the fly whether to say "seventy-five" or "aged seventy-five."
- Ch. 5:129 "*Lina Vogt? – 1974?*" The question marks and dash can't be voiced. It needs a narrated "with question marks" (which 131 provides, but only after the parent has already read it oddly).
- Ch. 6:163 "the box *1974: October to December*." Fine, but the italics label needs a pause.
- Ch. 8:133–139: reading aloud, the floating fragment "Small. Barely bigger than her palm." (139) has no subject after a paragraph about the light and humming. Listeners lose the referent.

### Unclear who is speaking
- **Ch. 6:43** "Theo said a lot of things. Theo also wanted to get out…" There's no tag, and Theo speaks about himself in the third person. A listener may think Nora is talking about Theo. Suggest adding a tag ("Theo said. 'Did anybody…'") or letting the parent's voice carry it.
- **Ch. 6:61→63** two consecutive Nora paragraphs. Line 63's tag ("Nora said slowly") comes mid-sentence, so after the paragraph break a listener expects Theo. Suggest merging 61 and 63, or giving Theo a beat in between.
- **Ch. 6:137–139** "Forty years what?" A listener can't connect it to anything, because nothing was said (same bug as in Part 1).
- Ch. 8:59 "'Didn't you say there were tools…?' she said." The tag comes after a question that sounds like Theo's style. Fine on the page, slightly unclear aloud.
- Ch. 7:7–23 and Ch. 5:9–31 are fine. The short exchanges alternate cleanly.

### Rhythm stumbles
- Ch. 5:141 vs 187: the same question twice. Reading aloud, the parent will notice the repetition more than a silent reader.
- Ch. 5:133: one long thought-paragraph in the middle of quick fragments. The tempo dips.
- Ch. 6:131 "voluntarily… voluntarily" back to back.
- Ch. 7:117–119: "Don't forget." is repeated four times (103, 107, 117, 119). It's intended, but aloud it gets heavy. Consider cutting one.
- Ch. 5:209 and Ch. 7:133 both open a beat with "Then they heard it." It's a recurring formula, worth varying.
- Ch. 8:137 and 183: two near-identical flicker passages. Aloud, the second feels like a repeat rather than an escalation.

### Words that sound like "Shadow"
- **No "shadow/shadows" as a common noun anywhere in Ch. 5–8.** The translation consistently uses "dark shapes" (Ch. 8:137, 183). That avoids the name clash, but it creates a different problem: "dark shapes on the wall" sounds like creatures, not light effects. Consider "the outlines of the furniture jumped on the wall" or "the dark on the walls stretched" so listeners don't imagine monsters.
- Near-sounds checked: "shutters" (5:73), "showed" (5:149), "show/showing" (7:161, 8:21), "shone" (8:119, 167), "shook" (several). None is confusable in context. 7:161 ("As if he wanted to **show** them…" right after "**Shadow** kept scratching") and 8:21 ("He's **showing** us") put "show" next to the dog's name, which is mildly echo-y but not confusing.
- Possessive "Shadow's" (5:17, 7:153, etc.) sounds identical to "shadows" aloud. In every instance the following noun ("Shadow's paw," "Shadow's breathing") disambiguates it. No action needed.
