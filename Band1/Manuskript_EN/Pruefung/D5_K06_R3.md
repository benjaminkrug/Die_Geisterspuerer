# D5 Line Edit: Chapter 6 "The List of Names" (Round 3)

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9-12, spooky-funny). I read only `Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_06.md`. German street names, "Mrs. + German surname" and short fragments at scary beats are treated as deliberate.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L5** · "Nora jumped down the stairs, two at a time." · TRANSLATED · The US idiom is "took the stairs," and "jumped down" plus "two at a time" is a slightly mixed picture. · *Nora took the stairs two at a time.*

2. **L5** · "The dog's claws scraped over the wood." · TRANSLATED · "Scraped over" sounds like a calque of "über das Holz kratzen." American writers use "clicked/skittered on." · *Shadow's claws skittered on the wood.*

3. **L7** · "No ice, and she couldn't see her breath." · RHYTHM · It sits between two fragments, starts as a fragment and then turns into a clause, so the beat stumbles. · *No ice. No frosty breath.*

4. **L11** · "As if out here was a different world." · TRANSLATED · This reads like "Als ob hier draußen eine andere Welt wäre." A kid narrator would say "like." · *Like out here was a whole different world.*

5. **L17** · "Pause." · STIFF · It works like a stage direction or script note inside the prose and pulls the reader out. · *He paused.* (or cut it and let a paragraph break do the work)

6. **L19** · "Her heartbeat slowly went back to normal." · STIFF · It reads like a medical report, and it also breaks the house rule that emotions stay physical and vivid. · *Her heart finally stopped hammering.*

7. **L23** · "On a wooden floor where nobody was standing." · UNCLEAR · Footsteps belong to someone moving, not standing, so the logic wobbles. The line has no speaker tag either: after Nora's line, a young reader has to guess that this is Theo. · *…where nobody was.* plus add "Theo said."

8. **L31** · "We had a stray outside our door." · TRANSLATED · "We had" for "da war" sounds off. Americans say "There was" or "A stray dog showed up." · *A stray dog shows up at our door.*

9. **L33** · "Kirchgasse was quiet in front of them." · TRANSLATED · The German word order ("vor ihnen lag die Kirchgasse ruhig") comes through, and "in front of them" adds nothing. · *The street was quiet.*

10. **L45** · "The fear gave way to curiosity." · TOO ADULT · This is an abstract emotional summary in an adult register, and it tells rather than shows. · *Her heart was still pounding, but her brain had already started asking questions.*

11. **L55** · "His eyes were calm. Attentive." · TOO ADULT · "Attentive" is a vocabulary word for 10-year-olds and sounds clinical. · *Alert.* or *Ears up.*

12. **L61** · "Nora pictured the blue note again." · UNCLEAR · Without context "blue note" can read as the music term, and the list was described as "on the wall," not as a note. Which object is it? · *the list on the blue paper*

13. **L61-63** · "'Whatever Mrs. Silber was doing,' Nora said slowly, …" · OTHER · Nora speaks in two back-to-back paragraphs, each with its own "Nora said." In US convention a new paragraph for dialogue signals a new speaker, so readers will trip. · Merge them, or give Theo a beat in between.

14. **L69** · "We investigate ghost cases." · TRANSLATED · "Geisterfälle untersuchen" carried over directly. "Ghost cases" isn't an English phrase, and the line falls flat as a punchline. · *We hunt ghosts.* / *We solve ghost mysteries.*

15. **L73** · "A wide building of yellow sandstone with tall windows." · TRANSLATED · "Building of sandstone" is the German "aus" construction. English says "made of," or uses the material as an adjective. · *A big yellow sandstone building with tall windows.*

16. **L85** · "Here, there were answers. Here, there was order." · TRANSLATED · This is the most German-sounding line in the chapter ("Hier gab es Antworten"). In English the comma plus "there were" is clunky. · *Libraries had answers. Libraries had rules.*

17. **L87** · "Smells like Grandma's attic. Just without the cookies." · OTHER · The joke doesn't land logically, since nobody keeps cookies in an attic. · *Smells like Grandma's basement. Minus the cookies.* or switch to "Grandma's house"

18. **L125** (also L117, L127, L143) · "That was all. Three paragraphs." · RHYTHM · "Three paragraphs" comes up four or five times on one page. Theo's hamster joke at L127 needs it once, but by L143 it has gone flat. · Cut it at L125.

19. **L135** · "Behind her, only empty rows of chairs and the buzz of the fluorescent tubes." · RHYTHM · The hum and buzz of the fluorescent lights is repeated two sentences after "The fluorescent lights hummed." The room was also set up with shelves and three computers, not rows of chairs. · *Behind her, only shelves and silence.*

20. **L135** · "She thought of Shadow, waiting up there outside the library." · STIFF · "Up there outside" stacks prepositions awkwardly. · *She thought of Shadow, waiting upstairs by the bike rack.*

21. **L137** · "What could scare a woman who lived alone in a house like that for so long—" · UNCLEAR · "A house like that" points at something the reader can't name: the haunted building? The apartment? A 10-year-old won't follow where Nora's thought is going, so the broken-off line feels random rather than suspenseful. · Give the half-thought a clearer anchor, e.g. "...a woman who lived with ghosts for forty—"

22. **L167** · "It's like Netflix without the picture or the sound." · OTHER · The joke contradicts itself, because the pictures are right on the screen. Netflix without picture or sound is nothing at all. · *It's like Netflix, but with no sound and no plot.*

23. **L169** · "Focus. We need 1974." · UNCLEAR · They are already holding the 1974 box, so the line makes no sense. They need November. · *Focus. We need November.*

24. **L173** · "He was whining. She couldn't hear it, but she could see it in his body." · UNCLEAR · She states that he's whining and then admits she can't hear it. The second sentence is also flat ("see it in his body"). · *He looked like he was whining. She couldn't hear him, but she could see it.*

25. **L199** · "Nora checked the edges of the picture." · STIFF · "Checked the edges" sounds technical. She is scanning faces. · *Nora's eyes moved from face to face.*

26. **L203** · "A girl her age." · RHYTHM · This is redundant right after "Twelve. Just like her." The third repetition weakens the beat. · Cut it.

27. **L203** · "Back when there were no cell phones and newspapers were stored on strips of film." · UNCLEAR · This garbles the timeline. Microfilm storage isn't a feature of 1974 life, it's how the library stores the papers now, so the "back when" contrast doesn't hold. · *Back when there were no cell phones, no internet, nothing.*

28. **L207** · "Her voice was barely audible." · TOO ADULT · "Audible" is a stiff, adult word, and the phrase is a cliché. · *Her voice came out as a whisper.*

29. **L209** · "Read." · RHYTHM · On its own the one-word sentence reads like an imperative (Nora telling him to read again) rather than an action. · *He read it.*

30. **L211** · "The blood drained from his face like water out of a bathtub." · OTHER · A goofy domestic simile right at the peak scare undercuts the reveal. The humor belongs after the beat, not inside it. · *All the color drained from his face.*

## Summary questions

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, mildly. The dialogue is mostly natural and the Theo lines in particular sound native ("This is my life now," "Bless you," "Spookology," "My hamster report was longer"). The narration, though, has clear German fingerprints. These include calqued constructions ("Here, there were answers. Here, there was order.", "We had a stray outside our door," "We investigate ghost cases," "a building of yellow sandstone," "As if out here was a different world") and stage-direction fragments ("Pause.", "Read."). Repeated summary-style sentences ("Her heartbeat slowly went back to normal," "The fear gave way to curiosity") point the same way. An attentive editor would suspect a translation within the first page.

**(2) Voice ratings (1-5)**
- **Nora (12): 3.** She is believable as the rational one ("Could I stop you?", "That counts as school."), but her narration often drifts into adult summary ("attentive," "barely audible," "gave way to curiosity"). Her inner voice needs more kid-specific texture.
- **Theo (10): 4.** He is funny, anxious and well-timed, and his third-person "Theo said a lot of things" bit is great. He loses a point for the two jokes that don't land logically (the Netflix line and the attic cookies).

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Yes, mostly. Moving from "Read the address" to "Kirchgasse 14" to "That's our address" is a classic, effective cliffhanger, and Shadow staring down the street is a good callback. Two things weaken it. The bathtub simile (L211) breaks the tension right at the peak. The final "Home." is also a little ambiguous: the reader can't tell whether the point is that Lina died in their building or that something is happening at home right now. If it's the second, one more concrete sensory hint (Shadow pulling at the leash, for example) would make the last beat hit harder.
