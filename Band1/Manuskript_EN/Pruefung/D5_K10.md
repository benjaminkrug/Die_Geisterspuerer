# D5 Line Edit – Chapter 10: The Basement

Reviewer stance: US middle-grade line editor (9–12, spooky-funny). German setting, "Mrs." + German surnames and short fragments at scary beats accepted as house style.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L3** · "Nora pointed her phone's light down them." · STIFF · "Them" points back to the stairs from the previous sentence, which is clumsy. "Phone's light" is vague. · *Nora aimed her phone's flashlight down the stairs.*
2. **L13** · "Hackles up, steps calm. He just went." · TRANSLATED · "Steps calm" is German compression. In English it reads like a note to self. · *His hackles were up, but he didn't hesitate.*
3. **L19 (also L81 "decades of damp")** · "Old wood, swollen from the damp." · BRITISH · "The damp" used as a noun is British. US kids say "the moisture" or "dampness". · *swollen and soft with moisture*
4. **L19** · "Nora pushed down the handle." · TRANSLATED · American doors have knobs. "Pushed down the handle" reads like a literal *Klinke runterdrücken*. A German lever handle is fine for the setting, but the phrasing gives it away. · *Nora pressed the handle.*, or *Nora tried the handle.*
5. **L19** · "Beyond it lay darkness." · TOO ADULT · Inverted literary syntax, more Gothic novel than middle grade. · *Behind it, everything was dark.*
6. **L31** · "Concrete, fluorescent tubes, outlets." · TRANSLATED · "Fluorescent tubes" comes from *Neonröhren*. US usage is "fluorescent lights". · *fluorescent lights*
7. **L35** · "The ceiling sagged lower." · UNCLEAR · "Sagged" suggests the ceiling is about to collapse, which is a different fear. · *The ceiling hung lower.*
8. **L37** · "…somebody says, Come on, let's go down to the basement?" · OTHER · Quoted speech inside dialogue needs marking, such as italics or single quotes. As it stands, the question mark also turns the whole clause into a question. · *somebody says, 'Come on, let's go down to the basement!'*
9. **L43** · "Like blood. Like old coins." · OTHER · Blood is excluded in the series guidelines, and "old coins" already does the job. · *Like old pennies.*
10. **L61** · "And then he did something Nora had never heard him do quite like this." · STIFF · "Did … heard … quite like this" is a tangle. It also clashes with Ch. 9, where Shadow whines several times. · *Then he made a sound Nora had never heard from him.*
11. **L65** · "Not the short, high whine from earlier, at the diary." · STIFF · "Earlier, at the diary" is a translated time-and-place tag. · *Not the little whine from upstairs.*
12. **L67** · "Nora's skin tightened. Like an electric shock." · UNCLEAR · Mixed image: tightening skin and an electric shock are different sensations. · Pick one: *A shiver zapped down her arms.*
13. **L71** · "…as if he were grieving." · OTHER · This gives away the reveal at L113 ("Grief."), so the later one-word beat loses its punch. · *as if he were hurt.*
14. **L75** · "A weight pressed down on her chest." · OTHER · The same figure appears in Ch. 9 (L119), and the "weight in her chest" pattern is starting to repeat. · Vary it, e.g. *Her stomach dropped.*
15. **L91** · "Only the dripping. And Shadow's whining, soft now, almost too quiet to hear." · TRANSLATED · The opening "Only…" has no anchor (German *Nur noch …*). English needs "Silence" or "There was" before it. · *Nothing but the dripping.*
16. **L93** · "Theo swallowed so loud that Nora heard it." · OTHER · Grammar: it should be "loudly". In narration a kid copyeditor would catch it. · *Theo gulped so loudly Nora could hear it.*
17. **L95** · "Something was spreading through her chest. Not fear. Something else. Something deeper. Sadness." · RHYTHM · "Something" three times in a row, and it ends on an abstract label instead of a physical feeling. · *Not fear. Her throat ached like she was about to cry.*
18. **L111** · "Suddenly. From one moment to the next." · TRANSLATED · "From one moment to the next" is *von einem Moment auf den anderen*. It's also redundant after "Suddenly". · *Just like that.*
19. **L133** · "His face twisted. Not from fear. From confusion." · STIFF · "Twisted" is far too strong for confusion. · *He frowned.*
20. **L145** · "Like a hand opening. Like breathing settling down." · UNCLEAR · Two abstract similes in a row, and "breathing settling down" doesn't call up a clear picture for a 10-year-old. · Keep *Like a fist letting go.*
21. **L149** · "The thickness behind Nora's back faded away." · STIFF · "The thickness" as a noun is odd and sounds translated. · *The heavy feeling behind her faded.*
22. **L157** · "But on the floor." · RHYTHM · The fragment reads like a cut-off sentence rather than a deliberate beat. · *But the floor wasn't.*
23. **L159** · "There were tracks in the dust on the basement floor." · RHYTHM · Repeats "floor" from the line above, and "basement" is obvious by now. · *Tracks. In the dust.*
24. **L161** · "They led from the corner with the scratch marks to right behind the spot where Nora had just been standing." · RHYTHM · At 20 words with a "to right behind the spot where" chain, it's too long for a key scare. · *They led from the scratched corner. Straight to where Nora had been standing.*
25. **L163** · "These prints were barefoot. A child's feet." · STIFF · Prints can't be "barefoot". "A child's feet" also repeats L159 word for word. · *These were bare feet.*
26. **L167** · "With that look that knew too much." · TRANSLATED · A look that "knows" is a German construction (*mit diesem Blick, der zu viel wusste*). · *Like he knew more than they did.*
27. **L173** · "He grabbed hers back." · STIFF · Small, but it reads oddly. · *He squeezed back.*
28. **L175** · "But the footprints stayed in the dust." · RHYTHM · A flat summary line right before the kicker, and it slows the drop into "Run." · Cut it.
29. **L79** · "Like someone who had scratched at the stone with their fingers." · OTHER · Tone check: a trapped child clawing at a wall is the darkest image in Book 1 so far. It's acceptable, but don't add to it, and make sure later chapters soften it. · (Editorial query only.)
30. **L25** · "past the old bike" · OTHER · Continuity: L21 says "Old bikes" (plural). · *past the old bikes*

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **Yes.** The dialogue passes, and Theo is fully American ("rear guard", "Not even to get a soda"). The narration, though, has a cluster of tells within a few pages: "steps calm", "pushed down the handle", "fluorescent tubes", "from one moment to the next", "the thickness", "a look that knew too much", "Only the dripping." Any one of them would pass. Together they give it away.
2. **Voice:** Nora **3/5**. She is almost pure sensation in this chapter: taste, skin, weight, goosebumps. She gets few lines and little personality beyond "I'll go first." Theo **5/5**. The movie-theater bit, "Not normal cold", and "New rule: no more basements" show his voice at its best. This is the funniest scary chapter so far.
3. **Ending strong?** **Mostly yes.** The footprints that end exactly where she felt the presence are an excellent image. "Run" works as a kicker, but two things weaken it. L175 is a filler line that stalls the momentum. And the presence was just shown as gentle, so the reader may ask *why* run. A half-beat of fresh threat before "Run" (a sound, Shadow's hackles going up again) would make the ending land harder.
