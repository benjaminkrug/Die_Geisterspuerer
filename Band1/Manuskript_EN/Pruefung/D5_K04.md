# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 4: "Condensation"

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). Read in isolation, chapters 2–4 only.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. **L1** · "Chapter 4: Condensation" · OTHER · A flat science-class word as a chapter title. Every other title is evocative. · → "Writing on the Window" or "HELP"
2. **L3–7** · "The window was fogged up. … The letters were clear, the edges a little blurred." · OTHER · This is the third chapter in a row that opens by re-describing the previous cliffhanger. It also contradicts Ch3 L197 ("clear and sharp"). · → Cut to one line of recap and keep the description consistent.
3. **L11** · "Pressure spread beneath her ribs." · TRANSLATED · Abstract and anatomical, a calque-like body cue. · → "Her chest went tight."
4. **L13** · "PS: Were you cold last night too?" · TRANSLATED · US style is "P.S." with periods and no colon. · → "P.S. Were you cold last night too?"
5. **L19** · "Theo came shuffling into the kitchen in bare feet." · OTHER (continuity) · In Ch3 L171–185 Theo is already sitting at the table drinking hot chocolate and has already told Nora about Mom's note. Here he enters fresh. This is a major continuity break.
6. **L19** · "…in bare feet." · STIFF · Americans say "barefoot." · → "Theo shuffled into the kitchen barefoot."
7. **L33** · "Her hands were shaking, but she ignored it." · STIFF · The pronoun doesn't agree ("hands … it"). · → "…but she ignored them."
8. **L37** · "Just a damp streak on her sweater." · OTHER · A sweater in July, after three chapters of insisting it's summer. It's justified only if she's wearing it because of the cold, so say so. · → "on her sleeve"
9. **L45** · "And for a moment—one single, short moment—she believed it." · TRANSLATED · "One single, short moment" mirrors *einen einzigen, kurzen Moment*. · → "And for one second, she believed it."
10. **L47** · "Her knees buckled, and she grabbed for the counter." · OTHER (repetition) · Knees give out in Ch3 L115, here, and again at L161. The body cues repeat. · → Use a different reaction once (stomach, hands, throat).
11. **L53** · "Her little brother, who was scared of his own shadow." · UNCLEAR · The dog is named Shadow, so kids will read this as a pun or a reference to the dog. · → "who was scared of the dark."
12. **L59** · "Mom had picked it up the night before, along with a leash." · OTHER (continuity) · Ch2 says the plan was to buy things "tomorrow," and Mom fell asleep on the couch that night. Ch3 L187 also has Shadow under the table, not in a bed. · → "Mom must have picked it up on her way to work."
13. **L59** · "He had lifted his head and was looking at the window." · STIFF · The past-perfect/progressive mix blurs the moment. · → "He lifted his head and looked at the window."
14. **L63** · "She knew it in the part of her that wasn't rational." · TOO ADULT · "Rational" is an abstract, grown-up self-analysis. · → "She knew it deep down, in the part of her that didn't care about logic."
15. **L69** · "First day of school. We can't miss it." · UNCLEAR · Earlier chapters hammer "July." A US kid will ask why school starts in July. It's plausible in Germany but needs one clause. · → "First day at the new school. Here, summer break ends in July." (or drop "July" earlier)
16. **L93** · "Hundreds of kids. Nobody knew them. A teacher explaining fractions." · RHYTHM · The list jumps from crowd to them to a classroom, and L101 "In math class" then repeats the fractions. · → Move the teacher to L101: "In math class. Between the fractions on the board."
17. **L105** · "…for the blink of an eye, she saw something behind her." · TRANSLATED · A calque of *für einen Wimpernschlag*. US usage is "in the blink of an eye," which means fast, not "briefly." · → "for a split second"
18. **L133** · "They went up. Second floor. Apartment door open." · UNCLEAR · Does this mean they opened it, or that it was standing open? An open door should be alarming, but nobody reacts. · → "They went up to the second floor and unlocked the apartment."
19. **L147** · "The window had gone blind." · TRANSLATED · A German idiom (*blind* for fogged glass). In English it means nothing, or it suggests blinds. · → "The window had fogged over again."
20. **L147** · "…from the bottom to the middle. In the middle of the afternoon." · RHYTHM · "Middle" twice in a row. · → "…halfway up. In the middle of the afternoon."
21. **L153** · "Next to it, an arrow. Not a letter. A line with a point." · STIFF · "Not a letter" is unnecessary, and "a line with a point" is how an adult explains an arrow. · → "Next to it, an arrow."
22. **L161** · "Her fingers wouldn't close right. The glass clinked against the counter. Her breath caught. Her knees went weak." · RHYTHM · Four body cues in a row deaden the moment. One strong one would do more. · → Keep the clinking glass and cut two others.
23. **L169** · "No joke. No sarcasm. Just wide eyes and pressed lips." · TRANSLATED · "Pressed lips" is a calque of *zusammengepresste Lippen*. · → "Just wide eyes and a tight mouth."
24. **L185** · "The ticking of the radiator." · OTHER · A ticking radiator means the heat is on, in July. That could be a great creepy clue, but as written it reads like a mistake. Also see L59 ("next to the radiator"). · → If it's intentional, have Nora notice: "The radiator was ticking. In July."
25. **L191** · "He was looking to the left. Toward the stairwell. Up." · UNCLEAR · Shadow is inside the apartment facing a closed door, so he can't look toward the stairwell. · → "He was staring up at the ceiling, at the corner nearest the stairs."
26. **L23–31** · "NORA!" / "I TOLD YOU!" / "SOMEBODY WHO ISN'T THERE!" · OTHER · Five all-caps bursts in ten lines. This feels shouty on the page and dilutes the caps of HELP/HERE. · → Keep caps for the written words, and use italics or exclamation marks for Theo.
27. **L197–199** · "He was waiting for them. / Patient. Calm. As if he knew exactly where they had to go." · OTHER · The ending deflates. "Patient. Calm." also echoes Ch2 L121 ("Calm. Patient."). See (3).

## End-of-chapter assessment

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes. This chapter has the most clear tells: "The window had gone blind" (a German idiom that doesn't work in English), "PS:", "one single, short moment," "for the blink of an eye" (wrong meaning), "pressed lips," and "pressure spread beneath her ribs." Continuity slips also add a "reworked from another version" feel: Theo entering twice, the dog bed appearing, the radiator in July. The dialog itself is still good ("Condensation doesn't spell!", "ghost flu," "Tell that to the window").

**(2) Voice:** Nora (12): 3/5. The "condensation" explanation is her best rational moment, and wiping the window is a great character action. But the near-collapse (knees buckling, "We shouldn't have gone up there") comes fast, and her interior lines turn adult ("part of her that wasn't rational"). Theo (10): 4/5. His role reversal ("Nora. Breathe.") is a lovely, earned beat, and "That's going on my tombstone" is great. He loses a point for the all-caps yelling, which reads younger than 10.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Medium. The real hook is HERE plus the arrow pointing up (L151–159), which is excellent. The chapter then winds down with a calm dog waiting at the stairs. The tension goes slack instead of spiking. Better to end on a new stimulus: a sound from upstairs, the third-floor door now standing open, or Shadow's hackles going up after all.
