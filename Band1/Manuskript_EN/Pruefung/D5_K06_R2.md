# D5 Line Edit, Round 2: Chapter 6 "The List of Names"

Reviewer: D5 (US middle-grade line editor, spooky-funny list)
Scope: Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_06.md only. German names and places, "Mrs." with German surnames, and short fragments at scary beats are house style and are not flagged.

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

1. L19 · "Her heart slowly calmed down." · STIFF · This reads as a flat report, and "calmed down" is an odd thing to say about a heart. · "Her heartbeat slowed."

2. L45 · "The fear gave way to curiosity. It always happened like that." · TOO ADULT · "Gave way to" is adult-register narration, and the line tells the reader what the scene should show. · "Then the questions started. They always did."

3. L61 · "Nora saw the blue note in front of her again." · TRANSLATED · A literal "sah ... wieder vor sich." In English, "saw it in front of her" suggests the note is physically there. · "Nora pictured the blue note."

4. L63 · "“Whatever Mrs. Silber was doing,” Nora said slowly, ..." · OTHER · Two paragraphs of Nora dialog in a row, with no beat between them. Readers expect a speaker change at a paragraph break and will think Theo is talking until the tag. · Merge with L61, or put a Theo beat between them.

5. L69 · "We investigate ghost cases." · STIFF · "Investigate ghost cases" sounds like a police procedural, not a 10-year-old. · "We hunt ghosts." / "We do ghost homework."

6. L77 · "And if anything creepy happens: bark." · TRANSLATED · A colon before an imperative is a German habit. It is minor because it is funny here, but a dash reads more naturally. · "And if anything creepy happens, bark."

7. L85 · "Here, there were answers. Here, the world had order." · STIFF · "The world had order" is abstract and slightly translated ("hatte die Welt Ordnung"). · "Here, things made sense."

8. L111 · "The newspaper's search page loaded slowly." · UNCLEAR · Which newspaper? No paper has been named, so "the newspaper's" has nothing to refer to. · "The search page for the *Gravenstedt Times* (or similar) loaded slowly."

9. L123 · "Margret Silber (75), a longtime resident of Kirchgasse, ..." · TRANSLATED · Age in parentheses is German press style. US papers write "Margret Silber, 75,". Since the setting is German you could defend keeping it, but US kids won't know the convention. · "Margret Silber, 75, ..."

10. L117/L125 · "Three paragraphs." · OTHER · The narration says three paragraphs twice, but the article as printed is one block. Kids will count. · Break the quoted article into three short paragraphs.

11. L135 · "...and the buzz of the fluorescent tubes." · RHYTHM · "Fluorescent" appears twice in this paragraph and again at L109, with hum and buzz back to back. · Cut the second mention.

12. L137 · "What could scare a woman who lived alone in a house like that for so long—" · UNCLEAR · The thought is never completed or explained, and "a house like that" has no clear meaning for the reader. Theo asks "What do you mean?" and gets no answer, so the reader is left hanging in the wrong way. · Finish the thought, or replace it with a concrete question.

13. L159 · "Little strips of film with photos of newspaper pages." · OTHER · Microfiche is flat sheets, and strips or reels are microfilm. The scene also has her turning a knob through pages, which is microfilm behavior. An adult will catch this. · Call it microfilm throughout, or say "sheets."

14. L167 · "It's like Netflix without the picture or the sound." · OTHER · The joke does not track, because the machine *does* show pictures. Kids will notice the logic gap. · "It's like Netflix, but every show is about furniture ads."

15. L169 · "Focus. We need 1974." · UNCLEAR · They already pulled the 1974 box, and what they need is November. · "Focus. We need November."

16. L195 · "Lina Vogt (12), daughter of the Vogt family, Kirchgasse 14, died on November 3, 1974, as the result of an accident." · TRANSLATED · The bracketed age and "daughter of the Vogt family" are German death-notice formulas. A cold tone is fine, but this specific phrasing is foreign. It is also one sentence, while the text says "six lines." · "Lina Vogt, 12, of Kirchgasse 14, died November 3 in an accident."

17. L199 · "Nora checked the edges of the picture. Bottom right. A girl with a dark braid. Lina Vogt." · UNCLEAR · How does Nora know which girl is Lina? No caption or arrow is mentioned. · Add "A tiny caption under her: *L. Vogt*."

18. L199 · "That was exactly what made it so hard." · TRANSLATED · "Genau das machte es so schwer" is abstract and adult. The feeling is told, not shown. · Cut, or make it physical ("Nora's throat closed up.").

19. L203 · "From a time when there were no cell phones and newspapers ended up on strips of film." · STIFF · "Ended up on strips of film" is a clumsy aside right at an emotional beat. · Cut after "Just like her."

20. L211 · "The blood drained from his face like water out of a bathtub." · OTHER · A comic simile on the chapter's biggest reveal undercuts the shiver, and the house rule is humor *after* the scare, not during. · "Theo's face went white."

21. L221 · "He hadn't been staring just anywhere." · STIFF · A negative, indirect build-up to the final word, with a slightly unnatural "just anywhere." · "He had been staring toward home."

## Chapter verdict

1. **Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, mildly. Theo's lines are pure American middle grade ("Bless you," "My hamster report was longer," "Spookology?"). The newspaper texts are the giveaway: "(75)", "(12)", and "daughter of the Vogt family" are German press conventions. Together with "saw the note in front of her," the microfiche/microfilm confusion and a few abstract lines, this chapter has the most tells of the four.

2. **Voice:** Nora 3/5. Her love of libraries is a nice character note, and the "That counts as school" fib is good. Elsewhere she mostly delivers plot. Theo 5/5. He is the engine of the chapter, and the rapid-fire summary at L31 is excellent.

3. **Ending:** Strong concept, slightly soft execution. "That's our address." is the real punch and could end the chapter. The coda (Shadow staring, "He hadn't been staring just anywhere. / Home.") works, but the stiff setup line weakens it. Also, the speaker of "That's our address" is ambiguous after "Nora nodded."
