# D5 – Line Edit, Chapter 14: The Map

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12). Mark and explain only; no rewrites.
Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

## Findings

- **L9** · "Saturday. No school." · OTHER (cultural) · The book says it is July (Ch. 15/16). For an American reader, July means summer vacation, so "No school" on a Saturday sounds strange. The same goes for "the teacher asks on Monday" in Ch. 13. German schools are often in session in July, but US kids won't know that. · Suggest: one line somewhere that explains it ("Two more weeks until summer break"), or drop "No school."
- **L51** · "But she couldn't do it." · STIFF · "Do it" is vague: do what? It sounds like a literal rendering. · Suggest: "But she never got to help her."
- **L69–73** · "Black ink on yellowed paper." / "drawn in black ink." · RHYTHM · "Black ink" appears twice in four lines. · Cut the second one.
- **L79** · "Right there, between two roofs, the blue light had flickered last night. Or somewhere close by. She wasn't sure." · UNCLEAR · "Right there" immediately followed by "Or somewhere close by. She wasn't sure" undercuts itself. A kid may be unsure whether the cemetery is the blue light's source or not. · Suggest: "That was about where the blue light had flickered last night."
- **L101** · "The ink was thick and dark. Dark red like old sealing wax." · RHYTHM · "Dark. Dark" stumbles. Many kids also won't know "sealing wax" yet; it only gets set up in Ch. 15. · Suggest: "The ink was thick, the color of dried cherries." (or keep the wax and drop the first "dark")
- **L125** · "'Convenient,' Theo said dryly." · TOO ADULT · A one-word ironic "Convenient" is a grown-up, TV-detective line. A sarcastic 10-year-old would push it harder. · Suggest: "'Oh, *super* convenient,' Theo said."
- **L151** · "'Mrs. Silber has been missing for months,' Nora said." · UNCLEAR · Nora skips a logical step. Readers have to work out on their own that she means Mrs. Silber couldn't have drawn the X. Many 10-year-olds will miss why this line matters, and the next beat depends on it. · Suggest: add "…so how could she?"
- **L163** · "But the map had been behind the board. Until a little while ago." · TRANSLATED · "Until a little while ago" is a literal time phrase. "The board" also loses the clearer "baseboard" from L61. · Suggest: "But the map had been hidden behind the baseboard until twenty minutes ago."
- **L177** · "And her own fingerprints from before." · TRANSLATED · "From before" (vorhin) is a German tell. Americans say "from earlier," or say nothing. · Suggest: "And her own fingerprints."

## End-of-chapter assessment

1. **Would you guess this was translated?** **No.** This is the cleanest of the four chapters. The breakfast banter (résumé, "gray hair by eleven," spiders) sounds like native middle grade. Only a couple of time-phrase tells remain (L163, L177), and most readers would never notice them.
2. **Voice:** Nora 4/5. She is focused and driven, and "Or the most dangerous." is a strong line, though L151 is too elliptical. Theo 5/5. His résumé list and the gray-hair math are exactly what a sarcastic, scared 10-year-old sounds like.
3. **Ending strong?** **Yes, very.** The wet ink, the undisturbed dust and Shadow's calm (which is "almost worse than growling") make a genuinely creepy, intelligent cliffhanger. "And yet the ink was wet." lands well. The chapter's best button.
4. **Counts:** TRANSLATED 2 · STIFF 1 · BRITISH 0 · TOO ADULT 1 · UNCLEAR 2 · RHYTHM 2 · OTHER 1 · **Total 9**
