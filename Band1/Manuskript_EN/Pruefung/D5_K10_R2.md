# D5 Line Edit: Chapter 10 "The Basement" (Round 2)

Reviewer role: US middle-grade line editor (ages 9–12, spooky-funny). I read only `Band1/Manuskript_EN/Kapitel_10.md`. Short fragments at scary beats are house style, so I only flag them when they stack up or blur the meaning.

## Findings

Format: line · quoted sentence · category · why · optional suggestion

1. L3 · "Nora pointed her phone's flashlight down the stairs." · RHYTHM · Repeats "stairs" right after "The basement stairs were steep." · "...down into the dark."
2. L13 · "He just went." · TRANSLATED · Sounds like a literal "Er ging einfach." In English it's vague and limp. · "He didn't hesitate." / cut it.
3. L19 · "Old wood, swollen with moisture." · RHYTHM · "wooden door. Old wood" is a clunky echo. · "Its boards were swollen with damp."
4. L19 · "Nora pressed the handle." · TRANSLATED · This is the German lever-handle gesture ("die Klinke drücken"). American readers think of turning a knob, so "pressed" reads oddly. · "Nora pushed down on the handle." / "turned the knob."
5. L21 / L25 · "Old bikes." … "past the old bike" · OTHER · Plural turns into singular three lines later. Continuity slip. · Pick one.
6. L21 · "Storage lockers with padlocks." · OTHER · This is understandable, but an American apartment basement has "storage cages" or "storage units." "Lockers" makes kids picture school. · "Wire storage cages with padlocks."
7. L23 · "His voice echoed between the walls." · TRANSLATED · Literal "zwischen den Wänden." In English, voices echo *off* walls. · "...echoed off the walls."
8. L31 · "Concrete, fluorescent tubes, outlets." · BRITISH · "Tubes" is British/European usage; Americans say "fluorescent lights." "Outlets" is an odd detail to list. · "Concrete, buzzing fluorescent lights."
9. L41 · "Then you know when it's getting dangerous." · STIFF · "Then" works here as a German "Dann", but a 10-year-old American would say "So you know..." · "That way you know when to panic."
10. L43 · "Evenly." · TRANSLATED · A literal "Gleichmäßig." Kids don't say "evenly" about dripping. · "Steady." (Note: "Steady" is used at L173, so it could echo deliberately.)
11. L47 · "Nora could hear only the dripping." · STIFF · The word order is formal. · "All Nora could hear was the dripping."
12. L49 · "Nothing. Again." · UNCLEAR · "Again" could mean "nothing again" or "she tapped again." · "She tapped it again."
13. L61 · "In front of a corner where two walls met." · STIFF · Redundant, since a corner is where two walls meet. · "In front of the far corner."
14. L61/L65 · "a sound Nora had never heard from him. Not like this." … "Not the short, high whine he had made over the diary." · UNCLEAR · "Never heard" is then contradicted ("not like this", and he has whined before). The logic wobbles. · "a sound Nora had never heard him make."
15. L67 · "Nora's skin tightened. Like an electric shock." · OTHER · The simile doesn't fit, because a shock jolts and doesn't tighten skin. · "A jolt ran over her skin, like static."
16. L71 / L113 · "as if he were grieving." … "Grief." · RHYTHM · Using "grieving" at L71 spends the word before the one-word reveal at L113, so the reveal lands flat. · Cut "as if he were grieving" at L71.
17. L75 · "A weight pressed down on her chest." · RHYTHM · Her reaction comes before we see what she's reacting to (the cracks at L77). The order is reversed. · Move it after L79.
18. L77 · "Always four, side by side." · TRANSLATED · A literal "Immer vier nebeneinander." The rhythm is stiff. · "Four at a time, side by side."
19. L79 · "Like someone who had scratched at the stone with their fingers." · STIFF · The relative clause makes this weak. "Like fingers." just before it makes it redundant. · "Like someone had clawed at the stone."
20. L81 · "Worn smooth by decades of moisture. Fifty years old." · TOO ADULT · "Decades of moisture" is a geologist's phrase. "Fifty years old." is a flat fact Nora can't actually read off a wall. · "Old. Worn smooth. Maybe as old as Lina's story."
21. L93 · "Theo swallowed so loudly that Nora heard it." · TRANSLATED · A literal "so laut, dass". · "Theo gulped. Loudly."
22. L95 · "Something was spreading through her chest. Not fear. Something else. Something deeper. Sadness." · RHYTHM · Three "Something"s and five fragments in a row. "Sadness" also names the emotion outright, where it should be shown. · Cut "Something else."
23. L97 · "With fingers that scratched at stone." · STIFF · The tense and construction are awkward. · "Scratching at the stone."
24. L101 · "Slowly. Bit by bit." · RHYTHM · Redundant pair. · Keep one.
25. L107 · "That's not the pipes, is it?" · UNCLEAR · A kid won't get why pipes would make breath visible. The joke or logic needs one more word. · "That's not just a cold pipe, is it?"
26. L129 · "You're really pale." · STIFF · Americans say "you look pale." "You're pale" reads like "Du bist blass." · "You look super pale."
27. L143 · "Shadow stood up." · OTHER · Continuity: Shadow was already standing (L61, L71, L111). · "Shadow moved."
28. L143 · "His body wasn't trembling anymore." · OTHER · We were never told he trembled, so "anymore" refers to nothing. · Add trembling earlier, or cut.
29. L145 · "Like a hand opening. Like fast breathing slowing down." · TOO ADULT · These are abstract, literary similes for a temperature change, and a 10-year-old may stall on them. · Keep one, e.g. "Like someone letting out a long breath."
30. L149 · "She hadn't noticed until now how tense she had been." · STIFF · Clunky past-perfect stack. · "She hadn't even noticed how tense she'd been."
31. L151 (also L37, L93) · "He swallowed." · RHYTHM · Theo's swallowing tic shows up three times in one chapter, which is repetitive. · Vary it ("He gulped" / cut).
32. L153 · "Her neck cracked softly." · OTHER · A neck "cracks" in a way that sounds painful; American kids say "popped." · "Her neck popped."
33. L159/L163 · "Bare feet. A child's feet." (twice) · RHYTHM · Repeating it word for word at L163 feels like an editing leftover, not a deliberate echo. · Cut from L163.

## Summary questions

**(1) Would I guess this was translated?** Yes, slightly. Most of the chapter reads like a natural, well-paced English spooky book, and Theo's jokes land in idiomatic American (rear guard, soda, "everybody in the theater screams NO"). A few sentences give it away: "pressed the handle", "echoed between the walls", "Evenly.", "Always four, side by side", "swallowed so loudly that", "fluorescent tubes", "You're really pale." These are the fingerprints of German syntax and objects. Fix those dozen lines and I'd stop guessing.

**(2) Voice ratings**
- Nora (12): 3/5. She's mostly carried by narration. She has few lines and they're terse ("We're not in a movie," "Yeah," "Run"), which fits her character, but she doesn't have much personality of her own in this chapter. Her one real voice moment, "Me neither. But she's there.", works well.
- Theo (10): 4.5/5. He's funny, scared and believable. The movie-theater bit and "New rule: no more basements… Not even to get a soda" are exactly right for the age group. The only drag is the repeated swallowing.

**(3) Is the ending strong?** Mostly yes. The footprints ending exactly behind Nora is a great image, and "Lina had been here." lands. Two problems:
- L175 ("stayed in the dust") softens the moment just before the final beat.
- "Run," comes after the presence has already faded and calmed down, so the motivation reads a beat late. It would hit harder if something renewed the threat first (the dripping stops, the light flickers again, Shadow growls), or if "Run" followed directly after "Lina had been here."
