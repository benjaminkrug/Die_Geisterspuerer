<b>A word on the foggy window. A dog who can sense ghosts. And a house that won't let go.</b><br><br>

When Nora (12) and Theo (10) move with their mom to the city of Gravenstedt, the stairs creak and the stairwell smells of old wood. One floor up, there's an empty apartment where the air is ice-cold — in July. And on the front step sits a thin stray dog with amber eyes, looking at them as if he has been waiting for exactly this moment.<br><br>

Then the knocking starts. At night. From the wall behind Nora's headboard. Three times. Pause. Three times.<br><br>

The next morning, someone has written a word in the condensation on the kitchen window: <b>HELP</b>.<br><br>

Nora believes in cold pipes, drafts, and logical explanations. Theo believes in ghosts. And Shadow — their new dog — leads them up the stairs into the empty apartment. On the wall hangs a list of names, each with a date, many with a small check mark. At the very bottom, in blue ink, with no check mark: <i>Lina Vogt? – 1974?</i><br><br>

<b>Fifty years ago, a girl lived in this house. Nobody believed her when she said something was wrong. Now someone is finally listening.</b><br><br>

<i>The Whispering Window</i> is Book 1 of <b>The Ghost Listeners</b> — a series about a brother and sister who don't fight ghosts. They listen to them.<br><br>
