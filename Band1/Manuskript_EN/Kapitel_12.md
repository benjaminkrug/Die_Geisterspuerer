# Chapter 12: Please

Shadow barked.

The sound jolted Nora out of her sleep. Loud. Deep. A single, sharp sound that echoed through the whole apartment.

Nora sat bolt upright in bed. Her whole body was tense. Her hands dug into the covers. Shadow was standing on the floor next to her. Stiff. Upright. Legs locked, hackles up, his eyes fixed on the bedroom door.

He barked again.

Goosebumps prickled over Nora’s skin. In all the time they had known him, Shadow had never barked. Not once. He had growled, whined, refused to go in. But barked? Never.

Until now.

That evening, the knocking had stopped before they worked up the nerve to open the door. Nora had lain awake for a long time.

“Nora!” Theo’s voice came from the hallway. Thin. Scared. “Nora, are you coming?”

She threw back the covers. The cold hit her like a punch. The floor was ice-cold under her bare feet. Her breath came out in a white cloud.

She ran into the hallway. Theo was standing outside his bedroom door, his arms wrapped around himself. He was shivering. Shadow pressed against Nora’s leg and growled softly.

“Look at the windows,” Theo whispered.

Nora turned around. Her fingers went cold.

Every window in the apartment was fogged up. Thick, milky condensation. On the kitchen window. On the hallway window. On the living room window. Everywhere.

And there were words on every window.

DIARY.

READ.

PLEASE.

DON’T FORGET.

As if written with a finger. Big, hurried letters. Water ran down at the edges, but the words stayed.

Nora swallowed. Her throat was tight. The cold crept through her whole body. But it wasn’t just the temperature. The moonlight cast dark shapes on the walls. They were moving. Slowly. Even though nothing in the room stirred. A presence. Stronger than ever before.

“She’s here,” Nora said quietly.

“Yeah.” Theo’s voice cracked. “Nora, I saw something.”

“What?”

He pointed down the hallway. To the end, where the hall turned off toward the bathroom. His finger was shaking.

“A shadow. But not our Shadow. A different one. It was as big as a kid. It was standing there. Just for a second.” Theo swallowed hard. “Then it was gone.”

A cold tingle ran over Nora’s arms. She stared down the dark hallway. Nothing. Only darkness and cold.

Shadow was still growling. Soft, deep, in his throat.

“Did you see a face?” Nora whispered.

“No. Just the outline. Like a figure made of smoke.” Theo rubbed his arms. “Nora, she wrote PLEASE. On every window.”

Nora looked at him. His face was pale in the moonlight. He was shaking all over. But his eyes were serious.

“Do you think Mom heard anything?” he whispered.

Nora listened. Nothing came from Mom’s bedroom. No rustling. No footsteps. Only silence. Mom was sleeping like a rock, the way she always did.

“No,” Nora said.

Nothing but the ticking of the kitchen clock. The words on the windows shimmered in the moonlight. PLEASE. READ. DON’T FORGET.

Theo swallowed. “What do we do?”

Nora took a deep breath. The cold air burned in her lungs. She thought of Mrs. Silber’s notebook. Of the method. Find out the story. Understand it. Show the ghost that someone remembers.

They had read Lina’s diary. But only for themselves. Not for Lina.

“We’re going to help her,” Nora said. “Now. Tonight.”

Theo’s eyes went wide. “How?”

“We’ll read to her,” Nora said. “The diary. Out loud. All of it. Every word. In her room.”

“Her room is your room.”

“Yeah.”

Theo looked at her. Three seconds. Then he nodded. “Okay.”

“Okay?”

“I said okay, Nora. Before I change my mind.” He rubbed his arms. “But afterward I want hot chocolate. With marshmallows. And we never talk about tonight again.”

Nora almost smiled. Almost. She went into her room and took the diary out of her nightstand drawer. The leather cover was cold under her fingers.

Shadow followed her. He wasn’t barking anymore. He didn’t growl. He walked beside her, close against her leg, and his eyes were bright and calm.

---

They sat down on the floor in Nora’s room. Lina’s room. It was so cold that Nora could barely feel her own fingers. Shadow lay down between them, his head on his paws. Alert. Still.

Theo pulled the comforter off Nora’s bed and wrapped it around his shoulders. “If I freeze to death, put this on my tombstone: He just wanted to help.”

“You’re not going to freeze to death.”

“You sure?”

“No.”

Theo snorted softly. Then he got serious. “Start reading.”

Nora opened the diary. Her fingers were stiff with cold. But the shaking didn’t come from the cold. It came from something bigger.

She stopped. What if it didn’t work? What if it made everything worse? What if the cold came back stronger than they could stand?

Theo looked at her. Waiting. Trusting.

Nora swallowed. Then she made up her mind. For Lina.

She began to read.

This time she wasn’t reading for herself. Not for Theo. She was reading for the room. For the cold. For the girl who had slept here before it was Nora’s room.

She read all of it. Every word. The birthday and the purple ink. Luna fetching a stick out of the river. The stars on the roof. She could see her breath above the paper.

Then came the dark pages. The cold room. The scratching in the wall. Lina’s mom, who said *imagination*.

The cold got stronger. Not weaker. Stronger.

Frost spread across the window glass in feathery patterns. The pages went stiff under Nora’s fingers. Theo pulled the comforter up to his nose.

“Nora,” he whispered. “It’s getting worse.”

Nora’s voice got quieter. But she didn’t stop.

Something changed.

It happened so slowly that Nora didn’t notice at first. Her fingers weren’t numb anymore. The air didn’t bite quite as hard. The cold gave way. Not all at once. Like a block of ice melting. Drop by drop.

“*Nobody believes me*,” Nora read.

She read the sentence a second time. Slower. So it would stay in the room.

Theo sniffled. Shadow lifted his head. He looked at a spot next to the bed where nobody was sitting. Then he laid his head back down.

Nora kept reading. Page after page. With every sentence, the cold gave way a little more. Like a wave sliding back from the beach.

Then she reached the last page with writing on it. The shaky letters. The sentence about the basement.

Her voice was only a whisper now. But she read it to the end.

She closed the book.

Silence.

The cold was almost gone. Nora couldn’t see her breath anymore. Her fingers tingled. Feeling came back, slowly, like blood flowing back into hands that had fallen asleep. The air was still cool, but not icy anymore. Like a fall morning. Not like a freezer.

Theo sat there, his knees pulled up to his chest. Tears ran down his cheeks, but he was smiling. A small, shaky smile.

“She was listening,” he whispered. “The whole time.”

Shadow lay still between them. His fur was soft. His body warm. His eyes were half closed. Relaxed. For the first time that night.

Nora hugged the diary to her chest. The leather cover wasn’t cold anymore. It was warm. Hand-warm, as if someone had held it for a long time.

“We heard you, Lina,” Nora said into the silent room. “We know your story. You weren’t alone.”

Nothing happened. No sound. No wind. No flickering.

But something came loose. Like a knot coming undone. Like a breath going out at last. The heaviness was gone.

Theo leaned back against the wall. “I think she’s crying,” he whispered.

Nora nodded. Her own eyes burned. She blinked the tears away.

Shadow sighed deeply. One long dog sigh. Then he closed his eyes all the way.

---

Minutes went by. Maybe longer. Nora didn’t know.

Then she stood up. Her legs were stiff. She went to the window. The condensation was almost gone. The words had faded. READ. PLEASE. DON’T FORGET. All gone. Erased, as if the warmth had wiped them away.

But there, on the last strip of condensation at the bottom of the glass, was a new word.

THANK YOU.

Nora’s breath caught. She put her fingertips against the glass. It was warm. Not cold. Warm.

“Theo,” she whispered. “Come here.”

Theo stood next to her. He read it. His chin trembled.

“You’re welcome, Lina,” he murmured. He rubbed his arms. “Next time, maybe not at three in the morning.”

Nora smiled.

Then a second word appeared right next to it. Letter by letter. As if an invisible finger were writing on the glass.

UP.

Nora stared at the word. Her smile vanished. Something cold stabbed through her chest. Up. Mrs. Silber’s apartment was right above them. On the third floor.

Theo looked at her. His eyes were wide. “What’s upstairs, Nora?”

Nora didn’t answer. She held on tight to the diary and stared at the words in the condensation.

THANK YOU. UP.

Shadow lifted his head. His eyes went to the ceiling. His tail tapped the floor once. Then he was still again.
