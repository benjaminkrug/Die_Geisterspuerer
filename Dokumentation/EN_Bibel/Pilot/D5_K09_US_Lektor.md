# D5 – US Middle-Grade Editor Pass: Chapter 9 "The Diary"

**Reviewer role:** Editor, US children's publisher (middle grade, 9–12, spooky/funny)
**Source read:** `EN_K09.md` only (no original, no notes)
**Scope:** Mark and explain only. No rewrite. One-line suggestions are optional.

**Paragraph numbering:** Every text block between blank lines counts as one paragraph. The chapter title and the `---` scene breaks do not count. P1 = "The cold hit Nora like a slap." The final paragraph is P98. Source line numbers (L) are given too.

---

## Overall impression (before the line notes)

This is an emotionally strong chapter. The diary device works: the entries escalate cleanly from cotton candy to "I live in one" to the blank pages. Kids will feel it. The core problems are on the surface of the prose:

- **Staccato overload.** Nearly every paragraph breaks into fragments ("Alert. But calm." / "Heavy. Cold." / "His warm body. His calm breathing."). One or two per page is punchy. This many reads like a style rule applied mechanically, and it is the biggest single "translated" tell.
- **A handful of literal idioms** that no American writer would produce: "tongue stuck to the roof of her mouth," "Dad says imagination," "nobody who believed her," "the others will believe me," "ceiling lamp."
- **Repeated beats:** "quietly" (4x), "whispered/whisper" (6x), "Shadow lifted his head" (3x), "Nora swallowed" (3x), "as if he knew" (2x), voice cracking/breaking (3x).
- **POV slips:** "the children," "a ten-year-old boy" — Nora wouldn't think of herself or her brother that way.

## Summary questions

### (1) Would I guess this was translated?

**Yes. I'd guess it fairly quickly, though not from the first page.** The dialogue is mostly natural American and the emotional beats are well judged. But three things give it away:
- **Literal idioms and calques:** "tongue stuck to the roof of her mouth," "Dad says imagination," "ceiling lamp," "in rhythm," "nobody who believed her," "the others will believe me," "there was a pressure in her chest," "the tears just ran."
- **Relentless verbless fragments** ("Alert. But calm." / "Heavy. Cold."). That pattern is typical of a short-sentence rule carried across from another language. A native MG writer would vary it more.
- **Small logic and continuity slips** that look like nuance lost in translation: "blamed it on the heat," "Her best friend ... Not even her," "someone ... it," Nora's comment printed in diary italics.

### (2) Voice authenticity (1–5)

| Voice | Score | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Nora (12)** | **3** | She gets very little dialogue ("Yeah," "I know" x2). Most of her character sits in narration that drifts adult ("pulse pounded in her temples," "a ten-year-old boy"). The apology ("I shut you down") sounds like a grown-up. "This is home, Theo" is a strong moment. |
| **Theo (10)** | **4** | The most convincing voice. The sarcasm about sleepwalking chairs and "would've been even more scared" sound exactly like a 10-year-old. The shift into crying feels earned. One unclear line: "At least she had a dog." |
| **Lina's diary (12, 1974)** | **3** | The simplicity and escalation feel real, and "I live in one" is great. But it has no "Dear Diary," no period texture and no kid punctuation habits. Some words are bookish ("scolded," "refuses," "the others," "something strange"), and every entry has the same tidy register. It could be 1974 or 2024. |

### (3) Is the final paragraph a strong chapter ending?

**Partly.** The setup is excellent: "What's in the cellar, Nora?" plus Shadow's reaction plus a sound from below. But the actual hook is the noise in P96, and the chapter then steps *down* from it into a talky, formal line ("That ... is what we have to find out"). That line is a stock series cliché and the sentence split sounds unnatural for a 12-year-old. Either end on the sound itself (e.g., Shadow growls at the floor, end) or keep the line but make it short and kid-like ("“I don't know,” Nora whispered. “But we're going to find out.”"). Also fix "cracked" / "house" so the final image is precise.
