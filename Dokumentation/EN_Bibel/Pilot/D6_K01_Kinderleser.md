The front half is packed with jokes. In the back half (from about L117) the humor gets thin, and there's no real laugh anymore. That's okay for the ending, but the scare at L101–115 is followed by a weak joke (L117).

### Scariest moment

1. **L221–235: the dog growls at the SILBER door.** Hackles up, Theo grabs her sleeve, "A sound Nora didn't feel in her ears. But in her stomach." → **5/10.** Good tingly feeling, no nightmares.
2. L101–115: footsteps upstairs, "It didn't." / "It had." → **4/10.** "It had." is a really good line on its own.
3. L57–63: handprint in the plaster → 3/10.
4. L161–163: the dog looks past Nora into the stairwell, "Nobody." → 3/10, creepy in a quiet way.

Overall the chapter is at about 3–5/10, which fits Book 1.

### Unintended silliness / knock-knock moments

- **L5 "Kirchgasse"**: a 10-year-old reads "Kirch-GAS-ee" and giggles (gas = fart). **High risk.** Repeating it would make it worse.
- **L109 "That's just the wood."**: almost a knock-knock line ("Wood who?" / "Wood you open the door?"). Low risk.
- **L15 "Old house means old."**: you can read it as unintentionally silly. It works because Theo is the one saying it.
- **L223 "hackles"**: sounds like "cackles". Low risk.
- **L219 "SILBER"**: a kid might read it as "Slobber" or "Silver" with a typo. Low to medium risk. Maybe add a pronunciation hint in a later chapter, or have Theo say "Silver?" so the joke happens on purpose.
- **L171 "muzzle"**: you picture a dog wearing a muzzle, then putting it in Theo's hand. Funny picture, not on purpose.

### Would I read chapter 2?

**Yes.** Because:
- I want to know who's behind the SILBER door and who was walking upstairs.
- The dog is cool. I want to know if they keep him and what his name is.
- Theo is funny. I'd read more for him.

## Part 2 – Read-aloud (parent reading to a kid)

### Tongue-twisters / sounds that are hard to say

| L | Spot | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| L5 | "Kirchgasse 14" | The parent doesn't know how to say it either (KEERSH-gah-seh). Stops the flow at line 2 of the book. |
| L35 | "The stairs creaked under their feet." → L45 "creaky floorboards" → L57 radiator "ticked" | Lots of hard k sounds close together. Fine on its own, but it piles up. |
| L121 | "Sorted by size. Order helped. Order always helped." | s/z and "order always" run together. |
| L193 | "Matter-of-factly, as if he had done it a thousand times." | "Matter-of-factly" trips the tongue. |
| L213 | "Purposeful. Without sniffing, without stopping." | p-/s- clusters. Okay but tricky. |
| L225 | "The hair on the back of Nora's neck stood up." | "Nora's neck" runs together. Minor. |

### Sentences that are hard to read aloud

- **L85** "The meeting at the hospital. First time meeting everyone, I can't move it." The comma splice and "meeting … meeting" make the reader stumble. Suggestion: "It's the meeting at the hospital. I meet everyone for the first time. I can't move it."
- **L45** "The apartment door was brown and heavy and squeaked when it opened. Behind it was the apartment." "Apartment" twice in a row sounds clunky.
- **L147** Long paragraph with a dash ("Thin—even from up here she could almost see his ribs."). The parent has to switch tone at the dash. It works but needs practice.
- **L187** "under his fur. His fur was dull …" repeats "fur" at a sentence break. Sounds like a mistake aloud.
- **L231–233** "A sound Nora didn't feel in her ears. / But in her stomach." "Didn't feel in her ears" sounds odd aloud, because you hear with your ears, you don't feel with them. Suggestion: "A sound Nora didn't hear with her ears. / She felt it in her stomach." This is the climax, so it has to read smoothly.
- **L19** "Too wide. Too forced. New job, new city, new life." Three fragment groups in a row, then the next sentence. Hard to find the right tone.

### Unclear who is speaking

- **L75** "Or the house hates us." There's no tag, and narration about Nora (L73) comes right before it. The parent has to guess it's Theo. Suggestion: "Or the house hates us," Theo said.
- **L43** "Antique and broken." No tag after the mother's line. Probably Theo, mostly clear.
- **L119** "Stop whining and help me unpack." No tag. Clear from context (Nora).
- **L153–155** "A stray." / "He's waiting for someone." The alternation works, but with no tags the parent can lose track. Mostly clear.
- **L183–185** Fine.
- Overall: speakers are mostly clear. **L75 is the one real problem.**

### Where the rhythm stumbles

- **"Their mother"** appears about 8 times (L19, 31, 41, 65, 81, 85, 95). Aloud it sounds stiff and like a translation. In US kids' books the narration would say "Mom" (Theo says "Mom's gone" himself in L117). Suggestion: say "Mom" in the narration, or "their mom".
- **L5–7** Many fragments in a row ("Cobblestones, houses that leaned… Nobody in sight.") followed by a long sentence. The opening reads a little choppy.
- **L101** "Only the ticking … The dripping … And somewhere above them …" Nice rhythm, works well aloud.
- **L113–115** "It didn't." / "It had." Great beat for reading aloud. Keep it.
- **L195–205** The quick back-and-forth works very well aloud.
- **L107** The early mention of the nameplate is a content stumble too: the listener asks, "What nameplate?"
