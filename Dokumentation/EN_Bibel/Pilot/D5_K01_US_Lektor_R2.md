**Text reviewed:** `EN_K01.md`, "Chapter 1: New City, Old House"
**Reviewer role:** US middle-grade editor (ages 9–12, spooky/funny). This was a cold read: I had no original, no notes, and no earlier reviews.
**Paragraph numbering:** every non-empty line after the chapter heading counts as one paragraph, so P1 = "The moving truck was parked crooked…" and P117 = "He was growling at the door."

## Summary

### (1) Would I guess this was translated?

**No.** On a normal read it passes as an original American middle-grade draft. The voice is snappy and the dialogue is genuinely kid-like, especially Theo's. The jokes land in English ("Normal trees have leaves," "Have you ever asked one?", "It was a big grocery store."). An editor who looks closely would notice a few faint tells:

- European nouns: "name card," "front" for façade.
- A few noun-phrase fragments in dialogue ("The meeting at the hospital.").
- A slight over-stacking of one-word fragments in description ("Moldy. Damp." / "Amber eyes. Calm. Alert.").
- Small repetitions that suggest the sentences were built one at a time.

None of these would make a kid reader stop.

### (2) Character voices

- **Nora (12): 4/5.** She's clearly the rational older sibling, and her inner life is shown physically ("Order helped. Order always helped.", "It had."). She loses a point because some of her lines sound adult ("Not properly insulated."), and she rarely gets a funny or sharp line of her own. She could use one moment of dry wit to match Theo.
- **Theo (10): 5/5.** Funny, anxious and sarcastic in a way that sounds like a real 10-year-old. His shift to sincerity with the dog ("He trusts me." / "Can you just not explain everything for once?") is the emotional high point of the chapter.

### (3) Is the ending strong?

**Yes, mostly (about 4/5).** The build-up up the stairs, the dust, the name SILBER on its own line, the hackles rising "like a wave", and Theo grabbing her sleeve are all very effective, and the pacing tightens nicely. Two weak points:

- "A sound Nora didn't hear with her ears" muddles the climax (see #33).
- The final line, "He was growling at the door.", restates what the reader already knows. It's a clean, quiet button. It would be even stronger if the last beat hinted that something answered: a creak, a draft under the door, or the doorknob. This is a suggestion only, and the current ending already works as a cliffhanger.
