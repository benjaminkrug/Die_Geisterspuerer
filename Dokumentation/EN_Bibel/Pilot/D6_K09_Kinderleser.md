# D6 – Kid Reader + Read-Aloud Test: EN_K09 ("The Diary")

Source: `Dokumentation/EN_Bibel/Pilot/EN_K09.md` (only file read).
References use the **line numbers** of EN_K09.md, written as "L37".
Reader persona: 10-year-old American kid who likes spooky stories. I have not read chapters 1–8.

---

## Part 1 – Kid Reader

### Overall gut reaction
"This one is really sad. I wasn't bored for most of it, because the diary keeps getting creepier and I wanted to know what happened to Lina. The ending made me want to go down into the basement. Wait, they call it a cellar? Whatever."

### Where I got bored / would skim

| Where | Why |
|---|---|
| **L37** (the summary of school, cotton candy, stars) | Big block with no dialog. It's short enough, but this is where my eyes start jumping ahead to find the scary part. |
| **L43** ("Summer. The pool. Ice cream...") | Fine because it's short. I'd skim it, but it doesn't hurt. |
| **L115–L119** (the guilt part) | "A hot stab… Guilt. Hot and sharp." then "a pressure in her chest, heavy and tight." That's the same feeling twice. I'd skip to the next diary entry. Also, Theo already made a joke so it was over, and then Nora feels bad afterward. That's backwards. |
| **L129–L143** ("Keep reading" / "I want to go home") | I liked "I want to go home", but the whole thing takes a while before she reads again. Right when it's getting scary we stop for a lot of crying. |
| **L169–L177** (silence, crying, Shadow lying there) | Three slow paragraphs in a row. The first one is good, then I want something to happen. |
| **Shadow beats in general** (L15, L35, L57, L77, L95, L133, L177, L203) | Shadow lifts his head or presses against Theo like eight times. By the middle I stopped noticing. The ones where he **growls** (L57) and the one at the very end (L203) are the good ones. |

The diary entries themselves (L47–L103) are **never** boring. That part is the best in the whole chapter.

### Words or phrases I wouldn't know / would trip over

|---|---|---|

| L15 | "**amber** eyes" | I sort of know it (it's a color?), but I'd have to guess. |
| L19 | "**ceiling lamp**" | We say "ceiling light". Lamp sounds like something on a table. |
| L29 | "**scolded**" | I know it, but it sounds like an old book. "yelled at me" or "got mad at me" is what kids say. |
| L35, L203 | "ears **pricked** forward" / "ears turned forward" | "Pricked" is a weird word. I'd guess what it means, but I'd stumble. |
| L37 | "**astronomer**", "**constellations**" | I know these from school, but some kids won't. Fine, because "count stars" is right there to explain it. |
| L61 | "scratch **in rhythm**" | I know "rhythm", but it's hard to spell and say. "In a pattern" or "like a beat" is clearer. |
| L71 | "**Choppier**" | Weird word here. I think of water or a haircut. |
| **L83** | "her parents blamed it on **the heat**" | **I got confused here.** I thought "the heat" meant hot weather. It was the cold room! It took me a second to remember "Mom turned up the heat" (L51). Also, L47 says it was **Dad** who blamed the **window**. |
| L83 | "Like you did with the water pipes" | I don't know this one (it's from an earlier chapter). That's OK, it made me curious. |

| L189 | "No **sarcasm**" | Most 10-year-olds know it, but not everyone. |
| L197 | "stomach **clenched**" | OK, but "her stomach twisted" or "knotted" is easier. |

Not a word, but it confused me:
- **L39** "At least she had a dog." Theo is petting **his own dog** while he says this. So... they have a dog too? I didn't get what he meant. (Maybe he meant "at least she wasn't totally alone"? Then he should say that.)
- **L167** "The day before she died." How does Nora know she died, and the exact day? Maybe it's from an earlier chapter. If not, it seems like the book is telling me, not Nora.
- **L179** "Over the little heart." What heart? Nobody said there was a heart on the cover. L9 says the name is on the first page, not the cover.
- **L147** "*October 30. Only two entries left*" – I thought Lina wrote "only two entries left" in her diary. That made no sense. (Nora is the one saying it, but it looks like diary writing.)

### Did anything make me sad?

**Yes. This chapter worked.** In order from "a little sad" to "really sad":

1. **L27** – "Twelve. Like her." Small, but it sticks. *(a little)*
2. **L89** – "I don't watch scary movies. I live in one." Great line. Cool and sad at the same time.
3. **L99** – Her mom calls her a baby and says "Stop it." **This made me mad and sad.** I've had grown-ups not believe me.
4. **L111–L113** – Nora says sorry to Theo. Nice, but Theo's joke made it OK quickly.
5. **L123** – "If something happens to me, somebody should know that I told the truth." **This is the saddest line in the chapter.** I'd feel it in my throat.
6. **L137–L141** – "I want to go home." / "This is home, Theo." **Really sad**, maybe sadder than Lina's part, because it's about Theo.
7. **L153** – The ink is smeared from tears. Good, because I can picture it.
8. **L159–L165** – The empty pages. "And the one after that. And the one after that." **Really good.** I'd get goosebumps.
9. **L181** – "We heard you, Lina." Nice ending to the sad part.

Sad score: **8/10.** Lina writing so that someone will know she told the truth, and then the empty pages: that part works best.

Slightly too much: Theo cries three times (L125 sobs, L171 tears dripping, L183 red eyes). By the third time it feels like a lot. A 10-year-old boy reader might roll his eyes at the second one.

### Accidentally funny or silly moments

- **L65** – "Her tongue stuck to the roof of her mouth." I'd picture it literally stuck there and giggle a little.
- **L125** – "Theo sobbed." Then the next paragraph says "Once. Briefly." That sounds like a TV announcer. It's a little dramatic.
- **L39** – "At least she had a dog," said while petting his own dog. A kid would point at the book and say "YOU have a dog!"
- **L101** – "She heard him hold his breath." How do you *hear* someone hold their breath? That's silence.
- **L105→L119** – Nora closes her eyes "just for a moment", then has a whole conversation with her eyes closed, and only opens them at L119. I pictured her talking with her eyes squeezed shut.
- **L117 → L119** – "Hot stab… Hot and sharp" and right after "Her hands were ice-cold." Hot, cold, hot. It's like the weather keeps changing.
- **L69** – "Because chairs move by themselves all the time." **This one is funny on purpose**, and it's good. The only real Theo joke in the scary middle part. I wanted one more.

### Scariest moment + score

**L103:** "Last night the door opened by itself. Slowly. I watched it. Nobody was standing there. But the cold came in like a wave. Luna screamed. Really screamed. Like a person."

**Score: 6/10.** A dog screaming like a person is creepy. I'd think about it at night a little, but I wouldn't have nightmares.

Runners-up:
- L93 "At night someone stands in my room. I can't see it. But I can feel it. Cold. Right next to my bed." – **5/10**, very creepy if you read it in bed.
- L203 "Under their feet, deep in the house, something cracked." – **4/10**. It would be scarier as "creaked" or "knocked", because "cracked" made me think the floor broke.

### Would I read chapter 10?

**YES, for sure.** "What's in the cellar, Nora?" plus a noise under their feet is a really good ending. Lina went down there and never came back, so now I *have* to know what's down there. I'd also want to know whether Luna went with her and what happened to Luna. Nobody says anything about Luna at the end, and I noticed that.

## Part 2 – Read-Aloud (parent reading to a child)

### Main problem: who is speaking (diary vs. kids)

When you read aloud, the listener can't see the italics. So the diary voice and Nora's voice sound the same unless the parent changes their voice.

| Line | Issue |
|---|---|
| **L147** | **Biggest problem.** `“*October 30. Only two entries left*,” Nora said quietly.` "Only two entries left" is Nora's remark, but it's styled in italics inside the diary-date quote. Read aloud, it sounds like Lina wrote it. Then **L149** starts a new quote with no speaker tag. Fix: `“October 30,” Nora read. Only two entries left. She could feel it…` and put the remark in the narration. |
| L51, L61, L67, L75, L89, L99, L103 | Diary entries without a tag. Usually OK, because a narration line comes first. But **L75** comes right after Theo's joke (L69) and a narration line, so a listener might think Theo is still talking. Add "Nora read" to one or two of these. |
| L93, L123, L155–L157 | Good. They have "Nora read" plus a description of her voice, so they're easy to follow. |
| L79–L83 | Theo speaks twice with one short Nora line in between. Fine, but L83 starts a new paragraph without "Theo said" until after the first sentence. Clear enough. |
| L107–L113, L185–L195 | "Theo?" / "Yeah." / "Nora." / "Yeah." Short back-and-forth with no tags. On the page it works. Read aloud, by "She went down to the cellar" (L191) the listener has to count who's who. One tag would help ("…,” Theo said."). |
| L29 | "she read on" in the middle of the quote: easy. |

**Tip for the parent (and maybe the layout):** Diary entries spoken in a different voice will carry the chapter. A small visual cue, such as an indented diary block or a handwriting font, would help kids reading alone.

### Hard-to-read sentences / tongue-twisters

| Line | Sentence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| L7 | "Nora's pulse pounded in her temples." | p-p-p alliteration. It's fine, but you have to watch it. |
| L37 | "She had drawn the constellations, tiny dots in purple ink, joined by wobbly lines." | Three parts with two commas. You run out of breath. |
| L37 | "An entry about a trip to the roof with a flashlight, because Lina wanted to count stars." | Not a full sentence, and long. It's hard to give it the right tone. |
| L67 | "I've never sleepwalked." | "Sleepwalked" feels awkward to say. "I don't sleepwalk" or "I've never walked in my sleep" is easier. |
| L61 | "But mice don't scratch in rhythm." | "scratch in rhythm" has a lot of consonants together. Hard to say quickly. |
| L117 | "A hot stab went through her chest. Guilt. Hot and sharp." | "Hot" twice in a row sounds like a mistake when spoken. |
| L133 | "Shadow pressed his muzzle harder against Theo's arm. Theo's fingers dug into his fur." | "his fur": whose? Clear on the page, but it trips you up aloud. |
| L159–L163 | "Nora turned the page. / Empty. / The next page was empty. White. Untouched." | "Empty… empty" sounds repeated by accident. Cut "The next page was empty," and go straight to "White. Untouched. / And the one after that…" |
| L167 | "On November 2, 1974." | The parent has to decide between "November second" and "November two". Small, but it trips some readers. Same for dates in L25 and L47. |

### Rhythm stumbles

- **L105–L119: the order of the beats.** Eyes close → apology → Theo's joke → "Almost a laugh" → guilt stab → eyes open → pressure in chest. Read aloud, the guilt comes **after** the joke has released the tension, so it lands flat. Suggestion: put the guilt (L117) **before** "I'm sorry" and cut L119's second chest feeling.
- **L3–L5:** "The cold hit Nora like a slap." is a strong opener. "The light bulb flickered, then went back to normal." deflates it right away. "went back to normal" sounds flat aloud.
- **L15:** "His amber eyes were open. Alert. But calm. As if he knew that something important was happening now." The last sentence is long after three short fragments. "now" at the end is unnecessary.
- **L153:** "In some places the purple ink was smeared, as if drops had fallen on the paper. Tears." This one works well aloud. Keep it.
- **L165:** "And the one after that. And the one after that." Excellent aloud. Keep it.
- **L203:** "something cracked." The final sound word matters a lot aloud. "Cracked" is short and flat. "creaked", "knocked", or "something scraped" gives the parent more to perform.

### Things that read really well aloud (keep)
L27 ("Twelve. Like her."), L69 (chairs joke), L89 ("I live in one."), L93 (the "imagination" triple), L103 (Luna screamed), L123, L157 ("If I understand it…" twice), L165, L181, L201–L207 (ending).
