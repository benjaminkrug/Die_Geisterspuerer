## (1) Would you guess this was translated?

**No, though an attentive editor would get suspicious in a few spots.**

Most of the chapter reads like native American middle-grade. The vocabulary is US throughout (Mom, cotton candy, flashlight, corner store, turned up the heat). The dates are in US format. Theo's sleepwalking line and "If you'd believed me, I probably would've been even more scared" are natural and funny.

A few things give it away to a trained ear:
- "the knocking we hear"
- "water pipes"
- "I cut you off" (meaning "dismissed")
- "a picture of Lina took shape"
- "She had to be strong now"
- the verbless fragments "His warm body. His calm breathing."
- "making such a fuss"

A kid reader would not notice. An agent or editor might notice in two or three places.

## (2) Voice ratings (1–5)

- **Nora (12): 3/5.** In this chapter she is mostly a reading voice plus body reactions. Her apology (¶52) is the best moment. It is muddied by "cut you off", and her own personality (rational, older sister) is missing in the last line. Too many "voice cracked/whispered" beats make her passive.
- **Theo (10): 4/5.** He has the sarcastic edge in the sleepwalking line and the reversal "I would've been even more scared". "What's in the basement, Nora?" is a great button. He loses a point because he cries three times, which dulls the impact.
- **Lina's diary (12, 1974): 4/5.** It is honest, short and believable. The arc from cheerful to scared works well ("I'm scared. I'm really scared."). It is a bit too neat and grammatical, though ("I've never walked in my sleep", "If I understand it"), and it lacks a kid's messy emphasis (CAPS, !!).

## (3) Is the ending strong?

**Emotionally, yes. As a cliffhanger, almost.** The run from "She went down to the basement." / "And she didn't come back up." / "What's in the basement, Nora?" through to the creak under their feet is excellent. It is the scariest and best-paced part of the chapter.

Two things weaken it:
1. "Something creaked" is the weakest possible sound. Old buildings creak all the time. A sound that ties back to the chapter would land harder, for example the rhythmic scratching or knocking from the diary.
2. Nora's last line, "That is what we've got to find out," is a stock line that releases the tension the creak just built.

Either end on the sound, or give Nora something that turns the scare into a decision kids will remember.
