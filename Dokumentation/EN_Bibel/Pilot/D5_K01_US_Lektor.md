# D5 – US Middle-Grade Editor Pass: Chapter 1 "New City, Old House"

**Reviewer role:** Acquiring/line editor, US children's imprint (MG 9–12, spooky-funny: *Small Spaces*, *Goosebumps*, *Lockwood & Co.*)
**Source read:** `EN_K01.md` only. No original, no notes.
**Paragraph numbering:** every block of text after the chapter heading counts as one paragraph, including one-line dialog and the single-word line "SILBER". P1 = "The moving truck sat crooked…", P117 = "He was growling at the door."

**Overall impression:** This is a strong opening. The sibling banter works, the pacing is tight, and the dog's entrance is a good hook. Most of my marks are small. Together, though, they leave a faint "this was written in another language first" texture. The most common sign is the narration calling the mom "their mother" every time.

## Summary Questions

### (1) Would I guess this was translated?
**Yes, but only on a careful read.** A casual 10-year-old reader probably wouldn't notice, because the dialog is lively and the pacing is good. An editor notices several patterns quickly:
- The narration calls her "their mother" over and over instead of "Mom".
- There are literal-sounding interjections ("So?", "That's silly").
- Some compound nouns are carried over word for word ("kids' rooms", "food box", "nameplate").
- There are false friends ("everything was strange" meaning *unfamiliar*).
- Inverted place-first word order shows up twice ("On the stone step… sat a dog", "Next to the frame hung a nameplate").
- The "not in her ears, but in her stomach" construction doesn't land in English.

None of these is serious alone. Together, they make the text read like a good translation rather than an original.

### (2) Voice ratings
- **Nora (12): 4/5.** She's rational, organizing, and protective ("Order helped. Order always helped."), and that's great characterization. She loses a point because a few lines sound like a parent or teacher, not a 12-year-old ("That's silly," "Stop whining," "Not properly insulated"). Some of that is intentional older-sister bossiness, but she needs one or two more natural kid lines.
- **Theo (10): 5/5.** He's the best thing in the chapter. "It's July, Nora. Normal trees have leaves." "Hand-shaped old plaster. Sure." "Have you ever asked one?" "It was a big supermarket." That's exactly the *Goosebumps*/*Small Spaces* sidekick register: funny, scared, and a little sweet with the dog.

### (3) Is the final paragraph a strong chapter ending?
**Good, but not as strong as it could be.** The build-up is excellent: SILBER, hackles rising "like a wave", ears forward, the growl felt in the stomach. But "He was growling at the door." only *restates* what we already know from P114 ("Then the dog growled"). The strongest beat is actually "But in her stomach." Two options:
- (a) End on the stomach line and cut the restatement.
- (b) Make the last line *new* information that raises the stakes, e.g. something from behind the door answers: a creak, a shadow under the gap, the doorknob moving. For a series opener, (b) is what a US MG reader expects. The final line should make them turn the page, not confirm what they already know.
